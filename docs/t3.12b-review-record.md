# T3.12b — REVIEW RECORD: THE THREE SIGNALS DO NOT SHARE A CAUSE

Spec committed at `ffe28b2` **before any measurement**; commit order is the evidence (§7.13).
Diagnosis only — nothing fixed, nothing re-pinned, no band moved.

## THE HEADLINE: R3 FIRED, AND IT KILLED THE HYPOTHESIS

The spec pre-committed R3 as the reading that could falsify the shared-cause story, precisely so
the convenient answer could not survive by default. **It falsified it.**

```
starvationDeaths per seed, 20 seeds, 650 turns:  min 0   max 0   mean 0.0
seeds with ZERO starvation deaths:               20 of 20
crashCount across all seeds:                     0
```

The proposed pathway was *misallocated migration → a settlement holds more people than its food
supports → starvation deaths → lower world population*. **There are no starvation deaths.**
Migration is a `Ledger.Transfer` between settlements — people conserved, law 1 — so with the
transmission mechanism measuring exactly zero, **migration cannot have moved world population by
any amount, in any seed.** S3 is not a migration effect, whatever else correlates.

This is B-2 again wearing yet another costume: ~1,240 years of grain reserve means nothing
starves, so the one channel by which migration could cost population is held shut.

## R1 — VOLUME, decomposed as pre-committed (§7.15)

`migrationGrossPerDecade` is **already normalised by person-years** (`AutoplayMetrics.cs:139`,
`moved / personYears * 10`), so these are not mechanical scale artifacts:

| correlation | value |
| --- | --- |
| migration × final population | **+0.737** |
| migration × arable km² | **+0.815** |
| migration × density | +0.386 |

**Reading: the POSITIVE branch, which the spec named as "benign / scale".** More populous,
more-arable worlds migrate *proportionally* more — the opposite sign from the misallocation
hypothesis, which required more migration to cost population.

**VOLATILITY, reported separately because "migration signal" is composite:**

```
sign flips in the migrationGross series (650 turns): min 380  max 417  mean 395.7
corr(volatility, final population) = -0.190
corr(volatility, density)          = -0.124
```

Migration reverses direction on roughly **60 % of all turns, in every seed**. That is the
ping-pong already filed in `queue.md` — confirmed universal — but it is **flat across seeds**, so
it cannot be what makes seeds differ.

**Reading: oscillation is uncorrelated with density** — the lowest- and highest-density seeds sit

**AND A LIMITATION I WILL NOT PAPER OVER.** The director observed Thiathiariath moving

different order log from his session, so **this measurement does NOT reproduce his observation
and therefore does not explain it.** What it establishes is that low-amplitude oscillation is
universal; the high-amplitude event he saw remains unexplained and needs his session's own log to

## S1 REFRAMED — seed 9's breach is a CORRIDOR-SPECIFICATION question, not a migration defect

Seed 9 is **the smallest world in the sweep**: population 81,160, the minimum of 20 (next
smallest 98,853; largest 132,280).

| seed | population | migration | density |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **9** | **81,160 (min)** | **0.000980 (breaches floor)** | 1.1742 |
| 15 | 98,853 | 0.001567 | 1.1817 |
| 2 | 132,280 (max) | 0.001595 | 1.6018 |

Given corr(migration, population) = +0.737 on a population-normalised metric, **the smallest
world having the lowest migration intensity is the expected reading, not an anomaly.** The
corridor floor is absolute and takes no account of world size. So the finding is not "migration
misbehaved in seed 9" — it is that **the migration corridor's floor may be mis-specified for
small worlds**, and one seed in twenty falling 2 % under it is what that looks like. Not
dispositioned here; handed to whoever homes the migration-weight packet.

## VERDICT

**The three signals do NOT share a cause**, and the pre-committed falsifier is what settled it:

- **S3** is not migration — the transmission mechanism measures zero across all 20 seeds.
- **S2** is real and universal but flat across seeds, so it cannot explain S3.
- **S1** is consistent with seed 9 simply being the smallest world; it indicts the corridor's
  floor, not the migration weights.

**What DOES explain S3 is therefore still open.** World population is set by births and deaths
integrated over the era table, so an across-seed population spread of 1.63× must originate in
founding conditions compounding through the demographic integration — **ADR-017's endowment
jitter at 0.69 is the obvious candidate and it is NOT bisected.** Named, not asserted.

**The migration-weight packet (T3.4c ruling 2) is NOT exonerated** — its own finding stands
(design point missed 2.3×–8.1×, metric unstable in N and seed, still unhomed). What this packet
establishes is narrower and worth stating exactly: **it is not the common cause of these three
signals**, and homing it will not, by itself, resolve the density corridor.

The shared-cause story was the director's, and this packet was commissioned to test it. **It was

**Why this is a §7.10 instance and not merely a wrong guess:** the story was measurement-shaped

that channel measures exactly zero. One line of law-1 reasoning, written into the spec before
