## THE MECHANISM AS BUILT

Two sinks in `ConsumptionSystem.BoundStore`, applied **after** eating (people eat before the store
spoils or overflows; the reverse order would starve a settlement for an evaluation-order reason):

1. **SPOILAGE** — `ReasonIds.Spoilage (14)`, `Ledger.Flow` sink on the grain stock.
   **Integrated as a survival exponential `1 − exp(−rate·dt)`, not `rate·dt`** — at the canonical
   dt of 10 years the linear form would destroy 80 % of the store in one turn and 100 % at
   dt = 12.5. **The exponential form is dt-invariant.**
2. **GRANARY CAPACITY** — `ReasonIds.GranaryOverflow (15)`, sink on the excess above
   `1.5 × annual grain demand`. Denominated in years of the settlement's OWN demand, so it scales
   with population and adds no per-world constant.

**Two reasons, not one, deliberately:** "why is the store not growing?" has two different answers,
and merging a decay process with a capacity constraint would destroy the audit trail. Conservation
is exact — stocks stay `long`, both losses go through the Ledger.

**Stated, not hidden:** neither loss banks a sub-unit remainder, so a store whose annual spoilage is
under half a unit does not spoil. A remainder field would change the serialized row shape, which is
outside this packet's fence.

## 1. B1 — STARVATION OCCURS ON EVERY CANONICAL SEED, AND IT IS EPISODIC

**Not one seed of twenty.** At the shipped 1.5×, **all five canonical seeds starve**: 22, 109, 326,
263, 70 deaths, on **1–5 turns out of 650**. Episodic, not chronic — which is the shape a famine
should have. Population still grows on every seed.

**But the magnitude is very small and I will not call it "plausible" without saying so plainly:**
**1.3e-06 to 2.5e-05 deaths per decade per capita** is of order **0.0001 % to 0.003 % of population
per decade**. **A world that starves 70 people out of 126,875 across 6,500 years has cleared
"nothing starves" and has not yet reached anything a chronicler would record as famine.**
**The blocker is cleared in kind, not in degree**, and that distinction is the director's to rule
on rather than mine to resolve by moving a constant.

## 2. THE RESERVE-DEPTH DELIVERABLE (B-2a's requirement, per the manifest's §7.12 finding)

**Measured mean reserve at the shipped 1.5×: 1.275–1.320 years of demand** — inside B-2a's
comparison band of *"one to a few years"*, and **the manifest's pre-committed prediction of
"~1.5 years, at or near capacity" is CORROBORATED with one refinement: stores settle at ≈88 % of
capacity, not at it.** The 12 % gap is consumption drawing the store down between harvests, which
is the store doing its job.

**The relation is near-linear in the constant:** 0.880 / 1.320 / 1.760 against 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 —
a constant ratio of **0.88** at every setting. **The ceiling is what sets the depth**, as derived.

## 3. THE SENSITIVITY — **THE WORLD IS BALANCED ON THE CONSTANT, NOT ROBUST TO IT**

**This is the packet's most important measurement and it is a finding, not a result.**

Reserve depth moves **linearly** with the constant. **Deaths move by two orders of magnitude:**

**A 33 % change in one derived constant moves mortality by 25×, and at 2.0× seed 7 stops starving
entirely.** Between 1.0 and 2.0 the world changes character: from a world with recurring lethal
shortfalls to one where two of five seeds have essentially none.

**Per the director's stated criterion — "if behaviour changes character between 1.0 and 2.0, the
world is balanced on a constant — that is a finding" — IT DOES, AND IT IS.**

**What this does NOT license:** moving 1.5 in either direction. It was derived from a stated
reference class before measurement and the measurement corroborated its reserve-depth prediction.
**The finding is that scarcity's SEVERITY is not yet robustly determined by the mechanism** —
seed 3 starves at every setting while seed 7 starves at none by 2.0×, so what varies is not
uniform across worlds either.
