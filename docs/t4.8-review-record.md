bookkeeping outside the Ledger — the property Option B was taken for"*, and R-1's binding

part of the packet, not a follow-up."*

R-1 chose **Option B — A NOTABLE IS A PERSON**, "extracted from the bucket via `Ledger.Transfer`; a

So unlike T4.5, **a schema change is expected here, not avoided** — m4-spec §143 lists T4.8's cost

**PURCHASE IS OUT.** T4.1's escalation found R-1 holds for three of four lifecycle events:
*"born, dies and defects are clean Ledger flows; IS BOUGHT is not — the person moves, but the

## 2. IMPLEMENTATION — ONE ROW, THREE OPERATIONS, ONE AUDIT FIX

**`NotableRow`** (`Sim.Core/State/WorldState.cs`) — `Id`, `Settlement`, `Allegiance`, `CohortIdx`,
and `Count`, a **conserved `Population` stock**: 1 while this notable lives here, 0 once the slot is
vacated. A plain struct with FIELDS, not a positional record, because the Ledger mutates conserved
stocks by `ref` — the same shape `BucketRow`, `GoodStockRow` and `HousingRow` use.

`CohortIdx` is the age band the person was extracted FROM, kept so a notable stays a person in the
demographic sense rather than an age-less token. **Deliberately absent:** competence, traits,
experience and every battle field — those parameterize the resolver, which is M6.

**`NotableId`** (`Ids.cs`) — stable identity so the person can be followed while the ROW holding
them changes. An id, never a name: ADR-001 keeps display names out of sim rows.

**`NotableLifecycle`** (`Sim.Core/State/NotableLifecycle.cs`) — three pure functions:

| operation | mechanism | conservation |
|---|---|---|
| **Born** | `Ledger.Transfer` moves exactly 1 person bucket → notable | world total unchanged |
| **Dies** | `Ledger.Flow` SINK under `ReasonIds.Deaths` | exactly 1 person leaves the world |
| **Defects** | `Ledger.Transfer` moves the person to a new row under the new allegiance, same `NotableId` | world total unchanged |

`Dies` reuses `ReasonIds.Deaths` rather than inventing a notable-specific reason: it is the same
event — a person died — and splitting one audit question ("where did the people go?") across two
answers buys nothing. Vacated rows are **kept at Count 0, never deleted**, exactly as an emptied
bucket is, because deleting would shift every later index.

**THE LAW-1 AUDIT FIX, which is packet content and not incidental.** `ConservationAuditor` summed
`Population` from **buckets only**. With a second carrier that made every promotion look like a
population loss — three tests failed on it before the fix. The auditor now spans both carriers, so
`Σ stocks + Σ sunk − Σ sourced = 0` holds across a notable's entire life. This is precisely the
"law-1 audit as packet CONTENT" R-1 required.

## 3. NO SPAWNER — AND WHY THAT IS THE CORRECT SCOPE

Nothing in M4 decides WHEN a notable emerges; D-010 puts that trigger in the aggrieved-bucket
mechanism, and inventing one here would be a spawner nobody ratified. So the lifecycle operations
are **static functions over (Ledger, tables)**, not a pipeline system: they register no slot,
run on no turn, and therefore **change no existing world's behaviour**. What M4 owes — the
conservation surface, from day one — ships complete and is exercised by the test harness directly.

Consequence, stated plainly: `Notables` is EMPTY in every live world today. The structural readiness
is proven (§4, §5), not asserted.

## 5. TESTS — 12 targeted, all passing

`Born` extracts exactly one person with the world total unchanged; carries the settlement and cohort
of the bucket it left; **refuses an empty bucket and leaves no half-made row behind** (law 1, never
from nothing). `Dies` sinks the person under `Deaths` and vacates the slot without deleting the
identity; **dying twice takes nobody the second time**. `Defects` moves the same person, keeps the
identity, changes allegiance and settlement, and leaves **exactly one living row for the identity** —
a notable in two places at once is the duplication R-1 exists to prevent; defecting from a vacated
slot moves nobody. The whole life — born → defects → dies — is asserted conservation-exact **at
every step** via `ConservationAuditor`. A promotion sequence that exhausts its bucket proves the
third attempt is refused rather than minting anybody. A scope-fence test asserts the lifecycle file
carries no battle vocabulary in CODE (comments are stripped first — the file's own prose names
`BattleSetup` to say it is absent, and a fence that fired on its own documentation would be
measuring the wrong thing; the banned list is proven non-vacuous against a sample battle line).
