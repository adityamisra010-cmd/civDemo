## 1. WHAT THE TREE DOES TODAY, read from code, not remembered

`Comfort` is need id 6 in `Sim.Data/content/needs.json`: `bound: true`, `weight: 0.3`,
`varietyWeight: 0.15`, and — decisively — **no `source` field**, so it defaults to `"basket"`.
Its basket entries are pottery and cloth, per person-year:

| class | good | perPersonYear |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (peasants) | pottery | 0.02 |
| 1 (peasants) | cloth | 0.03 |
| 2 (artisans) | pottery | 0.05 |
| 2 (artisans) | cloth | 0.08 |

So today Comfort is a **flow**: pots and cloth are *eaten* every year exactly as grain is, out of
`GoodStocks`, through `ConsumptionSystem`'s basket fill. Nothing is owned; nothing persists.

`Shelter` (need 2) is the tree's ONE existing stock-sourced need: `source: "housingStock"`. Its
plumbing is the shape T4.13 must follow structurally and depart from behaviourally:

- `NeedRecord.Source` is a **closed vocabulary in DATA** — `NeedsConfig.cs:180` refuses anything
  that is not `"basket"` or `"housingStock"`, so a typo cannot silently pick a source.
- `NeedsConfig.cs:198-208` is an **ambiguity guard**: a stock-sourced need must have NO basket
  entries, because two satisfaction sources for one need means the code silently picks one.
- `NeedsGrievanceSystem.cs:245` is the single read site (`if (need.FromHousingStock)`).
- Dwellings are a conserved capital stock (`ConservedQuantityIds.Dwellings = 4`), sourced by
  `HousingBuilt`, sunk by `HousingDecayed`, with the materials they consume sunk separately under
  `HousingMaterials` — three reasons because the audit question has three different answers.

## 2. DIMENSIONAL DECLARATION (S8 §4.1 requirement 2 / m4-spec §3.2 format)

| quantity | dimension | status |
|---|---|---|
| **household-goods stock** | units of household goods — a conserved `long`, a CAPITAL stock like `Dwellings`, never sitting in `GoodStocks` and never traded | **NEW** — needs a `ConservedQuantityId` and a serialized row |
| **household-goods standard** | units of household goods per PERSON — the per-head holding at which Comfort is fully met | **NEW; must be DERIVED from the existing basket, not chosen** (see §4) |
| **wear rate** | fraction of the goods IN USE per SIM-YEAR — law 3, integrated with `dtYears`, never per turn | **NEW** |
| **craft inflow** | units of household goods per sim-year, produced from pottery and cloth | **NEW**, but its inputs are the existing `pottery`/`cloth` good stocks |
| **Comfort satisfaction** | dimensionless in [0,1] — stock ÷ (population × standard), clamped, exactly as Shelter is capacity ÷ population | **EXISTING SHAPE**, new source |
| `perPersonYear` (pottery, cloth) | units of good per person per sim-year (`needs.json` baskets) | **EXISTING — cited, not asserted**; it is the derivation input for the standard, and it LEAVES the basket when Comfort stops being basket-sourced |
| population | conserved `long` persons (`BucketRow.Count`) | EXISTING |

**Types (law 7):** the stock is a conserved `long`; the standard, the wear rate and satisfaction
are `double`. **dt (law 3):** wear and craft inflow are per-sim-year rates integrated with
`ctx.DtYears`; neither is ever expressed per turn.

## 3. THE MECHANISM, and why it is NOT housing's shape

Housing's equilibrium is **maintenance-driven**: dwellings decay when their upkeep materials are
not paid, so the stock is bounded by the materials budget. Copying that would make Comfort a second
housing and would be the thing the packet forbids.

The packet's own words name a different driver: *"depleted by USE and replenished by crafting."*
That is a **use-driven** equilibrium:

```
inUse        = min(stock, population × standard)        // only what people actually use wears
wearPerYear  = inUse × wearRate                          // NOT stock × wearRate
stock' = stock − wearPerYear·dt + crafted·dt
```

The load-bearing asymmetry is `inUse`, not `stock`, in the wear term. Its consequences are the
acceptance property:

- **A surplus does not evaporate.** Goods beyond what the population can use sit idle and do not
  wear, so the mechanism does not silently punish abundance the way a `stock × rate` decay would.
- **But a surplus buys nothing either.** Satisfaction is `stock ÷ (population × standard)` clamped
  at 1, so holdings above the standard are dead weight — which is what stops the stock being a
  score that ratchets upward forever.
- **The equilibrium tracks population, not time.** Setting `stock' = stock` gives
  `crafted = population × standard × wearRate`: a settlement must keep crafting in proportion to
  the people it has, forever. **A settlement that stops crafting loses Comfort on a timescale set
  by `1/wearRate`, and a settlement that grows loses it by dilution without losing a single pot.**
  That second sentence is the one P4 is asking for — the stock cannot saturate at 1.0 forever
  because population is in the denominator and growth alone breaks the ratio.

**No free-floating modifier (law 2).** Every term above is a stock, a population, or a rate inside
a resolution equation. There is no "+20% comfort" anywhere, and no permanent buff.

## 4. THE STANDARD IS DERIVED, NOT CHOSEN

`needs.json` already states what a person consumes per year for Comfort. When Comfort stops being
basket-sourced those numbers must not simply be deleted — they are the only ratified statement of
how much household goods a person needs, and they become the derivation input:

> A person's annual Comfort consumption today is `perPersonYear(pottery) + perPersonYear(cloth)`.
> Under a stock model that same annual figure is what WEARS OUT per person per year, i.e.
> `standard × wearRate = Σ perPersonYear`. One equation, two unknowns — so exactly ONE of the two
> is free, and it is the wear rate (a service life), with the standard following from it.

This keeps the packet honest about what it is inventing: **one** new number, a service life for
household goods, expressed as a rate per sim-year. Everything else is arithmetic off ratified data.
The class-dependence is preserved for free, because artisans already consume ~2.6× what peasants
do and that ratio carries straight into their standard.

## 5. BLAST RADIUS (what this packet must touch, enumerated before it is written)

- `Sim.Data/content/needs.json` — Comfort gains `source`, and its basket entries move out. **Both
  edits at once, or the ambiguity guard at `NeedsConfig.cs:198` refuses the file** — which is the
  guard working, not a defect.
- `Sim.Core/Systems/NeedsConfig.cs` — the closed source vocabulary gains a third member and its
  ambiguity guard must cover it.
- `Sim.Core/Systems/NeedsGrievance/NeedsGrievanceSystem.cs:245` — a second stock-sourced branch.
- `Sim.Core/State/Ids.cs` — a `ConservedQuantityId` for household goods; Ledger reasons for the
  craft source and the wear sink. Following `HousingBuilt`/`HousingDecayed`/`HousingMaterials`,
  the materials sink is its OWN reason: "where did the clay go?" and "why is Comfort falling?" are
  different audit questions.
- `Sim.Core/State/WorldState.cs` + `Sim.Core/Kernel/CanonicalSchema.cs` — one new row type, schema
  **v21 → v22**, and the three edits `CanonicalSchema`'s own contract names (write, read,
  ExpectedLength). Ships a POPULATED-table test with exact `ExpectedLength`, bit-exact round-trip
  and hash equality — empty-table coverage proves nothing (T1.1/T1.3 precedent).
- `Sim.Core/Kernel/ConservationAuditor.cs` — the new stock joins the per-turn audit.
- A system (or a Housing-adjacent one) owning craft + wear, registered in `SystemCatalog` and
  `pipeline.json`.
- **Goldens: all three behavioural pins plus `ci.yml`'s `FOUNDED_GOLDEN` will move**, because
  Comfort feeds grievance and grievance feeds nothing today but is serialized. Re-derived by
  measurement with OLD → NEW → CAUSE, never carried.

## 6. WHAT THIS PACKET WILL NOT DO

No trade in household goods (they are a capital stock, not a good — same call housing made).
No new class behaviour, no merchants (T4.11), no Comfort-driven migration term. T4.2's store
bounding is a DEPENDENCY, not scope: this packet consumes it and does not re-open it.
