## 1. THE REFERENCE CLASSES — FIXED HERE, BEFORE THE WORLD IS RUN

### 1a. SPOILAGE — fraction of stored grain lost per sim-year

**Class:** *threshed cereal grain in pre-modern FIXED storage — mud-brick or pit granary, temperate
to semi-arid, no chemical protection, no controlled atmosphere.* Not modern silo storage (which is
a different technology and belongs to B-2a's ENRICHMENT layer), and not field or transit loss
(which is a different stage).

**The class's range:** traditional fixed storage loses on the order of **5–10 % per year** to the
combined action of moulds, germination, insects and rodents; poorly maintained or damp storage runs
higher, well-maintained dry storage lower. **The physical carrier is decay — grain rots.**

**DERIVED VALUE: `grainSpoilagePerYear = 0.08`** — the midpoint of the class band, taken as a plain
midpoint rather than tuned. Integrated dt-correctly (`× dtYears`), per law 3.

**What this alone implies, computed here so it cannot be back-fitted:** a store fed by a constant
annual surplus `s` reaches equilibrium when loss equals inflow, `0.08 · S = s`, i.e.
**S = 12.5 years of surplus**. Spoilage alone therefore bounds the store but does NOT by itself
produce a one-to-a-few-year reserve. **That is the reason the second layer exists**, and it is
stated before measuring rather than discovered after.

### 1b. GRANARY CAPACITY — a per-settlement ceiling

**Class:** *household and communal grain storage in an agrarian settlement.* Historically an
agrarian household carried **on the order of one to a few years** of its own consumption — enough
to bridge a failed harvest and a sowing, not enough to bridge a decade.

**DERIVED RELATION, not a free constant:** capacity is expressed in **YEARS OF THE SETTLEMENT'S OWN
GRAIN DEMAND**, so it scales with population and needs no per-world tuning:

```
capacityUnits = granaryYearsOfDemand × (annual grain demand of the settlement)
granaryYearsOfDemand = 1.5
```

**1.5 is the midpoint of "one to a few", taken as the class's centre.** The physical carrier is a
structure of finite size, and it grows with the settlement because a settlement with more households
has more granaries.

**PREDICTED RESERVE DEPTH, stated before measurement:** the capacity ceiling (1.5 yr) binds far
below the spoilage equilibrium (12.5 yr of surplus), so **stores should sit AT or NEAR the
capacity**, i.e. **~1.5 years of demand — inside B-2a's stated comparison band of "one to a few
years".** If the measurement lands far from that, **it is reported as a finding, not adjusted to
reach it** (B-2a, verbatim).

### 1c. CONSERVATION — law 1, exactly

Spoiled grain **leaves through `Ledger.Transfer` to a named sink with a stated reason**. Conserved
stocks stay `long`; the spoiled fraction is computed in `double` and converted with the project's
existing whole-units discipline, remainder carried. **Exact equality in tests, no epsilon.**

### B1 — THE PACKET'S OWN READING: DOES ANYTHING STARVE?

**Stated in advance, with the horizon:** at a 1.5-year buffer, a settlement's store no longer
absorbs a bad weather year, so **starvation should appear in SOME seeds within a 650-turn run**,
concentrated in low-fertility or farming-suppressed settlements. **Expected magnitude: a nonzero
starvation rate that is a small fraction of population per decade — not a world die-off.**

- **starvation occurs at a plausible rate** → the blocker is cleared; **report the rate**;
- **nothing starves** → the bound is too generous **or the wrong quantity**. Report WHICH, with the
  measurement. **Do not tighten the constant to force a death.**
- **mass die-off** → **STOP and report.** Something is wrong with the mechanism, not the constant.
