The packet asked: what should `AttractivenessLandWeight` (currently `0.078125 = 5/64`) MEAN, now that

## 1. THE PRESCRIBED METHOD'S DENOMINATOR NO LONGER EXISTS

`docs/t3.4c-remeasurement.md:162` heads the prescription **"What a sound design point would fix
instead — PROPOSED, NOT APPLIED"**. Two things follow from reading it exactly:

- It **rules out** the method that produced the current value: *"A dispersion balance cannot be the
  design point, because dispersion is not a model constant"* (`:164`). That prohibition is standing.
- It **prescribes** (`:165-170`): *"name a reference fed state (a stated food-per-capita and
  arable-per-capita, from a reference class rather than from a measurement of the sim) and set
  `landWeight / foodWeight` so the two terms are equal there."*

**The prescription is a RATIO, and T4.10 deleted its denominator.** `foodWeight` no longer exists,
so "set `landWeight / foodWeight` so the two terms are equal" has no second term to equalise against.
The method is not merely unapplied here — as written it is **inapplicable** to the post-T4.10 model.
It also names no reference class; the class is left open.

This is not a reason to improvise a substitute. `docs/t4.1f-review-record.md:62-71` records what
happened the last time a landWeight was derived without a ratified basis: computed 0.088286, shipped
0.0859375, then **REVERTED** by director ruling — *"The derivation is sound but it is a TUNE change
carried by a packet whose purpose it does not serve, and a +10 % weight landing on main unexplained
is a constant nobody can attribute later."* It had also been fitted from a sim measurement, which is
precisely what T3.4c forbids.

## 3. MEASURED — THE WEIGHT IS FALSIFIABLE, SO STOP CONDITIONS 3 AND 4 DO **NOT** FIRE

Stated plainly because the packet asked for it specifically, and because the answer is the opposite
of T4.10's food finding. Canonical, 650 turns, scaling `AttractivenessLandWeight` alone:

| seed | ×0.25 | ×1 | ×4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.00014170 | 0.00019344 | 0.00023326 |
| 2 | 0.00018971 | 0.00023008 | (not run — see below) |

Monotone and material. **`LandWeight` is falsifiable and does not cancel from every observable.**
Cap-binding is a minority regime, which is why: of 132 ordered pairs, ~66 carry a positive gap at
all, and of those the cap binds on 2–30 (3–45 % of live pairs, varying by seed and turn). The
majority of live pairs are sub-cap, and in the sub-cap regime flow is linear in the weight. This
also agrees with the historical instrumentation (`docs/t3.4b-review-record.md:186`: 63 % of pairs at
the shipped rate; `docs/t4.1g-review-record.md:11-14`: 36.4 % at ×1).

## 4. THE FINDING — `LandWeight` IS REDUNDANT WITH `BaseRatePerYear`

This is the reason a unique value cannot be derived, and it is a consequence of T4.10 rather than a
pre-existing defect.

**Algebraically.** Write `K = Σ_buckets BaseRate × profile × count × dt × damping × viability`. Then
`gapDesire = K × gap`, `gap ∝ lw`, and `cap = f × m*` is **independent of `lw`** (T4.10 established
that `lw` cancels exactly from `m*`). Delivered gap-driven flow is
`perCount × damping × viability × gapScale × gap` with `gapScale = min(1, cap/gapDesire)`:

- **Sub-cap** (`gapScale = 1`): flow `∝ perCount × gap ∝ BaseRate × lw`. Scaling `lw` by `f` and
  scaling `BaseRate` by `f` produce **algebraically identical** flow — the first multiplies `gap`,
  the second multiplies `perCount`, and only the product appears.
- **Cap-bound**: `gapScale × gap = (cap/gapDesire) × gap = cap/K`, in which **both** constants cancel
  — the same structural cancellation `corridors.json` already records for `BaseRatePerYear`
  (*"the rate cancels out entirely"*) and `docs/t3.4b-migration-evidence.md:93-99` explains.
- **Overdraw-bound**: `scale = prevCount/desiredTotal` with `desiredTotal ∝ BaseRate × lw` — both
  cancel again.

So in **no** regime does `lw` carry information that `BaseRatePerYear` does not. Only the **product**
is identifiable.

**Measured, as a check.** Scaling `lw` by `f` against scaling `BaseRate` by the same `f`:

| seed | f | LandWeight arm | BaseRate arm | divergence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.25 | 0.00014170 | 0.00013481 | 5 % |
| 1 | 1 | 0.00019344 | 0.00019344 | 0 (identical — the control) |
| 1 | 4 | 0.00023326 | 0.00026366 | 13 % |
| 2 | 0.25 | 0.00018971 | 0.00016460 | 15 % |

The `f = 1` arms are bit-identical, which is the sanity control. The other arms agree to within
5–15 % rather than exactly. **What is and is not claimed:** the algebra above shows exact
redundancy *in the gap channel*, and famine flight — the one channel `BaseRate` scales that `lw` does
not — is inert here, since T4.10 measured `DeficitRatio` identically 0 across the canonical world.
The residual 5–15 % is therefore attributable to integer flooring (`ConservedMath.WholeUnits`), the
per-bucket migration remainder, and 650 turns of feedback through population into arable-per-capita.
**I did not isolate that residual with a dedicated experiment, so it is reported as unattributed
rather than explained.** It does not restore a second degree of freedom: no reading of a 5–15 %
discretisation divergence identifies a *level* for `lw`.

**Consequence:** any `(lw, BaseRatePerYear)` pair with the same product is observationally
near-equivalent. There is no unique `lw` to derive, and pinning one would require an external anchor.

## 5. THE ONLY AVAILABLE ANCHOR IS THE CORRIDOR, AND USING IT WOULD BE FITTING

With the ratio method's denominator gone (§1) and the coefficient redundant (§4), the sole remaining
way to pin `lw` is to choose the value that puts `migrationGrossPerDecade` inside `[0.001, 0.01]`.
That is **STOP condition 2** verbatim ("the derivation requires fitting to the old migration
corridor") and **STOP condition 8**, and it is the exact move `docs/queue.md:718` forbids: *"Do not
re-tune the floor to silence it — that would be fitting the instrument to the artifact."*

It is also worth recording that the corridor is a poor anchor on its own terms, for two reasons that
have nothing to do with this packet's preferences:

- **Scope mismatch.** The band's derivation (`corridors.json`, `canonical.migrationGrossPerDecade`)
  defines what it measures as *"LONG-DISTANCE PERMANENT INTER-SETTLEMENT RELOCATION between sites
  spaced >=480 km apart"*, and reasons its bounds from a frontier-agrarian reference class (LBK
  Neolithic, Bantu expansion, the Russian/Siberian and North American frontiers). **T4.1b moved
  `minSpacingKm` from 480 to 95.2.** The shipped world's settlements are ~5× closer than the
  phenomenon the band describes.
- **Possibly the wrong observable.** `docs/t4.1g-review-record.md:43-49` already records that
  *"gap-driven migration DOES respond to the rate lever; gross migration does not… something
  uncapped and rate-insensitive dominates gross flow"* — the corridor measures gross migration.

**Note, against the obvious inference:** the band itself is NOT invalidated by T4.10's model change.
It was derived from a reference class about the society, not from the attractiveness formula's shape,
so re-shaping `R` does not touch it. Its problem is the spacing scope above, which predates T4.10.

## 6. PHASE 5 — WHERE THE DEGREE OF FREEDOM SHOULD GO (recommendation, not applied)

The model carries one parameter too many in the gap channel. Two coherent resolutions, neither taken:

- **(i) Fix `lw` by convention and let `BaseRatePerYear` carry the rate.** Set `LandWeight = 1` so
  `R` is literally fertility-weighted arable km² and `instant` is literally arable km² per capita —
  a quantity with units and meaning, which a reference class can actually speak about. Then derive
  `BaseRatePerYear` against the migration reference class, which is what its name already claims it
  is: a per-year propensity. This puts the free parameter on the constant that has a physical
  interpretation and removes the one that does not. **Recommended.**
- **(ii) The converse** — keep `lw`, drop `BaseRatePerYear`. Rejected: `BaseRatePerYear` is the
  parameter with a stateable reference class (per-year relocation propensity); `lw` is a bare
  reconciler, and `docs/spine-s8-governance-freeze.md:97` only ever licensed such weights as
  *"dimensionless reconciler between X and Y"* — a justification that lapses when there is no Y.

Either is a TUNE change that moves behaviour and goldens, and (i) requires a fresh reference-class
derivation of `BaseRatePerYear`. That is a directed decision, not something to fold into a packet
whose ruling was "derive `landWeight`" — which is the T4.1f lesson exactly.

# DIRECTOR RULING — SEMANTIC NORMALIZATION, AND WHAT MEASUREMENT SAYS ABOUT IT

**RULING: `LandWeight = 1.0`,** so that `R_i` is fertility-weighted arable land and `instant_i` is
fertility-weighted arable land **per capita** — a quantity with a unit and a meaning, rather than a
bare scale coefficient — with `BaseRatePerYear` left as the parameter responsible for migration
response rate instead of the two compensating for one another. **The ruling states explicitly that
this is a normalization/design decision, NOT a claim that 1.0 was empirically discovered, and asks
that the distinction be documented. It is documented here, and it matters more than it first looks:
the ruling is about SEMANTICS, and the measurements below show the naive implementation of it would
have been a BEHAVIOUR change.**

**STOPPED AGAIN, at Phase 1 and again at Phase 4 — two independent triggers, both named in the
packet. No production file, config, test, corridor or golden was changed; tree verified
byte-identical to `abbb6fa`.**

## PHASE 1 — THE NEUTRALITY PRECONDITION FAILS

The packet's Phase 1 requires: *"Verify that setting LandWeight = 1 changes only the scale
representation of the attractiveness signal and does not introduce a new behavioral degree of freedom
by itself. If this is not mathematically true in the actual code, STOP and report."*

**It is not true, and here is exactly why.** Verified against the shipped code:

| quantity | line | dependence on `lw` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `resources[s] = lw × arableKm2` | `MigrationSystem.cs:188` | linear |
| `instant[s] = resources[s] / max(pop,1)` | `:189` | linear |
| `gap = max(0, smoothed[dst] − smoothed[src])` | `:265` | **linear** |
| `gapDesire += BaseRate × profile × count × dt × damping × viability × gap` | `:274` | **linear (via gap)** |
| `denom = resources[src] + resources[dst]` | `:280` | linear |
| `equalizing = (R_dst·P_src − R_src·P_dst)/denom` | `:281-283` | **CANCELS** |
| `cap = GapClosingFraction × equalizing` | `:284` | **CANCELS** |
| `gapScale = gapDesire > cap ? cap/gapDesire : 1` | `:285` | **asymmetric** |

`lw` scales the **desire** but not the **cap**. Raising it 0.078125 → 1.0 multiplies the gap channel
by **12.8×** against a fixed ceiling, which is a behaviour change, not a re-labelling.

**Measured, canonical, 650 turns:**

| arm | `lw` | `BaseRate` | seed 1 migration | seed 2 migration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CONTROL (today) | 0.078125 | 0.03 | 0.00019344 | 0.00023008 |
| **`lw`=1 ALONE** (the literal ruling) | 1.0 | 0.03 | **0.00032391 (+67 %)** | **0.00046388 (+102 %)** |
| **`lw`=1, product preserved** | 1.0 | 0.00234375 | 0.00018064 (−6.6 %) | 0.00019406 (−15.7 %) |

So `lw = 1` **alone is a +67 % to +102 % behavioural change**, not a normalization. (It is not the
full 12.8× because the fixed cap absorbs most of the amplification — more pairs simply become
cap-bound, exactly as the algebra predicts.)

**A true normalization exists and is arithmetically forced, not invented.** Because only the product
`BaseRate × lw` is identifiable (§4 above), the unique behaviour-preserving companion to `lw = 1` is
`BaseRatePerYear = 0.03 × 0.078125 = 0.00234375` — exact in binary. That arm measures −6.6 % / −15.7 %
rather than exactly 0; the residual is the same integer-flooring and 650-turn-feedback class already
reported in §4, **and it was not isolated by a dedicated experiment, so it is reported as
unattributed rather than explained.**

**This is the distinction the ruling asked to have documented, sharpened by measurement:** `lw = 1`
is a semantic choice, and it is only a *pure* semantic choice when the product is carried across. On
its own it silently doubles migration.

## PHASE 4 — OUTCOME **B**: IDENTIFIABLE, BUT WITH NO DERIVATION AT ALL

Searched exhaustively. **`baseRatePerYear = 0.03` has no derivation anywhere in the repository:**

- `Sim.Data/content/sim.json:111` carries **no `_doc` string** — conspicuous, since the same migration
  block documents `_docDamping` and gives `_docLandWeight` a full derivation record.
- `Sim.Core/Systems/SimConfig.cs:421` has **no per-field comment**, unlike its neighbours. The only
  enclosing statement is `SimConfig.cs:394`: *"Migration tuning (T2.5, all TUNE, per-sim-year)"* —
  classified as tuning, with no basis given.
- No reference class was ever stated for it, in pointed contrast to the corridor band, whose class
  (LBK Neolithic, Bantu expansion, the Russian/Siberian and North American frontiers) is written out
  in full in `corridors.json`.
- `corridors.json` denies its name is even operationally accurate: *"It is NOT a migration
  propensity… the rate cancels out entirely"* whenever the cap binds.
- `docs/spine-s8-governance-freeze.md` contains **no mention of migration or of this constant at
  all**, so it holds neither a provenance requirement nor an exemption — the S8 audit never covered
  it. It is also not exemption-eligible on the `attractivenessFoodWeight` precedent: that pass-state
  licensed a *"dimensionless reconciler between X and Y"*, which is not what a per-year rate claims
  to be.

Per the packet's own decision tree this is **outcome B — "identifiable but has no defensible existing
derivation. STOP and return a narrowly scoped director decision."** Phase 4 also forbids inventing a
value, and none was invented.

**Bearing on the normalization: there is no provenance to protect.** Moving the scale from `lw` to
`BaseRatePerYear` cannot damage a derivation that does not exist. But neither does it *create* one —
after normalization the model still carries exactly one underived scale constant. What changes is
that it then sits on the parameter that is *supposed* to carry rate and can be derived against a
reference class, instead of on a coefficient that cannot.
