**FINDINGS**

**F1 — (b) mismatch, escalated (§10 G1).** `HarvestWeatherSystem` holds the log-deviation's
STATIONARY variance at `σ²` for every dt (`HarvestWeatherSystem.cs:42-45`) and applies
`exp(x − σ²/2)` to the WHOLE turn's output. The state variance is dt-correct; the OUTPUT multiplier
is not: a turn's food is the turn-MEAN of the yearly multipliers, whose variance falls with `dt/τ`.
For an OU process with `σ = 0.2936`, `τ = 3`: `Var(mean over T) = σ² · 2(τ/T)[1 − (τ/T)(1 −
e^{−T/τ})]` → factor 0.427 at `T = 10` (`σ_decade = 0.192`), 0.948 at `T = 0.5`. At dt = 10 the
shipped decade multiplier is 1.5× too variable in σ: the 5th-percentile decade reads 0.59 where the
yearly σ/τ imply 0.72, and the era table alters the decade-scale climate. Consequence: at Libur's
`ρ ≈ 1.3` the recorded t117 draw (0.4427, a 0.4th-percentile decade as shipped) produced `d = 0.30`;
the cascade's M3 arm at the corrected σ produced 0.079 (`cascade-report.md:134`). The redesign
separates famine from variability by CAUSE and by the disaster band's derivation; F1 decides how
lethal an ordinary SEVERE decade remains (§3.2, §9 R1) and whether ordinary decades reach SEVERE at
all (corrected: 5th-percentile `d ≈ 0.02` at ρ = 1.3, i.e. STRESS only). NOT corrected here.

**F2 — (c) chosen, never derived** (RECORDED, queued, not corrected — the S8 common case):
`0.12`, `1.5`, `1.3`, `3.0`, `0.5`, `0.08`, `8.0`, `0.03`, `CohortProfile`, `0.25`.

**F3 — (a) physically impossible in one unit system, INFORMATION, not blocking:** `0.12/yr` at
`d = 1`. The linear-in-`d` hazard is what CR-012 §2b ratified; the redesign changes the ARGUMENT
(`d_eff`), not the form or the constant.

**F4 — the dt-vs-buffer artefact makes "famine-class" a dt-dependent property (escalated, §10 G8).**
`ConsumptionSystem` settles the whole turn's balance at once: obtained = `min(required, opening
store + the turn's production)`, then the closing store is capped at `G` years (`:195-230`,
T4.2). At dt = 10 a settlement with surplus ratio `ρ` therefore has an EFFECTIVE buffer of `B_eff =
G + dt·(ρ − 1)` years of demand against a shock inside the turn — 4.5 y at Libur's `ρ = 1.3`,
12.5 y at the world's grain mean `ρ ≈ 2.1` (`scratchpad/answer-resources-full.txt`: world
harvest 24.35 M vs eaten 11.63 M over 161 turns; `libur-resources.txt` row 110: 7458 harvested vs
5538 eaten, closing 830 = 1.5 y) — while the physical granary is 1.5 y. At dt = 0.5 the same
settlement's surplus overflows every half-year (spoilage + overflow are 52 % of the world harvest)
and only the granary plus the surplus DURING the event count. A production failure of `s·D`
production-years therefore produces a deficit at dt = 10 iff `s·D > B_eff/ρ = (dt(ρ−1) + G)/ρ`
(1.5 at ρ = 1; 3.46 at 1.3; 4.33 at 1.5; 5.95 at 2.1) and at dt = 0.5 iff `1 − ρ(1 − s) > G/D`.
This is the t4.2 record's open "dt-versus-buffer question" (`t4.2-review-record.md:279-289,
382-387`), inherited by every deficit reader; this spec neither fixes it (CR-012 §5 keeps
Consumption frozen; the mandate preserves the stock model) nor hides it: §3.3 derives the
disaster band against `B_eff` at the coarsest dt at a DECLARED `ρ_ship`, §6.2 pins the dt-10 vs
dt-0.5 classification of one physical event as a DIFFERENCE, and G8 asks the director to
acknowledge the artefact or authorise its M5 fix.

**F5 — Σ over food goods is not feedable nutrition (corrected in §3.6a).** Substitution is INTO
the staple only (`ConsumptionSystem.cs:150-176`): a livestock surplus cannot cover a grain
shortfall. Libur t110 (`cascade-report.md:48`, `libur-resources.txt:110`): food produced 12 733
(all goods) vs required 6291 reads as a 2.02× limit, but grain is 7458 and the non-staple

### 3.1 FoodState — the derived four-state classification

`Sim.Core/State/FoodState.cs` (new; static, pure, non-serialized; sibling of `SettlementHappiness`).

```
enum FoodStateKind { Normal = 0, Stress = 1, Severe = 2, Famine = 3 }
enum FamineReason  { None = 0, Disaster = 1, Abandonment = 2, Both = 3 }

static FoodStateKind Of(IReadOnlyWorldState w, SettlementId s, SimConfig cfg, out FamineReason reason)
    d        = w.ConsumptionDeficits[s].DeficitRatio            (absent row → 0)
    struck   = w.Disasters[s].AppliedMultiplier < 1.0            (absent row → false)      // §3.3
    abandon  = IsAbandoned(w, s)
    if (d > 0 && (struck || abandon))  → Famine, reason = {Disaster|Abandonment|Both}
    if (d > SevereThreshold(cfg))      → Severe        // adaptation exhausted: starvation begins
    if (d > 0)                         → Stress        // adaptation absorbs the cut
    else                               → Normal

static double SevereThreshold(SimConfig cfg) = cfg.FoodState.AdaptationAbsorbableShortfall     // = a (0.20)

static bool IsAbandoned(IReadOnlyWorldState w, SettlementId s)
    row = w.SectorAllocations[s]  (absent → Sectors.Default(s), exactly as ProductionSystem.cs:148-153)
    return Sectors.Raw(row, Farming) == 0.0 && Sectors.Raw(row, Herding) == 0.0
```

**One boundary carries the kernel semantics (challenger #4).** `θ_sev := a`: SEVERE begins exactly
where the dead-zone ends, so the state label predicts the kernel's response — STRESS ⇒ starvation
hazard identically 0, SEVERE ⇒ starvation on the unabsorbed remainder, FAMINE ⇒ starvation on the
whole deficit. Revision 1 had `θ_sev = 1/FamineFertilitySuppressionSlope = 1/3` with `a = 0.20`,
which left `d ∈ (0.2, 1/3]` labelled STRESS while starving at up to 14 % per decade — the label
did not predict the response. The kernel's 1/3 birth full-stop (`DemographicsSystem.cs:136`) is
REAL and is documented as a boundary INSIDE SEVERE ("births stop at d = 1/3", or at the nominal
`d` where `d_eff = 1/3` if G3 rules suppression onto `d_eff`) — a kernel fact, not a state. The
structurally aligned alternative `a := 1/3` (dead zone = the band in which births still occur,
Minnesota's 50 % cut survived 24 weeks without deaths) is recorded as a TUNE value the director
may set; it is not the default because the director's own frame puts survivable cuts at 15–25 %.

**Abandonment predicate — exactly what PREV exposes.** `Farming == 0 ∧ Herding == 0` on the raw
row IN FORCE for the step. It is authoritative because it is the same row and the same fallback
that make farm labour and the herding pool zero (`ProductionSystem.cs:155-158`, `:175-179`):
"deliberate abandonment" and "food labour is zero" are one fact. Raw weights (not `Share`) so the
all-zero row — reachable only from a hand-written log, `SectorOrderFactory.cs:98-109` refuses it —
classifies as abandoned (nothing is farmed; `Share` would divide 0/0). Legacy `LaborAllocation`
pct = 0 (`PathBuildSystem.cs:80-88`) writes Farming 0 / Herding 0 / Construction 1.0 and IS
abandonment: `CollapseStabilityTests`, the FirstReign fixture and the skipped `FamineAtOneOfTwelve`
rig are therefore FAMINE rigs without re-rigging. Hand-built worlds with no row read
`Sectors.Default` (0.55/0.15) and are never abandoned. The single-sector cases (`Farming = 0 ∧
Herding > 0`, `Farming > 0 ∧ Herding = 0`) are NOT abandonment by the director's rule; what the
food model then does to a pure pastoralist is §10 G6.

**Timing (one-turn lag, no state).** Order at turn `t` → row in state `t+1` (PathBuild is last,
`SystemCatalog.cs:308-309`) → zero harvest at step `t+1→t+2` → `d(t+2) > 0` once the store is short
→ `FoodState.Of(state t+2)` = FAMINE at step `t+2→t+3`. Restoring farming at turn `u` → row
`u+1` restored → FoodState at `u+1→u+2` reads a non-abandoned row with `d(u+1) > 0` → SEVERE/STRESS
(adapted) for that one turn → first restored harvest at `u+1→u+2` → NORMAL. The disaster branch
reads the multiplier that was APPLIED to the harvest that produced `d` (§3.3), so a disaster
struck at `t` classifies FAMINE at `t+2→t+3`, the same turn its deficit first appears. The two
causes carry different lag semantics ON PURPOSE and it is written down: a policy is read as IN
FORCE (forward-looking — if it stands, the next harvest is zero too); an event is read as APPLIED
(backward-looking — it caused this deficit). Both keep `d > 0` necessary.

**Why this satisfies CR-003 §3 "decided by the food balance" while adding the cause qualifier.**
FAMINE never fires without a shortfall: a disaster a rich granary absorbs (`ρ = 2.1` at dt = 10
absorbs every event in the band — §3.3 table) is NOT a famine, and an abandonment while the store
still feeds everyone is NOT a famine (the "small vs large stockpile" pin, §6.1). The cause
qualifier changes the response FORM (no adaptation), never the magnitude, which stays the food
balance's own `d`. A famine-class event that strikes in a decade the weather also shortened and
tips a balance that would otherwise have held IS a famine by disaster — the event is in a band
defined to be capable of exhausting a typical granary alone (§3.3), so the attribution is to a
necessary famine-class cause, not to the weather. The amendment (N1) is needed for the "no …
threshold or event" wording, not for the substance.

### 3.2 Effective deficit — adaptation (mortality only)

```
static double EffectiveDeficit(double d, FoodStateKind state, SimConfig cfg)
    if (state == Famine) return d;                                   // no adaptation possible
    a = cfg.FoodState.AdaptationAbsorbableShortfall                  // 0.20
    return d <= a ? 0.0 : (d − a) / (1.0 − a);                       // dead-zone, exactly 1 at d = 1
```

**Form chosen: DEAD-ZONE**, over the two candidates and one hybrid, for these reasons:

1. It is the director's frame read literally: "sustained ration cuts of ~15–25 % are survivable"
   — survivable = no excess mortality. The dead-zone gives exactly that below `a`; a smooth form
   cannot (at physiological `a` the blended `d²/(d + a(1−d))` leaves `d_eff(0.2) = 0.11`, i.e.
   13 % adult starvation per decade at a survivable cut — §8.7).
2. `d_eff(1) = 1`: with nothing to eat nothing is adapted away. The pure saturating `d²/(d+a)`
   fails this (`1/(1+a)` at total loss — R3's finding).
3. Null arm `a = 0` is EXACT: `(d − 0)/1 = d` bit-for-bit, so today's linear response is one
   config value away and every "adaptation removed" mutant is a data change, not a code change.
4. One parameter, one frame, Glass-Box explainable: "below a 20 % sustained shortfall people
   tighten belts; above it the unabsorbed remainder starves them".

**`a = 0.20` CHOSEN**, plausibility frame: Minnesota Starvation Experiment (a ~50 % calorie cut for
24 weeks in healthy men, no deaths — an upper bound), WWII civilian rationing at 15–25 % cuts (no
famine mortality), Dutch Hunger Winter and post-war German rations at 40–70 % cuts (excess
mortality) — the director's own band, midpoint taken. Lives in a new `foodState` `sim.json`
section (used by three systems: Demographics, Migration via `FoodState`, and the chronicle), so
not `DemographicsConfig`; `RequireRate`-validated in `[0, 1)`. `a = 1/3` is the recorded
alternative (§3.1).

**Readers (challenger #6).** `d_eff` feeds ONE channel: starvation mortality (§3.6b). It does NOT
feed the flight hazard or the B1 readout — those read the NOMINAL `d` (§3.4): the physiological
frame justifies zero excess MORTALITY below `a`, not zero MIGRATION; a 15 % shortfall is exactly
the "ordinary migration pressure" the mandate preserves (cascade §4.1: exact hazard on nominal `d`
gives 2.4–5.9 % aggregate outflow at `d ≤ 0.05` and 11–16 % at 0.10–0.15), and under G1(b)
ordinary deficits shrink to ≈ 0.02–0.08, where a dead-zone on flight would kill every
weather-stress migration. Everything "shortage remains meaningful" stays on NOMINAL `d`,
byte-identical: fertility suppression (`DemographicsSystem.cs:136` — G3 may move it, one
argument), the rebound release gate (`:181`), destination viability (`MigrationSystem.cs:230`),
`SettlementHappiness.Food` (`:118-129`; feeds `RevoltSystem.cs:77` and viability's shade),
`AppropriationSystem.cs:139-150` (raid at `d > 0`), `ClassMobilitySystem.cs:226-231` (demote-first at
`d > 0` — an economic response, kept; ruling line N8b), `NeedsGrievanceSystem` (fill ratio, not `d`).

**Worked values (a = 0.2):** `d` 0.10 → 0; 0.20 → 0; 0.25 → 0.0625; 0.30 → 0.125; 0.40 → 0.25;
0.50 → 0.375; 1.0 → 1.0. Adult starvation per decade `= 1 − e^{−0.12·d_eff·10}`: STRESS `d ≤ 0.20`
→ 0 (today 21 % at 0.20); SEVERE `d = 0.30` → 14 % (today 30 %); `d = 0.40` → 26 % (38 %); FAMINE
`d = 0.15` → 16 %; FAMINE `d = 0.35` → 34 %.

### 3.3 DisasterSystem — the explicit famine-class production shock

`Sim.Core/Systems/Disaster/DisasterSystem.cs` (new, stateless; `WellKnownId = 23` — 17 is on
unmerged `t4.13`, 22 is `GovernanceSystem` on unmerged `m5-full-build`; `Name = "disaster"`;
registered in `SystemCatalog.All()` and `pipeline.json` immediately after `"harvestweather"`).

**Row** (serialized, `CanonicalSchema` v24 → v25, appended after `Structures`, width 4+4+8+8+8+8 = 40):

```
record struct DisasterRow(
    SettlementId Settlement,
    int    Kind,               // 1 = crop failure; 0 reserved (none). One kind at first cut.
    double Severity,           // fraction of FOOD output lost per ACTIVE year, [s_min, s_max]
    double RemainingYears,     // years of the disaster still ahead AFTER this step's overlap; 0 when it has run its course
    double Multiplier,         // what ProductionSystem multiplies food rates by in the step that READS this row
    double AppliedMultiplier)  // what ProductionSystem multiplied by in the step that WROTE this row (= prev.Multiplier, or 1.0)
```

Rows exist only where `Multiplier < 1 ∨ AppliedMultiplier < 1 ∨ RemainingYears > 0`; the table is
rebuilt every step (`TradeFlows` precedent `WorldState.cs:451-458`); absent row ≡ (1.0, 1.0, 0).

**Step**, per settlement in table row order, ALL from prev:

```
p = 1 − exp(−λ · dt)                                          // λ = cfg.Disaster.HazardPerYear
for each settlement s (row order):
    uH = rng(s).NextDouble(); uS = rng(s).NextDouble()         // ALWAYS both, so λ = 0 leaves RngStreams bit-identical to λ > 0 with no strike
    prevRow = prev.Disasters[s] (absent → none)
    applied = prevRow.Multiplier (absent → 1.0)
    if (prevRow.RemainingYears > 0)                            // an active disaster continues; no new onset while active (renewal process, biases stated below)
        severity = prevRow.Severity; remaining = prevRow.RemainingYears
    else if (uH < p)                                           // onset
        severity = s_min + (s_max − s_min) · uS; remaining = D  // D = cfg.Disaster.DurationYears
    else
        { if (applied < 1.0) write (s, 0, 0, 0, 1.0, applied); continue; }   // carry the Applied fact one turn, nothing else
    overlap    = min(remaining, dt)
    multiplier = 1 − severity · overlap / dt                   // dt-exact: the turn loses severity × (years active in the turn)
    remaining -= overlap
    write (s, 1, severity, remaining, multiplier, applied)
```

**Production reads it exactly where it reads weather.** `ProductionSystem.Step` computes once per
settlement `foodMultiplier = HarvestWeatherFor(prev, s) × DisasterFor(prev, s)` (`DisasterFor` =
`prev.Disasters[s].Multiplier`, absent → 1.0); `Farm` `:239` becomes `ratePerYear *= foodMultiplier`;
`FromDeposits(foodSector: true)` `:300-306` receives it in weather's own position; extraction keeps
`1.0`. `x × 1.0 == x` bit-for-bit, so every world without a strike produces identically.

**Constants (all TUNE, new `disaster` section; validated: λ ≥ 0, 0 ≤ s_min ≤ s_max ≤ 1 — s_max > 1
would hand `Ledger.Flow` a negative source under `Throw` — D > 0):**

Reference class: pre-modern European regional famines — England 1300–1700 ≈ 5–6 famine-class events / 400 y (≈ 1/70 y); France by région 1500–1800 (Le Roy Ladurie) 1/50–1/100 y; a single settlement's hinterland sees fewer than a région. **The band is DERIVED against the effective buffer at the coarsest dt (challenger #2).** Revision

a bad-weather decade. The corrected derivation: a famine-class event must be able to exhaust the
full buffer of a settlement at the shipped surplus ratio ALONE at the coarsest era dt, i.e.

`ρ_ship` enters only as the size of the buffer the event must be able to beat.

**Onset process — accepted biases, stated (challenger #7).** "No new onset while active" plus
booking the whole `min(remaining, dt)` loss in the onset turn gives: at dt = 10 no refractory (a

truncated; at dt = 0.5 a 5-year refractory, effective rate `λ/(1 + λD) = 0.952 λ`, i.e. ≈ 4.8 % rate
bias. Both ≈ 5 %, opposite in mechanism, ACCEPTED at λ = 0.01 and recorded in ADR-024 and in the
dt-correctness paragraph; the exact renewal (onset time drawn uniformly within the turn, loss
spilled into the next turn, superposed onsets multiplying) is queued (§11). The loss GIVEN onset is
dt-exact (`D_DtExact_FiveYearFailure`).

**What the disaster IS and IS NOT.** It multiplies the same two FOOD paths weather multiplies
(farming + herding/fishing), leaves `26.0` a statement about land, does not touch stores (a
"granary loss" kind is queued), does not read population/stores/deficits (like weather, it cannot
decide famine — `FoodState` does, from the balance). Every row in the single kind is famine-class
by the derivation above, so `FoodState` needs no per-row physics test; if a second kind with
sub-class severities is ever added, `FoodState.struck` must read a per-row famine-class predicate

### 3.4 Migration source — stress → hazard → bounded outflow → shares

Replaces the flight term at `MigrationSystem.cs:400-402` and `:415-417`; the gap channel, viability,

- **Exact integration.** `1 − exp(−rate·dt)` is the survival-kernel form the demographics and

- **HOW MANY separated from WHERE.** `φ_b` depends on the source's deficit and on ω (the BEST

  leaves `φ_b` unchanged and halves each share — the destination-count invariance test (§6.3).
- **ω = max** rather than a Σ-normalisation because it keeps ADR-012's "die at home" bit-exact

  magnitude term of the best exit only; ADR-025 rewrites ADR-012's "only redistributes WHERE"

- **Nominal `d`, a continuum from ordinary pressure to crisis flight.** At `d = 0.05` a profile-1

### 3.5 Migration destination — viability, equalisation bounds, and the vacancy bound

Three destination-side facts, three semantics, applied in this order to every flow into `j`
(each factor ≤ 1; pair → basin-destination → basin-source → vacancy; the overdraw scaler last):

**(a) Viability — CAN `j` accept at all** (`:225-235`, UNCHANGED, nominal `d_j`): anyFood gate ×
`max(0, 1 − Repulsion·d_j)` × happiness shade. A food-less ruin accepts nobody; a starving
settlement repels. This multiplies every flow of both channels exactly as today.

**(b) Equalisation bounds on the GAP channel — how far toward equal land-per-capita a turn may
move.** T2.8(a)'s pair cap `f × m*_ij`, `m*_ij = (R_j P_i − R_i P_j)/(R_i + R_j)` (`:375-379`), is
the pooled-equalisation flow for the pair; the same derivation for a BASIN gives the aggregate:

```
destination j, basin B_j = { i : gap-capped desire i→j > 0 }   (|B_j| ≥ 2, else skip — bit-identity by branch)
    P_pool = P_j + Σ_{i∈B} P_i,  R_pool = R_j + Σ_{i∈B} R_i        // j pooled with its sources
    M*_j^in  = max(0, R_j × P_pool / R_pool − P_j)                 // inflow that brings j to the pooled per-capita level
                                                                   // ≡ (R_j P_B − R_B P_j)/(R_B + R_j) over B alone; equals m*_ij at |B| = 1 ALGEBRAICALLY, not in bits (hence the skip)
    inflow_j = Σ_{i∈B} gapScale_ij × gapDesire_ij
    destScale_j = inflow_j > f × M*_j^in ? f × M*_j^in / inflow_j : 1.0

source i, basin C_i = { j : gap-capped desire i→j > 0 }         (|C_i| ≥ 2, else skip)
    M*_i^out = max(0, P_i − R_i × P_pool / R_pool)   over C_i ∪ {i}   // the mirror
    srcScale_i = outflow_i > f × M*_i^out ? f × M*_i^out / outflow_i : 1.0

gap desire i→j executed = gapScale_ij × destScale_j × srcScale_i × gapDesire_ij
```

- `f = GapClosingFraction`; `R`, `P` are the instantaneous physics T2.8 uses — no new constant.
- **Fan-out is IN SCOPE**: with 11 destinations a source's pair caps sum to up to `(k+1)/2 × f ×
  m*` (cascade §5.3 B2, measured 6.1–6.4× at Libur t119) — the mirror of the fan-in overshoot,
  bounded today only by the overdraw scaler and "pinned empirically" only on the dev world.
- Revision 1's §8.8 rejected "the orchestrator's `M* = max(0, R_j P_B/R_B − P_j)`" as not reducing
  to the pair `m*`; that was a notation misreading — with `j` INSIDE the pool (as the cascade
  §5.3 defines `P_B`, `R_B`) it is the same formula as above. Withdrawn; one formula, one notation.
- In the fed canonical world the gap channel moves ≤ 1 % of a settlement per decade
  (`migrationGrossPerDecade` 0.001–0.01); these bounds bite on the multi-source refill after a
  collapse (Libur t119's 352-person gap refill, `cascade-report.md:140`), which is their purpose.

**(c) VACANCY bound on TOTAL inflow — HOW MANY `j` can absorb per turn (challenger #1, mandate
items 3 and 4).** Revision 1 refused a destination-side bound on flight; the mandate's pipeline
ends in "bounded destination inflow" and a 1000-person famine source next to two viable 10-person
settlements piled 430 refugees into each (43× their population) with no bound but the sources'
totals. The bound:

```
destination j (all from prev):
    V_j      = FoodHeadroom.Vacancy(prev, j, cfg)                  // §3.6a: max(0, N_lim,j − N_j) in adult-equivalents; +∞ when j carries no demand row
    cap_j    = (1 − exp(−k × dt)) × V_j                            // the same relaxation law that bounds births (§3.6b); k = ln2/10 per year
    in_j     = Σ_i Σ_b ( gapExecuted_ib→j + φ_b × count_b × w_ij ) × w_{c_b}     // this turn's total desired inflow, in adult-equivalents
    vacScale_j = in_j > cap_j ? cap_j / in_j : 1.0                 // one factor on EVERY flow into j, both channels; the refused share stays at its source
```

- **Semantic — one law for growth and immigration.** `N_lim,j` is the population `j`'s last-turn
  FEEDABLE food influx sustains (§3.6a); `V_j` is the vacancy in food terms; a settlement's
  population approaches its food-influx limit at rate `k` whether the arrivals are born or walk
  in. VACANCY ≠ ATTRACTIVENESS made literal: a 10-person settlement on tiny land (`ρ = 1.3`,
  `V = 3`) accepts ≈ 1 person per decade however attractive it is; a 10-person colony on open
  land is labour-bound (`ρ ≈ 8`, `V ≈ 70`) and accepts ≈ 35 per decade, then more as its labour
  grows — the "empty high-capacity destination" fills geometrically, never in one turn.
- **Not an arbitrary cap.** No new constant: `k` is the growth-cap relaxation (§3.6b), `V_j` a
  derived state quantity. `inflow/population` (the cascade's D5 diagnostic) is NOT the bound and
  is not asserted — a vacant fertile valley may legitimately take many times its residents.
- **Both channels, one factor.** The gap channel already carries (b); (c) is the outer bound on
  the total. In the fed world `cap_j ≈ 0.5 × 0.3 N = 15 % of N` per decade ≫ the gap channel's
  ≤ 1 %, so `vacScale_j = 1.0` (literal; the multiply is guarded `if (scale < 1.0)`) — bit-identical.
- **Refused refugees stay at their source** ("die at home", ADR-012) — no redistribution to
  other destinations in the same turn (water-filling rejected §8.20: iterative, and the shares
  already encode where people would go; next turn's shares see `j`'s changed state). They do not
  become `UnplacedDeparture` (B1's condition is unchanged; §10 G7).
- **Absent demand row ⇒ `V_j = +∞`** — the same absent-row rule as the growth cap: every hand
  rig's destination without a `ConsumptionDeficitRow` (all of `MigrationTests`,
  `MigrationStabilityTests`, `HappinessMigrationTests`, `CollapseStabilityTests`) is bit-identical,
  and a settlement's FIRST turn (founding turn 1; a colony's first turn) accepts unboundedly for
  that one turn (stated; §9 R12). A destination with `d_j > 0` has `N_lim,j < N_j` in general ⇒
  `V_j = 0` ⇒ it accepts nobody — consistent with viability's repulsion and it closes the
  ping-pong (`queue.md:65`) at the destination end.
- **Order of scales is pinned** (pair → destination basin → source basin → vacancy → overdraw)
  and each is a pure function of prev, so the whole plan is computable before the transfer loop
  (`MigrationSystem.Plan`, §3.11) and the transfer loop recomputes the identical products.

**Rejected forms of the destination bound (§8.17–8.19):** land vacancy `R_j − P_j` (never
positive in the canonical world, §2); the pooled `M*_j^in` physics applied to flight (a land-rich
famine source — `A_i > A_j` — would be REFUSED everywhere although `j` has food to spare; and the
`f` fraction is a discrete-feedback stability fraction with no meaning for refugees); a per-head
ratio bound (`inflow ≤ population`: a modifier, law 2, and wrong for vacant land).

### 3.6 Demographics — the food-influx limit, effective-deficit mortality, and the headroom growth cap

#### 3.6a `FoodHeadroom` — ONE definition of the food-influx limit (challenger #3)

`Sim.Core/State/FoodHeadroom.cs` (new; static, pure; used by Demographics, Migration and the
observer — shipped in T4.21-1 so T4.21-2 ∥ T4.21-3 share no hunk):

```
static double Limit(IReadOnlyWorldState prev, SettlementId s, double[] cohortWeights, BasketBook baskets)
    // returns N_lim in adult-equivalents; +∞ when the settlement carries no deficit row, D == 0, or no vitals row
    D       = prev.ConsumptionDeficits[s].DemandUnits                     (absent or 0 → +∞)
    dtPrev  = prev.SettlementVitals[s].DtYears                            (absent → +∞)
    S       = prev.GoodStocks[(s, staple)].LastProducedUnits              // staple = BasketBook's substitution target (grain)
    for each non-staple g ∈ baskets.FoodGoods (registry order):
        NS_g = prev.GoodStocks[(s, g)].LastProducedUnits                  (absent → 0)
        b_g  = prev.GoodStocks[(s, g)].LastConsumptionDemandUnits / D     // realised basket share of g, [0, 1)
    // X = feedable food at the LIMIT population, at the current basket mix — the fixed point of
    // Consumption's pass 1 / pass 2 evaluated at demand X:  X = S + Σ_g min(NS_g, b_g·X)
    surplus := all g;  repeat (≤ |FoodGoods| times, deterministic):
        X = (S + Σ_{g short} NS_g) / (1 − Σ_{g surplus} b_g)
        move every g ∈ surplus with NS_g < b_g·X to short (monotone: X only falls; order irrelevant to the result, table order used)
    until no move
    return X / dtPrev                                                     // ae-persons the influx feeds per year

static double Vacancy(prev, s, cohortWeights, baskets) = max(0, Limit(...) − N_nutr(prev, s))   // N_nutr = Σ cohortWeights[c]·count over prev buckets
```

- **Exact mirror of Consumption's one-directional substitution (F5).** At the limit population the
  staple covers its own share plus every non-staple shortfall; a non-staple can cover only its own
  share. When every non-staple is in surplus the closed form is `X = S/(1 − Σ b_g) = S/b_staple`
  (Libur t110: `7458/0.9 = 8287`, `ρ_eff = 1.32`); when every non-staple is short, `X = S + Σ NS_g`;
  mixed cases are the fixed point. The challenger's conservative form `S + Σ min(NS_g, b_g·D)`
  under-states the limit by `Σ b_g · (X − D)` ≈ 10 % of the headroom in the Default mix — the
  population would plateau 10 % of its headroom short of the limit — so the exact form is used;
  it is at most three fixed-point iterations over ≤ 3 goods, table-ordered, no doubles sorted.
- **The basket mix is the STANDING population's** (`b_g` from the realised demand of the turn that
  wrote prev): exact under a constant class mix, and the class mix is a slow variable. Stated as
  the approximation it is.
- **Absolute, not ratio.** `N_lim = X/dt_prev` is the number of adult-equivalents the influx feeds
  per year — right for a land-bound settlement (production does not scale with arrivals) and
  conservative-then-geometric for a labour-bound one (next turn's `X` grows with its labour).
  `dt_prev` is on prev in `SettlementVitalsRow.DtYears` (`WorldState.cs:542`), so an era-boundary
  turn reads the dt that produced `X`, not the current one. Equivalently `N_lim = N_nutr(t−1) ×
  X/D` since `D = N_nutr(t−1) × dt_prev` (food entries sum to 1.0 per class, `needs.json:67`).
- **Weather is inside `S`** (the realised multiplier). The clamp `V ≥ 0` makes a bad draw harmless
  to the cap (births then only replace deaths); a good draw over-states the limit for one turn and
  the relaxation `k` limits how far that is chased. Weather-normalising `S` needs the APPLIED
  weather multiplier, which prev does not hold (the row holds the NEXT draw) — a
  `HarvestWeatherRow.AppliedMultiplier` field is the clean fix and is queued for the v25 window
  decision (§11), not taken here.
- **Null / identity arm:** `+∞` whenever the inputs are absent — every hand-built exactness rig
  (`new ConsumptionDeficitRow(s, d)` defaults `DemandUnits` to 0) and every founding turn.

#### 3.6b Demographics kernel changes

**(i) Mortality.** `:139` becomes `starveRate[c] = StarvationRate(d, c, dEff)` with `dEff`
computed ONCE per settlement from PREV beside `deficit` (`:128-135`); `:181` (release gate
`deficit == 0.0` on nominal `d`) is byte-identical; `:136` (`suppression`) reads nominal `d` as
specified, with the argument a one-line switch pending G3. dt-invariance holds by construction:
`dEff` is a per-turn scalar, `e^{−s h}` composes.

**(ii) Headroom growth cap.** Once per settlement per turn:

```
N_lim    = FoodHeadroom.Limit(prev, s, cohortWeights, baskets)                          // +∞ ⇒ cap skipped
N_now    = Σ_rows cohortWeights[c] × pop_row  over the OWNED buckets at step start       // post-migration, post-colonization, post-revolt (pipeline order)
H_0      = max(0, N_lim − N_now)                                                          // adult-equivalents; refugees who arrived this turn have used headroom
```

`H_0` is clamped at 0: the cap is a GROWTH limiter; decline is the deficit channel's job
(unclamped, one ordinary 0.44 draw with a full granary would cut births 73 % — R2's finding;
clamped, the worst an ordinary draw can do is "births replace deaths this decade"). A stockpile is
not an influx: an abandoned settlement living on its granary (`S = 0`, `d = 0`) has `N_lim = 0`,
`H_0 = 0`, births ≤ deaths — the "large stockpile" pin (§6.4). No `d == 0` gate: at `d > 0`,
`N_lim < N_now` in general ⇒ `H_0 = 0` ⇒ births ≤ deaths on top of suppression — consistent with
"reduce growth", and it never causes decline.

Inside the micro-loop, per step `h`, order-consistent with births-before-sinks (`:162-209`):

```
H_rem := H_0 at turn start (local; never state)
per step h:
  PASS A, per group g (anchored on its cohort-0 row), NO state mutation:
      unsuppressed_g = Σ_j f_c pop_j W(λ_c h) h                       (as :167-175, verbatim)
      base_g   = unsuppressed_g × suppression                          (verbatim expression of :177)
      bank_g   = ReboundRecoverableFraction × (unsuppressed_g − base_g)   // from the UNCAPPED pair — headroom-withheld births are never banked
      release_g = (d == 0.0 ∧ unsuppressed_g > 0) ? (reservoir_g + bank_g) × min(1, r h) : 0   // same add, same multiply as :181-183
      cand_g   = base_g + release_g
  CAP (skipped entirely when H_0 = +∞):
      D_pre    = Σ_rows pop_i (1 − e^{−(m_c + s_c) h}) × w_c            // this step's deaths of the standing population, ae (sinks compose: 1−e^{−(m+s)h})
      A_step   = Σ_rows pop_i e^{−(m_c+s_c) h} × (h/5) × (w_{c+1} − w_c) over rows whose aging destRow exists (mirrors :211-218's guard)   // nutritional drift from aging
      allowed  = (1 − e^{−k h}) × H_rem
      bornMax  = (allowed + D_pre − A_step) / (W(λ_0 h) × e^{−λ_0 h} × w_0)     // newborn survivors after this step's cohort-0 sinks, heads; w_0 converts
      m        = Σ_g cand_g > 0 ? min(1.0, max(0.0, bornMax) / Σ_g cand_g) : 1.0
  PASS B, per group g, committing:
      reservoir_g += bank_g                                              (verbatim :179)
      rel = release_g;  if (m < 1.0) { cand_g *= m; rel *= m; }         // guarded: the fed path executes today's instruction sequence
      reservoir_g −= rel;  born_g = cand_g
      birthsExact, survivors = born_g × W(λ_0 h), infant deaths, pop[cohort0] += survivors   (verbatim :190-194)
  sinks and aging                                                        (verbatim :199-218)
  H_rem −= (N_nutr_after_step − N_nutr_before_step)                      (only when capped)   // realised nutritional growth, aging drift included
```

- **Order-consistent:** the deaths this step's births must "replace" are a closed form of the
  pre-birth state (`D_pre`), so no lag, no carried state, no estimate. Newborns face the same
  step's sinks (the pinned double exposure at `:191-194, :204-207`) and age `h/5` into cohort 1
  within the step; `W(λ_0 h) e^{−λ_0 h}` accounts for the sinks, and the aging is weight-neutral
  ONLY because `cohortWeights[0] == cohortWeights[1]` (0.6, `sim.json:25-27`) — the exactness of
  `bornMax` DEPENDS on that data fact (challenger #8); the packet asserts it in a config test and
  the replica carries the general term `(w_1 − w_0)` so a future weight change degrades to a
  bounded, not silent, error.
- **Exactly 1.0 when headroom is large:** `min(1.0, big)` returns the literal `1.0`; the `if (m <
  1.0)` guard makes the fed path instruction-identical; `born = unsuppressed × suppression` and the
  bank/release arithmetic keep today's association. Verified in P3 by the goldens with the cap
  wired and every canonical settlement's `H_0` large (the attribution ladder, §4).
- **When the cap binds with `bornMax ≤ 0`** (deaths already exceed the allowed growth), births
  are 0 but `N_nutr` may still rise by `A_step − D_pre > 0` in a young structure (children
  maturing 0.6 → 1.0 outpacing deaths). The cap cannot and should not stop aging; so the exact
  statement is `N_nutr(end) ≤ N_lim + Σ_steps max(0, A_step − D_pre)` while binding, and the §28
  pin asserts THAT bound plus `d == 0` every turn (the substance: never overshooting INTO
  DEFICIT), with an all-weights-1.0 twin where the drift is identically zero and `N_nutr ≤ N_lim`
  is exact (§6.4). In heads the population never overshoots the head-count limit by more than
  the same drift term, converted by `w̄`.
- **Composition:** when binding, `H_rem(end) = H_0 e^{−k dt}` exactly regardless of how dt is cut
  into steps; `A_step` is included so the identity is exact, not approximate. Across a TURN
  boundary the cap re-reads `N_lim` from prev like every other prev scalar (CR-001 (a)); for a
  land-bound settlement (`S` constant, `D ∝ N`) the re-read equals the within-turn remainder, so
  `one dt-10 turn = two dt-5 turns` holds within flooring on such a rig (§6.4) — the pin the
  packet ships.
- **Reservoir: strict, deferred-not-invented (R2 form).** `bank` is taken from the uncapped pair
  because headroom-withheld births are not deferred conceptions — there is no physiological debt
  to refund; banking them would invent births under a sustained ceiling and overshoot (§28).
  `release` is scaled by `m` and the unreleased part STAYS banked (it was banked from real famine
  suppression and may release when headroom opens — after deaths, or when production grows).
  Under R1 (unscaled release) a post-famine rebound could overshoot the food limit by up to the
  bank; refused for §28.
- **`k = ln 2 / 10 ≈ 0.0693 /yr` CHOSEN** (`demographics.headroomRelaxationPerYear`), frame: the
  director's "the gap halves per decade"; `(1 − e^{−k h}) = 3.41 %` of remaining headroom per
  half-year. It is ALSO the vacancy relaxation of §3.5c — one law, one constant. Null arm: none in
  config by design (a structural bound — law 2 forbids a switch); the identity arm is `N_lim =
  +∞` (every existing rig); the removal arm is the mutant (§6). With natural growth at
  +0.076 %/yr the cap's visible footprint in the fed canonical world is small; its role is the
  §28 GUARANTEE, not a shaping force.
- **Units:** the cap is denominated in adult-equivalent persons throughout (`H`, `D_pre`, `A_step`,
  `H_rem`); births are heads and enter through `w_0 = cohortWeights[0] = 0.6`.

**Test replica** (`Sim.Tests/TestUtil/DemographicsReplica.cs:29-95`) gains `dEff`, `suppressionArg`

## §8. REJECTED ALTERNATIVES

1. **Population floors / quantisation** — a modifier over the real mechanism (ADR-012 already
   refused them; law 2).
2. **Recent-famine penalty / hysteresis on a serialized stress state** — history in state that the
   mandate forbids unless a persistent regime is demonstrated; derived `FoodState` + forensic
   "recent stress" gives the readout without the state. The disaster's `RemainingYears` is a
   physical duration, not stress history.
3. **Weather clamp / truncation of extreme draws** — forbidden by the mandate; also wrong in kind:
   F1 shows the shipped decade variance is a dt artefact, not a tail problem. Recorded as G1 instead.
4. **Bidirectional substitution (livestock ↔ grain)** — not taken by this spec; it is one of the
   three options the director must choose between for the pastoralist (G6), because the STOP
   condition IS reached there — revision 1's "never fires" is withdrawn.
5. **Food-flow rewrite (per-year consumption inside the turn)** — the "dt-vs-buffer" artefact (F4)
   is real and inherited; a rewrite of Consumption is exactly what CR-012 §5 keeps frozen and the
   mandate preserves. Declared (F4), pinned as a difference, put to the director as an M5 question (G8).
6. **A single "stability multiplier"** on mortality or growth — a free-floating buff (law 2); the
   redesign's four bounds (dead-zone, exact hazard, headroom, vacancy) each carry a physical semantic.
7. **Smooth adaptation `d²/(d+a)` or `d²/(d + a(1−d))`** — the first gives `d_eff(1) < 1`; both leave
   a survivable 20 % cut with ~11–13 % adult decade mortality at physiological `a`, contradicting the
   director's frame read literally; the dead-zone's `a = 0` null arm is exact.
8. **(WITHDRAWN)** Revision 1 rejected "the orchestrator's basin `M* = max(0, R_j P_B/R_B − P_j)`"
   as not reducing to the pair `m*`; with `j` pooled into `P_B, R_B` (the cascade §5.3 convention)
   it is the same formula as §3.5b. One notation is used.
9. **Reusing φ verbatim in the B1 readout** — identically zero under B1's condition (R3); colonization dead.
10. **Writing migration bounds onto `MigrationFlowRow` / a new diagnostics table** — schema churn and
    a second definition; the pure `Plan` static is one implementation and needs no schema.
11. **Unclamped headroom (`H < 0` drives births down)** — re-creates the missing-births pattern from a
    single ordinary draw (R2); decline belongs to the deficit channel.
12. **Weather-normalised `S` for `N_lim`** — needs the APPLIED multiplier on prev, which is not
    there; a `HarvestWeatherRow.AppliedMultiplier` field is the clean fix and is queued for the v25
    window decision, not taken.
13. **Rebound release unscaled by `m` (R1)** — can overshoot `N_lim` by the bank; violates §28.
14. **Disaster as a derived function of the RNG stream (no table)** — the `Applied` fact and a
    multi-turn duration cannot be recovered from the stream without replaying it; and Production
    must read a row like it reads weather (isolation, law 6).
15. **(WITHDRAWN)** Revision 1 refused any destination-side bound on flight ("a second gate on a
    channel viability already gates"). The mandate's pipeline ends in "bounded destination inflow"
    and viability is a GATE, not a quantity bound; §3.5c supplies the bound with a capacity semantic.
16. **Serialised chronicle/famine flags** — the chronicle is an observer (`ChronicleCollector` never
    writes state) and stays one.
17. **Land vacancy `R_j − P_j` as the destination capacity** — `attractivenessLandWeight` is an
    attractiveness scale (0.078 persons/km²) 5–10× below the canonical density, so `R < P` for every
    canonical settlement and the bound would refuse ALL immigration in the shipped world (§2).
18. **The pooled `M*_j^in` physics applied to flight** — equalisation, not capacity: a land-rich
    famine source (`A_i > A_j`) would be refused everywhere though `j` has food to spare, and `f`
    is a discrete-feedback stability fraction with no meaning for refugees.
19. **A per-head inflow ratio (`inflow ≤ population`, the cascade's D5 diagnostic)** — a modifier

20. **Water-filling redistribution of refused refugees** to destinations with spare vacancy — an

    next turn's shares see the changed states; single-pass scaling keeps the plan a pure function.
21. **Flight and readout on `d_eff`** (revision 1) — zeroes ordinary migration pressure below `a`
    and, under G1(b), every weather-stress migration; the physiological frame bounds mortality,
    not movement (challenger #6).
22. **`θ_sev = 1/slope` with a separate `a`** (revision 1) — two boundaries, the state label did
    not predict the kernel's response (challenger #4).
23. **Headroom on Σ food goods** (revision 1) — counts livestock/fish that one-directional
    substitution cannot turn into nutrition; the §28 guarantee was false in the Default-mix world

    ≈ 10 % of the headroom — the exact fixed point is used.
24. **A 3-year / `s ≥ 0.5` disaster band sized against the physical granary** (revision 1) — fully
    absorbed at dt = 10 at the shipped surplus ratio (F4; challenger #2).
25. **Refused refugees become `UnplacedDeparture`** — changes B1's condition (a colonization

## §10. GOVERNANCE CONFLICTS REQUIRING THE DIRECTOR

Precisely these eight; everything else in §5 is the mandate applied.

**G1 — Harvest-weather turn-mean variance (law 3 vs ratified T3.4b/T3.4c).** `HarvestWeatherSystem.cs:42-45`
holds the log-deviation's stationary variance at `σ²` for every dt, and `ProductionSystem.cs:239`
applies `exp(x − σ²/2)` to the whole turn's output. The STATE is dt-correct; the OUTPUT multiplier
is not: it should carry the turn-mean's variance, factor `g(dt/τ) = 2(τ/dt)[1 − (τ/dt)(1 − e^{−dt/τ})]`
(0.427 at dt 10; 0.948 at dt 0.5), i.e. `multiplier = exp(√g·x − g·σ²/2)`. At dt = 10 the shipped
decade is 1.5× too variable in σ, so ordinary decades produce deficits (0.30 at Libur's 0.4th
percentile) larger than a famine-class disaster's dt-10 deficit at the same settlement (≤ 0.20),
and the era table alters the decade-scale climate — the very hazard the comment names. Options:
(a) LEAVE (the mandate's "do not clamp" read strictly; the redesign then separates famine from
variability by cause and by the band's yearly-tail position only, and SEVERE decades from weather
stay as lethal as R1 says); (b) APPLY `g` (one factor, `σ`, `τ` and the AR(1) untouched; mean stays
exactly 1; goldens move); (c) apply `g` and re-derive `σ` — refused (σ is yearly and correct).
**Recommendation: (b), as its OWN packet T4.21-W with its own golden attribution step** (challenger
#10), so T4.21-1's layout-only control survives byte-for-byte. The redesign's mechanisms do not
change under either ruling.

**G2 — Aim of `DemographyRetuneTests.Famine_MortalitySpike_BirthDeficit_PostFamineRebound` (`:185-241`).**
A T2.7-ratified acceptance requiring a ≥ 1.35× per-capita mortality spike from a ~0.2 deficit on
an all-farming, weather-free, no-disaster, Default-sector rig. Under the mandate that rig is
STRESS (`d ≤ a`) and must NOT spike; under the dead-zone it does not. It cannot be re-pinned; its
aim is the question (ADR-015 §7.2). **Recommendation:** re-anchor phase 1 as "STRESS does NOT
spike" and add an abandonment TWIN (a 0 %-farm order) asserting the spike; phases 2 (birth
deficit) and 3 (rebound) stand as written.

**G3 — Fertility suppression's argument.** As specified it reads NOMINAL `d` (`:136`, bit-identical).
The numbers: at Libur's `d = 0.30` suppression is 0.1, removing 90 % of a decade's births ≈ 38 pp
of the population — the "39 pp missing births" half of the destruction the director's own
decomposition names, of which the reservoir returns 50 % later. Options: (a) nominal (as
specified; the kernel's 1/3 full-stop stays where it is; STRESS still loses up to 60 % of births
at `d = 0.2`); (b) suppression reads `d_eff` outside FAMINE (one effective deficit for both
exceptional demographic channels; at `d = 0.30` suppression 0.625, ≈ 16 pp; STRESS births
unsuppressed, growth then limited only by the headroom cap's "births ≤ deaths" when the influx is
short; the full-stop moves to `d = 0.467`); (c) a re-derived slope — refused (tuning).
**Recommendation: (b)** if the director reads "pathological destruction" as its own 49 + 39 pp
decomposition; (a) if fertility is to remain the first physiological response at any shortfall.
The mechanism takes either as one argument; both arms are tested.

**G4 — Packet placement.** T4.21 as a post-certification M4 packet (M4 is "at the exit gate, not
closed", `milestones.md:285,361`) versus an M5 hotfix spec under S8 §4/§4.1 if the director closes
M4 first. Recommendation: T4.21.

**G5 — Livestock/fish under the ladder** given the open T4.20 storage-asymmetry ruling
(`milestones.md:357`, `queue.md:1348-1352`): the disaster multiplies BOTH food paths exactly as
weather does, "herding → 0" is read from the sector row, and `FoodHeadroom` counts non-staples only

**G6 — The pastoralist and the STOP condition on one-directional substitution (NEW; challenger #5).**
The director's abandonment rule says `farming = 0 ∧ herding > 0` is productive and NOT famine; the
basket (`needs.json`, grain 0.9) and one-directional substitution (`ConsumptionSystem.cs:150-176`)

the ladder it is SEVERE with `d_eff = 0.925`: 67 % of adults starve per decade on those turns —
famine magnitude under a non-famine label, which is exactly the situation the mandate's STOP
condition ("STOP if famine semantics require changing one-directional substitution") was written
for. Options: **(a)** rule that pastoralists starve by design in this world (livestock is a
supplement, not a staple — D-035 as ratified); record it; `S_Pastoralist_Pipeline` pins the
outcome; **(b)** STOP → a substitution CR (livestock/fish substitute for grain at a stated

basket fact (T4.5 finding), and (c) is a label without a mechanism. Whatever is ruled, both
single-sector pipeline tests ship (§6.5).

**G7 — Refugees refused by vacancy (NEW; challenger #1).** Under §3.5c a source whose every viable

**G8 — The dt-vs-buffer artefact makes "famine-class" a dt-dependent property (NEW; F4; challenger

## §13. ORCHESTRATOR RULINGS AND RATIONALE (T4.21-0, 2026-09-17)

Recorded under the director's 2026-09-17 mandate, which grants the orchestrator ownership of
decomposition. Each paragraph states the ruling and the reasoning; the director may override any of
them, and G1 is escalated rather than decided. The ruling text itself is in the header block and in
`docs/adr/cr-015-famine-is-exceptional.md` ("Orchestrator decisions under the mandate's ownership
grant"); this section is the rationale.

**G1 — LEAVE, and ESCALATE (option (a) now; (b) recorded as a separate finding).** The
stationary-variance-at-every-dt property of `HarvestWeatherSystem` is not an accident: the
`√(1 − ρ²)` factor was DELIBERATELY ratified by T3.4b and re-measured by T3.4c (`m3-spec.md:52-53`,
`HarvestWeatherSystem.cs:42-45` "holds the STATIONARY variance at Sigma² for every dt … the era table
altering the climate"), and the mandate says in terms that weather is not to be modified in order
to prevent famine ("weather stays stochastic and unclamped"). F1's finding — that the OUTPUT
multiplier should carry the turn-mean's variance `g(dt/τ)`, 0.427 at dt 10 — is therefore a genuine
conflict between a frozen, ratified invariant and law 3, not a tuning preference, and S8 §3 says a
genuine conflict is recorded, derived and escalated, never silently applied inside an unrelated
packet. Applying `g` would also be a fourth behavioural cause moving every golden, which is exactly
what challenger #10 said must never share an attribution step with the layout move. So: T4.21
ships with weather byte-identical, the derivation and its consequences (`P(decade ≤ 0.57)` 3.9 % as
shipped vs 0.25 % corrected) are written into CR-015 §6 with options (a)/(b)/(c) and the
recommendation (b)-as-its-own-packet, and T4.21-W is not built. The redesign's mechanisms are
specified to be correct under either ruling (§1.2, §3.2 R1), so nothing in T4.21-1..6 waits on it.

**G2 — re-anchor phase 1; add the abandonment twin; phases 2–3 stand.** `Famine_MortalitySpike`
asserts a ≥ 1.35× per-capita mortality spike from a ~0.2 deficit on a Default-sector, weather-free,
disaster-free rig. Under the mandate's own words that rig is FOOD STRESS, and "only FAMINE activates
the exceptional response"; the test's teeth are real but its AIM is now the opposite of the ruled
semantics (ADR-015 §7.2 — teeth are not aim). It cannot be re-pinned honestly, so phase 1 is
re-anchored to assert what the system ought now to do (STRESS does NOT spike), and a twin with a
0 %-farm order (an abandonment FAMINE rig) keeps the original spike assertion alive where it is
now true. Phases 2 (birth deficit) and 3 (rebound) test properties that survive the ladder as
written and are left alone.

**G3 — (b): suppression reads the effective deficit outside FAMINE.** The director's own
decomposition of the Libur episode is the evidence: the cascade's M4 arm (`cascade-report.md:140,178`,
committed under `docs/t4.21-evidence/`) leaves a 59 % trough that is 49 pp deaths + 39 pp missing
births, and the missing births come from `suppression = max(0, 1 − 3.0·0.30) = 0.1` — a 90 %
suppression of a decade's births from a decade that under the ladder is SEVERE STRESS, not FAMINE.
A near-total birth stop is precisely the exceptional demographic response the mandate reserves for
FAMINE; the physiological frame that justifies a dead-zone on mortality ("a sustained 15–25 % cut
is survivable") justifies it equally on fertility, and one effective deficit for both exceptional
channels is one mechanism, not two. Under (b) the birth full-stop moves to nominal `d = 0.4667`
outside FAMINE and stays at `1/3` inside it; the rebound RELEASE gate stays on nominal `d == 0.0`
byte-identically, because release is a recovery signal (is there any shortfall at all?), not a
response magnitude. The mechanism takes the argument as one parameter and both arms are tested
(§6.4), so (a) remains one config line away if the director rules otherwise.

**G4 — T4.21.** M4 is "at the exit gate", not closed (`milestones.md:285,361`); T4.17–T4.20 are
the precedent for post-certification packets numbered in the M4 sequence and merged by the
director's ruling; no `T4.21` reference exists anywhere in the tree; and an "M5 hotfix spec" route
would have to carry the S8 §4.1 four items as M5 spec content, which is a larger instrument for the

**G5 — proceed; recorded against the open T4.20 storage ruling.** Everything the ladder needs from
livestock and fish is independent of how they are STORED: the disaster multiplies the two food
paths at the two places weather already multiplies them (`ProductionSystem.cs:239, :300-306`),

**G6 — (a): the pastoralist starves by basket design; not a STOP; substitution CR queued for M5.**
