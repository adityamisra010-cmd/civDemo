## 2. THE READINGS

### R1 — hand-set mix vs the derived default: **DIVERGED, and the decomposition is why**

Two arms, same world and seed (dev worldgen, seed 42, 40 turns), differing only in the sector
mix: **A** = the T3.5b derived default 55/15/10/12/8; **B** = a hand-set 40/10/15/25/10 (more
crafting, more extraction, less farming) — a policy the retired farm-% slider could not express.

| reading | A (default) | B (hand-set) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shelter | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| dwellings / maintenance m | 91 / 1.0000 | 91 / 1.0000 |
| Sustenance | 0.9103 | 0.9103 |
| **Comfort** | **0.7467** | **0.9048** |
| — pottery demand / eaten / fill | 90 / 90 / 1.0000 | 42 / 42 / 1.0000 |
| — pottery PRODUCED | 116 | 83 |
| — cloth demand / eaten / fill | 134 / 77 / 0.5746 | 63 / 55 / **0.8730** |
| — cloth PRODUCED | **77** | **55** |
| **population** | **444** | **213** |
| turnover rate | 0.08356/yr | 0.08216/yr |
| **grievance** | **10.68** | **6.92** |

**Pre-committed expectation vs what fired, item by item:**

- **Shelter does NOT move, because it is saturated** — **CONFIRMED**, and the §7.5 note written
  at pre-commitment time was the reason it was predicted: 1.0000 on both arms, dwellings
  identical at 91. A reading that had claimed "the better mix improved Shelter" could not have
  fired here, which is exactly why R1's headline load was placed on Comfort instead.
- **"Raise Comfort via the crafting arm"** — **DIVERGED.** Comfort did rise (0.7467 → 0.9048),
  and the naive reading — *more crafting share produced more goods* — is **WRONG**. The
  decomposition the spec pre-committed says so directly: **cloth production FELL, 77 → 55, and
  pottery production fell, 116 → 83, on the arm with more than double the crafting share.**
  What rose is the FILL RATIO (cloth 0.5746 → 0.8730), and it rose because **demand halved
  with the population: 444 → 213 people, cloth demand 134 → 63.**
- **"Lower grievance mainly through Comfort, since Shelter is pinned"** — **CONFIRMED in
  mechanism, inherited in cause.** Turnover is essentially unchanged (0.08356 vs 0.08216/yr),
  so the grievance fall is not demographic decay; it tracks Comfort. But Comfort's own move is
  the denominator effect above, so the grievance improvement inherits it.

**THE FINDING, STATED PLAINLY: the hand-set mix did not make the settlement more productive —
it made it SMALLER AND BETTER SUPPLIED.** Cutting farming 55 → 40 cut the food supply, the
population halved, and per-capita satisfaction rose because fewer people were dividing a
smaller pot. Every headline number "improved" while every production number fell.

**This is §7.15 catching a laundered conclusion on its first binding use.** Comfort is a
composite (fill = eaten ÷ demand, and demand scales with population), and the spec named that
composite and pre-committed the pottery/cloth decomposition plus the turnover arm BEFORE the
run. Without those, this packet would have reported "the new control lets the director improve
Comfort by 21%" — true as arithmetic, false as a claim about the control, and identical in
shape to T3.5b's density-denominator error that forced §7.15 into existence.

**Nothing was adjusted.** The T3.5b derived default stands (CR-003 §5.1 governs it); no rate,
weight or band was touched. The reading is reported, not acted on.

## 3. ITEM 2(b) — THE RIG, AND THE THIN SIDE THAT FIRED

Construction (stated in the rig's own comment, `TradePanelTests.TradeRig`): two hand-built
settlements, one distance row at travel cost 1.0, and **cloth** (bulkPerUnit 1.0, the lowest
non-numeraire bulk). Deadband = bulk × pathCost × costPerBulkCostUnit = 1.0 × 1.0 × 0.16 =
**0.16**; prices 0.50 / 2.00 give a gap of **1.50** — clearing the deadband ~9.4×.

**§7.16 in force: the asymmetric margin was named at the moment the numbers were chosen — and
the thin side fired on the first run.** The fat side is gap-vs-deadband (9.4×). The THIN side is
the FLOW QUANTITY: `units = f × (gap − deadband) / sensitivity`, floored to whole units, so a
rig with a real gap can still produce zero rows and look exactly like the zero-flow world it
exists to contrast with. It did:

- sensitivity = λ·dt·(priceLow/scaleLow + priceHigh/scaleHigh), and `MarketScale` floors at
  `marketScaleFloorPerYear × dt = 1.0` for a market with no stock and no production.
- The first rig gave the BUYER nothing — it is the settlement that *wants* cloth — so
  scaleHigh = 1.0, sensitivity = 0.4 × (0.5/1e6 + 2.0/1.0) = 0.8, and
  `units = (long)(0.25 × 1.34 / 0.8) = (long)0.418 = 0`. **No flow, from a rig whose gap cleared
  its deadband 9.4×.**
- **The binding quantity is the BUYER'S MARKET DEPTH, not the seller's stock** — the
  counter-intuitive part, and the reason it is recorded in the rig's comment rather than quietly
  fixed.

Repaired by giving the buyer depth (100,000) and the seller enough (2,000,000) that the sweep-2
stock cap does not bind. Measured after: **deadband 0.1600, gap 1.5000, rows 1, moved 206,790**
— matching the hand computation (0.335 / 1.62e-6 = 206,790) exactly, which is the check that
the mechanism is understood rather than merely made to pass. The test asserts `moved > 1`, not
`> 0`: "> 0" would sit one rounding step from vacuous.

The panel is asserted against the flows the sim produced (direction low→high, good name,
quantity), never against hand-written expectations. Two further discriminating cases:
`GapUnderTheDeadband_ReadsAsTheDeadband_NotAsNoSpread` (a 0.10 spread under the 0.16 deadband
must read differently from no spread at all — without it the two reasons could collapse into one
undifferentiated "nothing moved") and `Numeraire_IsLabelled` (grain cannot trade by
construction; counting it as a silent good would be a fabricated defect in the panel's summary).

## 4. THE GUARD, AND ITS MEASURED REDS (§7.4; §7.17 NOT triggered)

The packet adds exactly one guard: **Σ = 0 refused**. An all-zero allocation normalizes to five
zero shares — every sector pool empty, the settlement silently working at nothing, no log line
saying so. The sim *survives* it (`Sectors.Share` returns 0.0, never NaN), which is what makes
it dangerous: the config-fails-quietly class, as an order.

**§7.17 does not attach**: this is a NEW guard in new code, not a widening of an existing one.
No existing guard was modified — `OrderLog.ValidateRecord` is untouched. The trigger condition
is stated in the spec so it would be recognised rather than rationalised if implementation had
drifted into an existing guard.

Two arms, each defeated **alone** and measured (the T3.8 red-2 lesson: a red taken with two arms
down is ambiguous and proves nothing about either):

| arm | defeat | measured red |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Create`'s Σ ≤ 0 throw | `sum <= 0` → `sum <= -1` | `AllZeroAllocation_IsRefused…` FAILED |
| `CanSubmit`'s sum test | `sum > 0` → `sum >= 0` | `AllZeroAllocation_IsRefused…` FAILED (`Assert.False() — Expected False, Actual True`), AND `Session_Emits…RefusesGhostIds` FAILED by **throwing** `ArgumentException` out of `Create` |

The second row is the informative one: with only `CanSubmit` defeated, the factory's throw
becomes the backstop, so the session's contract ("returns false, writes nothing") degrades into
an unhandled exception. The two arms have genuinely distinct jobs — clean refusal vs hard
backstop — and the reds show it rather than merely failing. A first attempt at this proof
restored nothing (the file is untracked, so `git checkout --` silently did not apply) and
therefore ran with BOTH arms down; that run was discarded as ambiguous and both proofs redone
from a verified-green baseline.
