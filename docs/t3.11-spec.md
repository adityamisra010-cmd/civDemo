## 1. ITEM 1 — THE DRIVEN GOLDEN (BLOCKING)

### 1.1 The gap, and how much of it is still open

The blocking gap, in the M3 spec's words: every pinned golden runs the all-farming default, so
no good but grain flows, every price sits at exactly 1.0, the price step's exponent is exactly
0, and **ADR-016 changed the solver's mathematics while leaving all three goldens
byte-identical.**

Two later measurements, both in the tree, say how much of that is still true:

- **T3.6** measured a no-order founded world at 100 turns: `totalFlow = 0`, but **50 non-grain
  price series OFF 1.0**. So the goldens already exercise the PRICE side; the FLOW side is
  untouched. Half the gap closed itself when T3.2b/T3.5b spread production across sectors.
- **T3.9b** shipped the sector-allocation UI over `OrderKind.SectorAllocation = 3`, which has
  existed since T3.3 with a turn-exact delivery pin. The order needed to drive settlements
  existed all along.

**A consequence this packet must therefore measure rather than assume:** if the existing
founded golden already has non-grain prices off 1.0, it may ALREADY be sensitive to a price-step
perturbation. The red proof is run against **both** goldens, and what the driven golden adds is
reported honestly — it may be flow sensitivity alone, or price sensitivity of a different
magnitude, and either is a real answer. Claiming the new golden closed a gap the old one had
already half-closed would be exactly the kind of unmeasured claim this project keeps catching.

### 1.2 The driven world — mixes chosen, and why each

Canonical world (1024², N = 12), seed 42, 300 turns — the same world and horizon as the existing
founded golden, so the two differ ONLY in the order log and are directly comparable.

Driven by **`SectorAllocation` orders in the log**, never by changing a default: T3.5b's mix is
derived from a reference class and CR-003 §5.1 governs it.

**ASYMMETRY IS THE POINT.** T3.6's queue line gives the reason: a uniform mix produces no
comparative advantage, every settlement prices every good the same way, and every gap stays
identically zero — which is escalation 2's signature and precisely the condition under which a
"driven" golden would drive nothing. Settlements are therefore specialised into three groups by
`settlementIndex % 3`, a fixed integer rule with no RNG and no ordering over doubles:

| group | settlements | mix (farm/herd/extract/craft/build) | WHY THIS MIX |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A — granary** | 0, 3, 6, 9 | 70 / 10 / 5 / 5 / 10 | Food surplus and little else. Its non-food stocks stay thin, so its non-food prices should sit HIGH — the buy side of any pair. |
| **B — quarry** | 1, 4, 7, 10 | 30 / 10 / 45 / 5 / 10 | Raw extraction (timber, stone, clay, ores). Deep raw stocks, so raw prices LOW — the sell side for raws, and the buy side for crafted goods it does not make. |
| **C — workshop** | 2, 5, 8, 11 | 30 / 10 / 10 / 40 / 10 | Crafting (pottery, cloth, tools, bronze). Consumes raws, accumulates crafted goods — crafted prices LOW, raw prices HIGH. The mirror of B, which is what a spread needs. |

Every mix keeps farming ≥ 30 and construction at 10 deliberately: a golden that starves or stops
maintaining housing would re-measure T3.8's collapse instead of the goods economy, and a
regression instrument should exercise one thing loudly rather than everything faintly. Weights
are raw and normalise in the consumer (`Sectors.Share`), so the stated numbers are the applied
shares because each row sums to 100.

Orders land as ONE batch at **turn 2** — after founding settles, before the economy develops —
so the whole 300-turn trajectory runs driven. Delivery semantics are already pinned turn-exactly
by T3.3/T3.9b; this golden depends on them rather than re-proving them.

### 1.3 The red proof — two perturbations, each stating what it represents

Acceptance per the spec: the new golden must FAIL when the price step is perturbed, proven red
per §7.4. The step is, verbatim from `PriceSystem`:

```
double exponent = consumptionRate + inputDemandRate + productionRate + stockReleaseRate;
double raw = prevPrice * (Math.Exp(exponent) - 1.0);
```

| # | perturbation | what it REPRESENTS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P1** | `raw = prevPrice * exponent` (drop the exponential; keep the linear term) | **THE ADR-016 REGRESSION ITSELF** — reverting the solver from the exact closed form to the linear step. This is the exact class of change that left all three goldens byte-identical, so a golden that does not catch P1 has not closed the blocking gap, whatever else it catches. |
| **P2** | `exponent = consumptionRate + inputDemandRate + productionRate` (drop `stockReleaseRate`) | **A TERM-ATTRIBUTION REGRESSION** — the solver still exponentiates correctly, but one measured input stops driving price at all. A different failure mode from P1: P1 breaks the integration, P2 breaks an input. One arm can pass by luck; two failing differently is evidence. |

**§7.5 applies and is stated at design time:** grain is pinned at exactly 1.0 as the numeraire,
so its price rests against its own definition and a red proof whose only subject is grain proves
nothing. Both perturbations are therefore evidenced on NON-GRAIN prices, and the proof reports
which non-grain goods moved, not merely that the hash changed.

Perturbations are applied one at a time, measured, and reverted; neither is ever committed.

## 2. PRE-COMMITTED READINGS (§7.13, with §7.15 discrimination statements)

### D1 — WILL GOODS FLOW?

**Observable:** total `TradeFlows` volume across the driven run, per good.

**COMPOSITE? YES — and the packet's own instructions say why:** volume can move because price
GAPS opened, or because STOCKS changed (a gap that clears the deadband still moves nothing if
the seller holds nothing, and the T3.9b rig measured the buyer's market DEPTH as the binding
quantity in exactly that way). "Zero volume" is likewise composite: no spread at all, versus a
real spread under the deadband — two different states of the world with the same reading.

**PRE-COMMITTED DECOMPOSITION.** Whatever the volume, report per good, separately:
1. the **price SPREAD** across settlements (max − min) — did specialisation open a gap at all;
2. the **deadband** `bulk × pathCost × 0.16` for the reachable pairs — was any gap large enough;
3. the seller-side **stock** — was there anything to move.
The T3.9b `TradeModel` classification (`Flowed` / `GapZero` / `GapNoFlow` / `Numeraire`) is
exactly this decomposition and is reused rather than re-derived.

**Readings:**
- **Nonzero** → report volume and which goods; the golden is genuinely driven on the flow side.
- **ZERO even under asymmetric mixes** → this is the **EXPECTED** outcome given T3.6's
  escalations 1 and 2, and is **NOT this packet's failure**. Then state which half of the
  blocking gap closed (price/production) and which did not (flow), and escalate the remainder to
  M4 beside the existing escalations. **DO NOT** tune bulk values, the deadband, the price band
  or `f` to manufacture flow — every one of those is frozen or already escalated.

### D2 — THE RED PROOF

**Observable:** the driven golden's `WorldHash` under P1 and under P2, against its clean value.

**COMPOSITE? NO** — a hash is atomic; it changes or it does not. But the INFERENCE from it is
composite ("the golden exercises the solver" could be satisfied by any state change, including
one that has nothing to do with prices), so the proof additionally reports **which non-grain
prices moved**, per §7.5. Hash change alone is necessary, not sufficient.

**Readings:**
- **Hash changes under both perturbations** → acceptance met.
- **Hash does NOT change** → the driven world still is not exercising the solver. That is a
  BIGGER finding than the original gap. **Report and STOP.**

**Also measured, not assumed** (§1.1): the same two perturbations are run against the EXISTING
founded golden. If it also changes, the driven golden's marginal contribution is stated
accurately rather than overclaimed.
