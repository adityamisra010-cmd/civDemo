## THE REFERENCE CLASS — FIXED HERE, BEFORE MEASURING WHAT THE WORLD DOES WITH IT

Early Bronze Age (~6000–2000 BCE)* — the era the sim's opening actually depicts. **Not** classical
city-states, which are a later and sparser class describing polities rather than villages.

Reference points, stated with what each describes:
- **Sumerian cities, 30–50 km apart** (director's starting point) — but these are CITIES with

- **Athens–Corinth ~80 km** — classical POLIS spacing; recorded because the director named it,
  and explicitly treated as an UPPER bound from a later era, not a target.
- The class's own centre of mass: **village-to-village spacing in a settled agrarian landscape is
  of order 10–30 km**, which is a day's walk out and back to the far edge of a worked catchment.

**Derivation rule, fixed now:** the sim's settlement is a SETTLEMENT-PLUS-HINTERLAND (the
catchment is 50 km), so the sim's unit is closest to the **city-with-hinterland** end, not the
village end. Spacing should therefore be **derived from the catchment the sim actually works**,
with the reference class as the sanity band.

# T4.1b WIDENING — THE DEPOSIT SCALE FOLLOWS THE SPACING

**Director ruling, 2026-08-08. Declared, not silent (the `f8a19e1` precedent). Written BEFORE any
measurement of the new value (§7.13).** Two constants now move and they move TOGETHER.

## A6's FIRST REAL OUTING, RECORDED

Branch 2 offered (a) green with exactly seven moved, (b) an eighth moved, (c) red after re-pinning.
**The outcome matched none of them** — exactly seven moved, but three were semantic guards rather
than pins. The packet reported and halted instead of mapping the result onto the nearest branch.
**A6 was written for exactly this and it worked the first time it was tested.**

## WHERE THE COUPLING ACTUALLY LIVES — MEASURED FROM THE CODE, NOT ASSUMED

`WorldFounding.cs:198-211`: a deposit is a **POINT SAMPLE of a terrain channel at the settlement's
site cell**, times `1 + depositSpread × U`:

```
moisture  -> t.Moisture[site]                        (livestock, timber, fiber, hides)
elevation -> max(0, t.Elevation[site] - seaLevel)    (stone, copper-ore, tin-ore)
water     -> t.Moisture[site]^2                      (fish, clay)
```

**`depositSpread` is declared ONLY for copper-ore (0.8) and tin-ore (0.9)** — it defaults to 0 for
every other good. `goods.json`'s own `_doc` says why: *"ores add depositSpread — a wide seeded
spread that makes ore geography uneven, the precondition for comparative advantage."*

**So the design has TWO sources of unevenness, and only one of them is written down.** Ores get an
explicit seeded spread. **Everything else gets its unevenness from the TERRAIN, sampled at
settlement separations** — and that source is entirely implicit. It worked because 480 km happened
to exceed the terrain's own correlation length. **Nothing declared that dependency. That is the
undeclared coupling, named precisely.**

## THE RELATION, STATED WITH ITS JUSTIFICATION

The moisture field is built with **`moistureDecayPx = 40.0`** = **160 km** — its correlation
length. Two settlements yield distinguishable moisture samples **only if their separation exceeds
that correlation length**; inside one decay length they are sampling the same feature and must
return the same number.

| spacing | separation ÷ moisture decay length |
| --- | --- |
| 480 km (shipped) | **3.0** — three correlation lengths apart, samples independent |
| 95.2 km (ruled) | **0.60** — inside one correlation length, samples identical |

**THE RELATION: the deposit channel's correlation length must scale with the spacing, preserving
the separation-to-correlation ratio the shipped world was implicitly tuned at.**

```
moistureDecayKm(new) = minSpacingKm(new) ÷ 3.0 = 95.2 ÷ 3.0 = 31.73 km
moistureDecayPx(new) = 31.73 ÷ 4.0 km-per-px  = 7.93 px
```

**Why the ratio and not the length is the invariant:** the length is meaningless on its own — it is
a distance compared against a distance. What the old world guaranteed was that neighbours sat three
correlation lengths apart, and that guarantee is what produced heterogeneous endowments. Preserving
the ratio preserves the *property*; preserving the length preserves only a number.

**THIS IS NOT A SWEEP.** The value falls out of the ratio; it was not searched for, and
`GoodsTests` was not consulted in deriving it. **CR-003 §5.1: fitting a constant to a test is
forbidden, and the test here IS the property being restored.** If 7.93 px does not restore
heterogeneity, the relation is wrong and gets reported as wrong — **it does not get iterated.**

## PRE-COMMITTED READINGS (§7.15 — observable named, composite status stated)

**Observable:** the twelve founding deposit abundances per deposit-bearing good, seed 42.
**COMPOSITE? YES** — "heterogeneity restored" spans several goods on two different channels

- **Heterogeneity restored — `GoodsTests`' property true again** → report the measured deposit

**BLAST RADIUS, STATED IN ADVANCE BECAUSE IT IS LARGE:** moisture is not confined to deposits — it
drives **fertility**, hence food, hence the whole demography. A 5× change in its correlation length
is a change to the world's climate geography, not a deposit tweak. **The third branch firing is the
outcome I expect**, and expecting it is not a reason to skip the measurement.

# THE DEPOSIT MODEL — OPTION (b) RULED: SAMPLE THE CATCHMENT

**Director ruling, 2026-08-08. Statistic stated and justified BEFORE measuring (§7.13, CR-003
§5.1).** Option (a) was ruled out as a law-2 modifier; option (c) as unanswerable (elevation does
not determine livestock capacity).

## THE PREMISE, CHECKED BEFORE BUILDING ON IT — AND IT HOLDS, WITH ONE CORRECTION

The ruling says *"arable already works this way."* **Verified: it does, in footprint but NOT in
statistic**, and the difference is dimensional.

`CatchmentSummaryRow.EffectiveArableKm2` (`WorldState.cs:81-87`) is *"in FERTILITY-WEIGHTED km² —
the land content of a hypothetical ideal-suitability (f = 1.0) km², **summed over the catchment's
lattice blocks**."* **Arable is a SUM.** It is an EXTENT: land area adds, and a bigger catchment
means more arable.

**A deposit abundance is not an extent.** `WorldFounding.cs:209-211` uses it as a multiplier on
extraction RATES (T3.3), and the shipped roll is `channel × spread` — a dimensionless intensity in
roughly [0, 1]. **Summing it over a catchment would make abundance grow with catchment SIZE**,
which is the CR-002 error in a new costume: a per-unit-land property read as a total.

## THE STATISTIC, DERIVED FROM THE DIMENSION

> **AREA-WEIGHTED MEAN of the channel over the settlement's hinterland footprint, land cells only.**

Justified term by term, and each term is forced rather than chosen:

1. **MEAN, not sum** — because abundance is an **intensity** (per-unit-land), and arable is an
   **extent** (km²). Matching the statistic to the dimension is S8 §4.1 requirement 2's discipline
   applied to a value rather than a unit. **A sum here would be dimensionally wrong.**
2. **AREA-WEIGHTED** — equal-area pixels, so the mean over pixels IS the area-weighted mean. Stated
   explicitly because it stops being true the moment anyone samples the lattice instead (stride-4
   blocks are equal-area, pixels within a partial block are not).
3. **THE HINTERLAND FOOTPRINT** (`hinterlandRadiusKm = 50`, `sim.json:10`) — the same radius the
   catchment uses, so the deposit and the arable describe **the same territory**. This is the
   consistency the ruling asks for.
4. **LAND CELLS ONLY** — ocean is not hinterland. Including it would make coastal deposits a
   function of how much sea a disc happens to contain, which is a siting artefact, not geography.

**WHY THIS IS NOT A SWEEP:** every term above is fixed by a dimensional or definitional argument
and none was chosen with reference to `GoodsTests`. **No alternative statistic was tried.** If the
mean does not restore heterogeneity, the pre-commitment says report — and it explicitly forbids
falling back to option (a) inside this packet.

**ONE HONEST NOTE ON WHY IT MIGHT WORK, STATED AS A MECHANISM RATHER THAN A HOPE:** the site cell
saturates because siting selects for water access. **A 50 km disc is not selected for anything** —
it contains whatever the map put there, and at 4 km per pixel it spans ~490 land cells reaching
inland past the shore band. The mean over that disc can differ between settlements even where every
centre reads 1.0.

## THE ORDERING CONSTRAINT, MEASURED NOT ASSUMED

Deposits are rolled inside `WorldFounding`, where `world.Terrain` exists but **the catchment does
not** — `CatchmentSummaries` are owned by `CatchmentSystem` and computed from turn 1 (D-016). **The
footprint must therefore be computed from terrain geometry at founding, not read from the catchment
table.** That is a real constraint on the implementation and it is why the wording above says
"hinterland footprint" rather than "the catchment rows".

- **Still uniform** → catchment sampling is not the cause either. **Report; DO NOT try option (a)

was always going to be wrong.**
