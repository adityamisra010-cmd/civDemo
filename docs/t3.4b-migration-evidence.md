# T3.4b — migration evidence (pre-T3.2b baseline)

**Source:** director session on art build `ec19898`, pre-T3.2b weights. Recorded verbatim at the
director's instruction so the evidence lives in the repo rather than in a chronicle file outside
it. The settlement name in the original ("Nenatul") is a RUNTIME-GENERATED name from that run's
chronicle, not a repo artifact — it greps empty here by construction, which is why the evidence
is transcribed rather than referenced.

## The recorded observation, verbatim

> Twelve-settlement run, 1,070 sim-years, six labor orders all on settlement 2.
> Settlement 8 emitted 60+ migration-surge events continuously from year 30 to 1070, at 4%–40% of
> population per season, oscillating (40 → 32 → 28 → 7 → 34) rather than converging. No other
> settlement surged even once in the entire run. The ordered settlement never appeared in the
> migration log at all.
>
> Reading to verify: the land-per-capita term dominates attractiveness, so a settlement with
> structurally poor arable-per-capita becomes a permanent population pump — births refill,
> migration drains, the gradient never closes.
>
> Persistent export from marginal land is historically REAL and must survive the re-derivation.
> What must NOT survive: (a) 40%-in-one-season magnitudes, (b) the non-convergent oscillation,
> (c) one settlement absorbing all migration activity while eleven show none.

## What this binds in T3.4b

Two acceptance additions, directed:

1. **Report the post-correction profile** of the equivalent settlement against this baseline —
   surge count, per-event magnitude range, and whether the sequence converges.
2. **A corridor test** that no single settlement may account for more than a stated share of
   world migration events over a long run, **with teeth proven against this recorded pre-fix
   behaviour** — i.e. the test must FAIL when run against a configuration reproducing the
   one-settlement-absorbs-everything profile above (ADR-015 §7.4: prove the red).

## Reading it correctly

The distinction the director drew is the whole point and must not be flattened during the
re-derivation. Marginal land exporting people persistently is a real historical pattern and the
model should keep producing it. Three specific things are pathological rather than realistic:

- **Magnitude.** 40% of a settlement's population moving in one season is not migration, it is
  evacuation. Real sustained out-migration from poor land runs at a small percentage per year.
- **Non-convergence.** `40 → 32 → 28 → 7 → 34` is an oscillator, not a settling gradient. A
  population pump that never closes its own gradient means the attractiveness signal is not
  responding to the population change it causes.
- **Degeneracy of coverage.** One settlement out of twelve accounting for 100% of migration
  events, while the *ordered* settlement never appears at all, indicates the term is keying on a
  structural constant of the map rather than on evolving state.

The last one is the tell: the land signal is a fixed property of the site, so a settlement poor
in arable-per-capita is poor forever, and nothing any player does can move it.
