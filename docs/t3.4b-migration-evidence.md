# T3.4b — migration evidence (pre-T3.2b baseline)

**Source:** director session on art build `ec19898`, pre-T3.2b weights. Recorded verbatim at the
director's instruction so the evidence lives in the repo rather than in a chronicle file outside
it. The settlement name in the original ("Nenatul") is a RUNTIME-GENERATED name from that run's
chronicle, not a repo artifact — it greps empty here by construction, which is why the evidence
is transcribed rather than referenced.

## The recorded observation, verbatim

> Twelve-settlement run, 1,070 sim-years, six labor orders all on settlement 2.
> Settlement 8 emitted 60+ migration-surge events continuously from year 30 to 1070, at 4%–40% of
> population per season, oscillating (40 → 32 → 28 → 7 → 34) rather than converging. No other
> settlement surged even once in the entire run. The ordered settlement never appeared in the
> migration log at all.
>
> Reading to verify: the land-per-capita term dominates attractiveness, so a settlement with
> structurally poor arable-per-capita becomes a permanent population pump — births refill,
> migration drains, the gradient never closes.
>
> Persistent export from marginal land is historically REAL and must survive the re-derivation.
> What must NOT survive: (a) 40%-in-one-season magnitudes, (b) the non-convergent oscillation,
> (c) one settlement absorbing all migration activity while eleven show none.

## What this binds in T3.4b

Two acceptance additions, directed:

1. **Report the post-correction profile** of the equivalent settlement against this baseline —
   surge count, per-event magnitude range, and whether the sequence converges.
2. **A corridor test** that no single settlement may account for more than a stated share of
   world migration events over a long run, **with teeth proven against this recorded pre-fix
   behaviour** — i.e. the test must FAIL when run against a configuration reproducing the
   one-settlement-absorbs-everything profile above (ADR-015 §7.4: prove the red).

## Reading it correctly

The distinction the director drew is the whole point and must not be flattened during the
re-derivation. Marginal land exporting people persistently is a real historical pattern and the
model should keep producing it. Three specific things are pathological rather than realistic:

- **Magnitude.** 40% of a settlement's population moving in one season is not migration, it is
  evacuation. Real sustained out-migration from poor land runs at a small percentage per year.
- **Non-convergence.** `40 → 32 → 28 → 7 → 34` is an oscillator, not a settling gradient. A
  population pump that never closes its own gradient means the attractiveness signal is not
  responding to the population change it causes.
- **Degeneracy of coverage.** One settlement out of twelve accounting for 100% of migration
  events, while the *ordered* settlement never appears at all, indicates the term is keying on a
  structural constant of the map rather than on evolving state.

The last one is the tell: the land signal is a fixed property of the site, so a settlement poor
in arable-per-capita is poor forever, and nothing any player does can move it.

# POST-CORRECTION MEASUREMENT (T3.4b, weather-live, re-derived weights)

Measured on the dev world, seed 42, **9 settlements**, 1074 sim-years, full pipeline.

**The 12-settlement world of the baseline is no longer constructible.** T3.2b raised
`minSpacingKm` to 480 and the map now places at most 9 sites (`settlement siting could only place
9 of 12`). Stated rather than silently substituted: the comparison below is 9-vs-12 on the
STRUCTURAL properties the ruling names — concentration, magnitude, convergence — not on absolute
counts.

| property | recorded pre-fix | post-correction | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| surge events (≥4% of pop in one turn), world total | 60+, continuous yr 30→1070 | **4** in 1074 years | resolved |
| worst settlement's surge count | 60+ | **3** | resolved |
| peak single-turn out-migration | **40%** | **6.1%** | (a) resolved |
| worst settlement's sequence | 40 → 32 → 28 → 7 → 34 (oscillating) | **6% → 5% → 4%** (monotone, converging) | (b) resolved |
| settlements with ANY migration | 1 of 12; eleven at zero | **7 of 9** | (c) resolved |
| worst settlement's share of world migration VOLUME | ~100% | **17.8%** (uniform would be 11.1%) | (c) resolved |

Per-settlement volume share: `15.9 12.1 16.2 15.2 0.0 9.2 13.6 0.0 17.8`.

**Persistent export from marginal land SURVIVED**, as the ruling requires: S8 is still both the
highest-volume exporter and the most surge-prone settlement. What changed is that it now does so
at realistic magnitude, converging, alongside neighbours who also move.

## The gross-magnitude corridor — evidence for the director's ruling

`MagnitudeCorridor_FedPhaseDrift_WithTeeth` measures **0.43 %/decade** against a `[0.05, 0.30]`
corridor. It was **not** widened.

**It is not the surge pathology.** The profile above shows 4 qualifying surges in a millennium,
peaking at 6.1%, converging, spread across 7 settlements. The 0.43% is broad low-level movement,
not concentrated evacuation.

**It is not reachable by tuning the obvious knob.** Measured:

| `baseRatePerYear` | 0.03 | 0.018 | 0.012 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gross migration | 0.43 | 0.41 | **0.42** |

Halving and then near-quartering the base rate barely moves it, and non-monotonically. That is
the signature of a **binding cap**: the T2.8 gap-closing cap is `GapClosingFraction × m*`, where
`m* = (R_j·P_i − R_i·P_j)/(R_i + R_j)` depends only on resources and population.
`BaseRatePerYear` cancels out entirely whenever the cap binds — which, with weather continually
re-opening attractiveness gaps, is most of the time.

The lever that does move it is `GapClosingFraction`:

| `gapClosingFraction` | 0.30 | 0.25 (shipped) | 0.15 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gross migration | 0.45 | 0.43 | 0.37 |

Extrapolating, reaching 0.30 %/decade needs roughly **0.03** — which would not be tuning, it
would be **switching off the T2.8 stabiliser** that exists to make overshoot structurally
impossible.

## Reading (implementing agent's; the director rules)

The corridor was calibrated against a world with no weather and a dead land signal — a migration
process that barely responded to anything. `[0.05, 0.30]` measured the drift of a nearly-static
system. Weather gives migration something real to respond to, and the measured 0.43% is that
response: broad, converging, spread across settlements, with the recorded pathology gone on every
axis the ruling named.

So the honest options are (i) the corridor's basis has changed and it should be re-derived
against a weather-live world, or (ii) 0.43 %/decade is genuinely too fast for the period and the
weather amplitude (`sigmaLogYield`) is the parameter to revisit, not the migration mechanism.
The evidence does not distinguish these — that is a judgement about the target world, which is
the director's. What the evidence does settle is that it is **not** the surge pathology, and that
it **cannot** be fixed by the migration rate knob.

# ADDENDUM — the 12→9 settlement finding, and the sigma derivation

## The baseline world is no longer constructible

`settlement siting could only place 9 of 12 sites at minSpacingKm 480`. T3.2b raised
`minSpacingKm` to 480 km, and the dev continent now admits at most **9** settlements. The
baseline's 12-settlement world cannot be rebuilt, so every comparison above is 9-vs-12 on
STRUCTURE (concentration, magnitude, convergence) rather than on absolute counts.

**This interacts with the M4 colonization item and is queued.** A spacing rule that caps a
continent at 9 sites is a constraint expansion will hit immediately: colonization means founding
NEW settlements, and there is nowhere to put the tenth. Either `minSpacingKm` is
colonization-aware (a founding rule, not a worldgen-only rule), or the continent grows, or
expansion saturates at 9 and the Malthusian transition arrives by map exhaustion rather than by
land filling — which would be the trap hardwired by geometry, exactly what CR-003 forbids.

## sigmaLogYield was CHOSEN; now DERIVED — and it does not help

Tested first, as the cheaper and legitimate answer. It failed, in the informative direction.

**Derivation.** Reference class: rain-fed cereal agriculture without irrigation or modern inputs.
Best-documented proxy: medieval English demesne wheat (Winchester Pipe Rolls, 13th–15th c.),
interannual yield CV commonly placed around **0.25–0.35**. Early agriculture should sit at least
that high — landrace seed, no systematic rotation — and this model adds a reason to expect the
upper part of the band: a ~50 km hinterland is a spatially small sample, with little internal
averaging to smooth the draw. Taking CV = 0.30 and inverting the lognormal relation
`CV = sqrt(exp(σ²) − 1)` gives `σ = sqrt(ln 1.09) = 0.2936`. The prior 0.18 implied CV = 0.18,
**below the entire reference band**, and was never derived from anything.

*(The CV figures are recalled from the historical literature and are not verifiable inside this
repo; the derivation chain from CV to σ is arithmetic and checkable.)*

**Measured effect — reported because it does not help:**

| `sigmaLogYield` | 0.18 (chosen) | 0.2462 (CV 0.25) | **0.2936 (derived, CV 0.30)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gross migration %/decade | 0.43 | 0.58 | **0.76** |

A properly derived sigma moves migration **further** out of the old band, not toward it. Adopted
regardless, per the T3.2b precedent that *out-of-band with a derived constant beats in-band with
a fitted one*.

**A second consequence, worth its own line.** At the derived sigma the deliberate famine rigs
stopped being distinguishable from background weather: `Famine_MortalitySpike` measured the
famine arm at **lower** per-capita mortality than its own baseline (0.609 vs 0.707), because the
baseline had bad years too, and the grievance rig's window **fell** 5.96 → 2.04. Those are
controlled experiments; weather is a confound on both arms. They now run the pipeline minus
harvest weather, isolating the variable they exist to measure — the same treatment already given
to the hand-computed yield tests. This is not weather being suppressed; it is a control being
restored.
