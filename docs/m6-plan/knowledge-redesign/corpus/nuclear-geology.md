### 321 · Controlled nuclear fission (`nuclear_fission`)

A controlled chain reaction keeps uranium fission steady: each splitting nucleus releases two or three neutrons, and on average one causes the next fission. A moderator such as very pure graphite slows the neutrons; absorbing rods set the pace. The first such pile, CP-1, ran on 2 December 1942 beneath a Chicago stadium's stands, producing about half a watt.

### 323 · Thermonuclear weapon (`thermonuclear`)

A thermonuclear weapon uses a fission explosion to compress and heat hydrogen isotopes until they fuse, releasing far more energy than fission alone. The United States tested the first full-scale device, Ivy Mike, on 1 November 1952; the Soviet Union tested its own in November 1955. Such weapons became central to Cold War deterrence and later arms-control negotiations.

### 356 · Controlled fusion research (`nuclear_fusion_research`)

Controlled fusion research tries to join light nuclei such as deuterium and tritium in a plasma hot, dense and confined enough to yield net energy. ### 480 · Heavy-water reactors (`heavy_water_reactor`)

### 485 · Spent-fuel reprocessing (`reprocessing`)

Reprocessing dissolves spent reactor fuel in nitric acid and separates uranium and plutonium from fission products by solvent extraction, the PUREX method. Wartime separation chemistry worked out at Oak Ridge's X-10 was used at Hanford to extract weapons plutonium. Civil plants such as France's La Hague, operating since 1976, recycle plutonium into mixed-oxide fuel. The United States runs none.

### 487 · Radioactive ore processing (radium recovery and uranium milling) (`uranium_milling`)

Radioactive ore processing began with radium: in 1898 the Curies traced it through Joachimsthal pitchblende residues, and a Joachimsthal works held a radium monopoly in 1909-1913. ### 489 · Geophysical prospecting (magnetic, electrical, gravity and seismic) (`geophysical_prospecting`)

### 490 · Nuclear physics (nucleus, isotopes, neutron, fission) (`nuclear_physics`)

### 491 · Radiation detection and counting (`radiation_detection`)

### 492 · Radioactivity and radiochemistry (`radioactivity`)

Radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of radiation by unstable atoms. In 1896 Henri Becquerel found that uranium salts fogged wrapped photographic plates without sunlight. ### 493 · Radioisotope production and applications (`radioisotope_production`)

Radioisotopes can be made by bombarding stable atoms with particles. Irène Joliot-Curie and Frédéric Joliot made the first artificial radioactive isotope in 1934, and Ernest Lawrence's cyclotrons soon produced hundreds more. Reactors made isotopes plentiful: Oak Ridge sent its first, carbon-14, to a St Louis cancer hospital in August 1946. Cobalt-60 now sterilises medical devices, and iridium-192 serves industrial radiography.

### 494 · Radiometric prospecting (`radiometric_prospecting`)

Radiometric prospecting seeks uranium and thorium by detecting the gamma radiation around rocks rich in them. In the American uranium boom after the 1948 government price guarantee, Geological Survey teams drove roads with Geiger counters; airborne surveys were established by 1958. Modern spectrometers map potassium, uranium and thorium separately. About one exploration project in a thousand finds an economic deposit.
