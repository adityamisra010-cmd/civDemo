### 341 · Tractor and combine (`mechanised_agriculture`)

Engines began replacing draught animals. John Froelich's petrol tractor worked Iowa's 1892 threshing season; Benjamin Holt tested a tracked steam tractor on Californian peat in 1904. Mass-produced Fordsons followed from 1917, and combines reaped and threshed in one pass. Machinery helped shrink US farm work from over a third of workers in 1900 to under 2 percent by 2000.

### 391 · Drip irrigation (`drip_irrigation`)

Drip irrigation releases water slowly at the roots through plastic emitters, wasting less than flooding or sprinklers. Perforated-pipe trials predate it, but Israeli engineer Simcha Blass found that long, narrow passages inside an emitter slowed flow without the clogging of tiny holes. Tested at Kibbutz Hatzerim in the Negev, the design was commercialised by Netafim, founded in 1965.

### 392 · Precision agriculture (`precision_agriculture`)

Precision agriculture treats a field as a patchwork rather than a uniform unit. In the early 1990s, yield monitors on combines logged grain flow against satellite positions, and the maps showed yields varying within fields. Variable-rate machines, adopted from the late 1990s, adjusted seed and fertiliser by location. Uptake was uneven: early returns were inconsistent and data standards lagged.

### 419 · Crop genomics (`frontier_crops`)

Crop genomics reads and edits the DNA of food plants to guide breeding. The rice genome, published in 2005 by a project begun in 1997, mapped about 37,500 genes and became a reference for related cereals. Breeders pair such maps with editing tools like CRISPR, which alters chosen sites without leaving foreign DNA; rules for it vary by country.

### 424 · Climate engineering (`frontier_climate`)

Climate engineering means deliberate, large-scale intervention in the climate system. A 2009 Royal Society report split proposals into two families: removing carbon dioxide from the air, and reflecting some sunlight back to space. It judged removal preferable; reflection could cool quickly but would not lower carbon dioxide, and no method had been shown affordable with acceptable environmental effects.

### 496 · Scientific plant breeding (`scientific_plant_breeding`)

Scientific plant breeding applied Mendel's laws to crops. In 1905 Cambridge's Rowland Biffen showed that wheat's yellow-rust resistance was inherited in Mendelian fashion. In 1908 George Shull, and independently Edward East, described hybrid vigour in crosses of inbred maize. Hybrid seed, sold from 1926 and rebought yearly, covered 6 percent of Iowa's corn in 1935 and nearly all by 1942.
