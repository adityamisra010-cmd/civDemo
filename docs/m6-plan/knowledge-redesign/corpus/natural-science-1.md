### 190 · Astrolabe (`astrolabe`)

The planispheric astrolabe flattens the sky onto a plate by stereographic projection, so an observer who sights a star or the Sun can read off the hour and latitude. The projection goes back to Hipparchus and Ptolemy; the oldest dated instrument, signed by Nastulus, was made in the Islamic world in 927/928 CE, where astrolabes also timed prayers.

### 192 · Gunpowder (`gunpowder`)

Gunpowder mixes saltpetre (potassium nitrate), sulphur and charcoal; the saltpetre supplies oxygen, so the mixture burns fiercely without air. Chinese alchemists searching for elixirs came upon such mixtures by the ninth century. The Wujing Zongyao, compiled about 1040-1044, holds the earliest surviving military formulas, which were low in saltpetre and made incendiaries rather than explosives.

### 193 · Saltpetre refining (`saltpetre_refining`)

Saltpetre rarely occurs pure: it forms as crusts and in nitre-rich soils mixed with other salts. Refiners leached such earth with water, often adding lime and wood ash, then boiled the liquor and crystallised potassium nitrate, repeating to purify it. Where and when systematic refining began is uncertain; detailed European recipes survive in fifteenth-century gunners' manuals.

### 205 · Trigonometry (`trigonometry`)

Trigonometry relates angles to lengths. Aryabhata's Aryabhatiya of 499 CE tabulated the half-chord, the ancestor of the sine, at steps of 3 degrees 45 minutes. Astronomers in the Islamic world extended the method: in the late tenth century Abu al-Wafa used the tangent, compiled finer tables and proved the sine rule for spherical triangles, used in astronomical computation.

### 213 · Double-entry bookkeeping (`double_entry`)

Double-entry bookkeeping records every transaction twice, as a debit in one account and an equal credit in another, so the books can be checked by balancing them. Surviving early examples include a Florentine firm's ledger of 1299-1300 and Genoa's treasury accounts of 1340. Luca Pacioli's printed account of 1494 codified Venetian practice for a wide readership.

### 223 · Corned gunpowder (`corned_powder`)

Early gunpowder was a loose meal whose ingredients could separate in transport and burned unevenly. ### 231 · Heliocentric model (`heliocentrism`)

### 235 · Calculus (`calculus`)

### 236 · Barometer and air pump (`barometer_vacuum`)

### 274 · Quantitative chemistry (`chemistry_quantitative`)

### 282 · Dynamite (`dynamite`)

Patented in 1867, dynamite made blasting rock in mines, quarries and tunnels far safer.

### 283 · Electromagnetism (`electromagnetism`)
