### 1001 · Written law (`law_code`)

Written law sets rules and penalties down in a public text, so judges can apply a fixed wording rather than remembered custom alone. Among the earliest surviving collections are the laws of Ur-Namma of Ur, about 2100 BCE, and of Hammurabi of Babylon, about 1750 BCE, whose stele is in the Louvre. How closely courts followed such texts is debated.

### 1002 · Systematic law (`legal_code_roman`)

Systematic law organizes legal rules into a reasoned whole, with general principles, categories of persons, property and obligations, and trained jurists who interpret and teach it. Comparable traditions include Islamic jurisprudence and the Chinese statutory codes, such as the Tang code of the seventh century.

### 1005 · Patent system (`patent`)
