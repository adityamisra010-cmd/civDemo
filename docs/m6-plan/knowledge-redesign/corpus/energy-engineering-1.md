### 1 · Controlled-fire pyrotechnology (`fire_making`)

Controlled fire became a technology when people learned to hold hearths at chosen temperatures. At Pinnacle Point, South Africa, silcrete was deliberately heated by about 164,000 years ago, making the stone easier to flake into fine points. Managed fire also cooked food in earth ovens and burned landscapes to renew plants. When hominins first used fire remains debated.

### 27 · Oil lamp (`lamp`)

### 65 · Charcoal making (`charcoal`)

Charcoal is wood heated with little air, so water and volatile substances are driven off and nearly pure carbon remains. Burned in earth-covered piles, it gives hotter, cleaner fires than wood, and early metal smelting relied on it. Demand for it consumed woodlands; as charcoal grew costly in Britain, Abraham Darby smelted iron with coke at Coalbrookdale in 1709.

### 111 · Bellows (`bellows`)

### 237 · Atmospheric steam engine (`steam_atmospheric`)

Thomas Newcomen's engine of 1712 used the pressure of the air, not of steam, to do work. Steam filled a cylinder, a jet of cold water condensed it, and the partial vacuum let the atmosphere push a piston down, rocking a beam that worked mine pumps. Its first cylinders were brass; cast-iron cylinders came from Coalbrookdale in the 1720s.

### 248 · Separate condenser (`steam_separate_condenser`)

### 250 · Rotary steam engine (`steam_rotary`)

### 289 · Electric motor (`electric_motor`)

### 305 · Diesel engine (`diesel`)

Rudolf Diesel began from heat-engine theory. ### 456 · Water turbine (`water_turbine`)

A water turbine passes water under pressure through curved blades in a closed casing, extracting more energy than an open wheel, especially from high falls. Benoît Fourneyron's turbine of 1827 in France, James Francis's design at Lowell, Massachusetts (1849), and Lester Pelton's impulse wheel in California (1879) made high heads usable. Turbines later drove hydroelectric generators.
