### 386 · Genome sequencing (`genome_sequencing`)

Genome sequencing reads an organism's entire genetic material. Automated fluorescent-dye sequencers appeared from 1986, and computers assembled the overlapping reads. The first genome of a free-living organism, the bacterium Haemophilus influenzae, was published in 1995. The international Human Genome Project released a draft in 2001 and declared completion in 2003; a finished rice genome followed in 2005.

### 388 · Gene editing (`crispr`)

Gene editing changes a chosen DNA sequence inside living cells. Earlier tools needed a custom protein for each target. In 2012 Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier's teams showed that Cas9, a bacterial antiviral enzyme, could be guided by designed RNA to cut a chosen site, earning the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Rules for edited crops differ between countries.

### 394 · Catalytic converter (`catalytic_converter`)

A catalytic converter passes engine exhaust over platinum-group metals on a honeycomb, speeding reactions that clean it. Fitted widely to new United States cars from the 1975 model year to meet Clean Air Act limits, early converters oxidised carbon monoxide and unburnt hydrocarbons. Lead poisons the catalyst, so unleaded petrol spread alongside. Later rhodium-bearing designs also reduce nitrogen oxides.

### 396 · Carbon capture (`carbon_capture`)

Carbon capture separates carbon dioxide from gas streams for use or storage. Usually an amine solution absorbs the carbon dioxide, releasing it when heated. Patented around 1930 to purify natural gas, it supplied Texas oil fields from the 1970s. Storage aimed at limiting climate change began at Norway's Sleipner field in 1996; capture remains small beside global emissions.

### 488 · DNA sequencing (`dna_sequencing`)

DNA sequencing reads the order of the four bases along a DNA strand. Two methods appeared in 1977: Frederick Sanger's chain termination and Allan Maxam and Walter Gilbert's chemical cleavage. Both sorted labelled fragments by length on gels and read the order from ladders of bands. That year Sanger's group also published the first complete DNA genome, virus phiX174.
