### 386 · Genome sequencing (`genome_sequencing`)

Genome sequencing reads an organism's entire genetic material. In 1995 a whole-genome shotgun method, which needed no prior physical map, gave the first complete genome of a free-living organism, the bacterium Haemophilus influenzae. The Human Genome Project declared completion in April 2003, though about 8 percent, mostly repetitive DNA, stayed unread until 2022. A finished rice genome followed in 2005.

### 388 · Gene editing (`crispr`)

Gene editing changes a chosen DNA sequence inside living cells. In 2012 Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier's teams showed that Cas9, an enzyme from a bacterial defence system against viruses, could be guided by designed RNA to cut DNA at a chosen site, earning the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Rules for edited crops differ between countries.

### 394 · Catalytic converter (`catalytic_converter`)

A catalytic converter passes engine exhaust over a platinum-bearing catalyst that speeds reactions cleaning it. Under the 1970 Clean Air Act, new United States cars carried converters from the 1975 model year; these early units oxidised carbon monoxide and unburnt hydrocarbons. Lead coats the catalyst, so unleaded petrol spread alongside. Three-way converters, which also reduce nitrogen oxides, followed from 1977.

### 396 · Carbon capture (`carbon_capture`)

Carbon capture separates carbon dioxide from gas streams for use or storage. In amine scrubbing, used since about 1930 to remove carbon dioxide from natural gas and hydrogen, an amine solution binds the gas chemically. At Norway's Sleipner field, amines strip carbon dioxide from produced gas for storage, a process its operator found costly to develop.

### 488 · DNA sequencing (`dna_sequencing`)

DNA sequencing reads the order of the four bases along a DNA strand. Two methods were reported in 1977: Allan Maxam and Walter Gilbert's chemical cleavage, and Frederick Sanger's chain termination, which used modified nucleotides that halt DNA copying. That year Sanger's group also published the first complete DNA genome, of the virus phiX174, over 5,000 bases long.
