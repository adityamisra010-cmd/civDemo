### 24 · Dog domestication (`dog_domestication`)

### 25 · Pigment processing (`ochre_processing`)

### 78 · Lunar calendar (`calendar_lunar`)

### 91 · Sexagesimal place-value (`numeral_sexagesimal`)

In base-60 place value, one wedge sign can mean 1, 60 or 3,600 by its position, so one set of rules serves multiplication, division and fractions. Mesopotamian scribes adopted it for calculation probably in the Ur III period, about 2100-2000 BCE, converting their measures into it. Lacking a zero sign or point, readers judged magnitude from context.

### 92 · Babylonian arithmetic (`arithmetic_babylonian`)

Old Babylonian scribal schools, around 1900-1600 BCE, taught calculation through standard tables learned by copying: multiplication tables, and reciprocal tables that replaced division, since dividing by a number meant multiplying by its reciprocal. A school tablet from Nippur preserves a teacher's and a pupil's copy of the 45-times table, both containing errors. Such training served land measurement, rations and accounts.

### 106 · Civil solar calendar (`calendar_civil`)

Ancient Egypt's civil calendar had twelve 30-day months plus five extra days. Its origin is disputed; proposals cluster near 2800 BCE.

### 107 · Water clock (`water_clock`)

An earlier official, Amenemhet, claimed its invention in his tomb.

### 134 · Intercalated calendar (`calendar_lunisolar`)

### 140 · Axiomatic geometry (`geometry_axiomatic`)

Greek thinkers sought proofs from at least the sixth century BCE, and Euclid's Elements, compiled at Alexandria around 300 BCE, organised earlier work into thirteen books deducing hundreds of propositions from definitions, postulates and common notions. Doubts about its fifth, parallel, postulate eventually produced non-Euclidean geometries.

### 141 · Statics and simple machines (`mechanics_archimedean`)

### 164 · Geometric astronomy (`astronomy_geometric`)

Geometric astronomy models the motions of the Sun, Moon and planets with circles so that their positions can be calculated. Hipparchus, in the second century BCE, combined Greek geometry with older Greek and Babylonian observations to estimate precession and compile a catalogue of about 850 stars. Ptolemy's Almagest, written at Alexandria in the second century CE, systematised such models.

### 203 · Hindu-Arabic numerals (`hindu_arabic`)

### 204 · Algebra (`algebra`)

Algebra finds unknown quantities by general rules. In Baghdad around 820, al-Khwarizmi gave the first systematic treatment of linear and quadratic equations, justifying steps geometrically, and his title gave the subject its name. ### 432 · Counting-rod arithmetic (`counting_rods`)

In early China, numbers were laid out with rods on a counting board, one column per decimal place, an empty column standing for zero. Bamboo slips carbon-dated to about 305 BCE hold the oldest known decimal multiplication table. The Nine Chapters, compiled by Han times, used red and black rods for positive and negative quantities in solving linear equations.
