catchment is out of this packet's scope**, so it is reported, not adjusted.

**This is the director's own "a fixed spacing constant cannot be right across 6,000 years",

**Pre-committed reading M5 fires on its second branch:** the derived 95.2 km implies a median of

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE RULING — STATED, NOT DECIDED

The direction (spacing DOWN) **survives**, because the 4 % inconsistency is real and the late-game
Charter numbers are unreachable at 480. **But its justification changes**, exactly as M1's
pre-commitment anticipated: 480 is not a wrong constant, it is a constant **correct for the ancient
endpoint only**. Lowering it to ~95 km yields a late-game-density world **from turn zero**, which
contradicts the Charter's ancient figure and would found ~457 settlements in 4000 BCE.

**Three shapes the director may choose between, none chosen here:**
1. **Lower to a late-game value (~95–144 km)** and accept a dense ancient world;
2. **Lower modestly now** (a first step consistent with the playability coupling below) and take
   the rest when spacing derives from state;
3. **Make spacing DERIVE from computed state** (population pressure, land quality) per law 4 — the
   deferred item, which this measurement now shows is not merely nicer but **the only shape that
   satisfies both Charter endpoints.**

## RECORDED, NOT DESIGNED

- **A FIXED SPACING CONSTANT CANNOT BE RIGHT ACROSS 6,000 YEARS** — sparse settlement is plausible
  at 4000 BCE and absurd by 1000 CE. Spacing should eventually DERIVE from computed state per law
  4. **Open design item, no owner.** M3+M5 above is its supporting measurement.
- **PLAYABILITY COUPLING (the director's reasoning for two steps rather than one).** The Charter's
  300–800 is not reachable as a PLAY EXPERIENCE until **M5's delegation** exists: sector mixes are
  set per settlement by hand — workable at 12, tedious at 46, impossible at 300+. **And T4.16's
  clone work should land before the world grows**: the price solver is O(S²·G²) and buckets already
  collide with their ratified cap at Charter scale. Both argue the first step should be modest.
