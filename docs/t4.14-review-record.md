## §2. (a) ARTISAN EMERGENCE — WHY BIMODAL: **DIAGNOSED**

> *F4: "a mechanism that emits either one person or fifty-odd, never five, is the thing T4.14 has to
> explain."*

### 2.1 THE ANSWER IN ONE LINE

**There is no single mechanism, and that is the whole finding. "The first artisans set up their
workshops" fires on the first turn a settlement's artisan count is non-zero, and TWO unrelated
mechanisms can make it non-zero: the emergence latch promoting peasants in bulk, or a single artisan
arriving as a MIGRANT while the settlement's own latch is still inactive.** The chronicle line
cannot distinguish them. The low mode is not emergence at all.

### 2.2 THE DISCRIMINATOR — 94 EVENTS, CLASSIFIED BY THE LATCH BIT

`ClassStateRow.Active` at the first-artisan turn separates the two populations completely:

| mode | n | counts observed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **latch INACTIVE** (`active == 0`) — artisans arrived by migration | **24** | `1 ×23`, `3 ×1` |
| **latch ACTIVE** (`active == 1`) — `ClassMobility` promoted them | **70** | `3, 10, 17, 20, 20, 24, 26 ×4, 28, 32, 33 ×2, 34, 35, 36 ×2, 38, 42 ×3, 44, 48 ×2, 49, 51 ×6, 52, 54, 55 ×5, 56 ×2, 57 ×17, 58 ×9, 59 ×2` — **median 55** |

**Every one of the 24 low-mode events has (i) a non-zero migrant inflow that turn and (ii) at least
one other settlement already holding artisans on the previous turn. Zero violations of either
condition, in 24 of 24.** Migration preserves class (`MigrationSystem.cs:320,354` match on
`b.Class`) and slots after `ClassMobility` and before `Demographics`
(`Sim.Core/Systems/Migration/MigrationSystem.cs:88`), so an artisan can enter a settlement that has
never met its own emerge predicate.

### 2.3 WHY THE HIGH MODE IS A NARROW BAND AND NOT A SPREAD — TWO CONSTANTS CONSPIRE

`ClassMobilitySystem.cs:206-227`:

```
targetShare = min(TargetShareCap, max(0, TargetShareSlope × (surplus − 1)))
gapPeople   = targetShare × totalAdults − managedAdults
moveTotal   = min(PromoteRatePerYear × gapPeople × dtYears, fromAdults)
```

With `promoteRatePerYear 0.08`, `targetShareSlope 0.2`, `targetShareCap 0.2`
(`Sim.Data/content/sim.json:170-175`) and `dtYears = 10`:

**`moveTotal = min(48, 48 × (surplus − 1)) × (totalAdults / 300)`**

Two independent constants pin both factors at the emergence instant:

1. **The emerge predicate's own scale term fixes the adult count.**
   `"emerge": "food_surplus_ratio > 1.3 && population > 520"` (`sim.json:161`). Every settlement
   must grow into that same threshold, so every settlement crosses it at nearly the same size:
   **`totalAdults` measured 293–311 in 66 of the 70 latch events.**
2. **`targetShareCap = 0.2` binds whenever `surplus ≥ 2.0`.** Measured surplus at emergence runs
   **1.13 – 3.28**, so the cap binds in most events and the flow saturates at
   `0.08 × 0.2 × ~297 × 10 = ~47.5`.

Multiply by the **same-turn demographic amplification** of §2.5 (measured 1.11–1.25, n = 70) and the
predicted count is **~57**. Observed: **57 appears 17 times; 51–59 covers 40 of the 70 events.**

### 2.4 WHY THE LOW MODE IS *EXACTLY 1* AND NOT 2 OR 5

A migrant party is a small fraction of a settlement's population, and its artisan content is that
party times the source settlement's artisan share (10–17 % at the time these events occur),
distributed across 16 cohorts. **Measured inflows at the 24 low-mode events: 1–32 people, of whom
1 was an artisan in 23 cases and 3 in one.** One is the modal artisan content of an early migrant
party, and the chronicle fires the instant it is non-zero.

### 2.5 THE 1.2× FACTOR — MEASURED, EXPLAINED, AND **NOT** A DEFECT

Observed counts run **1.11–1.25 ×** the promotion flow, systematically. The cause is documented,
deliberate behaviour: `DemographicsSystem.cs:145-149` seeds its micro-loop from **PRESENT** integer
counts (`pop[i] = buckets[i].Count.Value`, `buckets = ctx.Owned.Buckets`), and its header states the
design — *"the Prev-sized dodge class is structurally … Prev-read; only the integration acts on
present stocks"*. `Demographics` slots after `ClassMobility`, so peasants promoted at turn T receive
that turn's full 10 years of births and aging as artisans.

Corroborated: `dir42` s8 t40 holds **59** artisans of whom only **36** are adults — 49 promoted
adults plus same-turn births, minus those aged past cohort 11. **Recorded as understood, not filed
as a finding.**

### 2.6 THE EMPTY GAP — **NOT STRUCTURALLY GUARANTEED. THIS IS THE ONE PART THAT WEAKENS F4's FRAMING.**

F4's data had *"NOTHING between 3 and 26"* in 35 events and called the split *"sharper than the
director's chronicle showed"*. Two of the 70 latch events measured here land **inside** that gap:
`base3` s6 at **3** and `base3` s11 at **10**.

The bridge is a **two-turn lag inside the latch/flow pair**, and it is visible in the code. The
latch is evaluated on `PrevVariable(prev, …)` — the surplus published LAST turn
(`ClassMobilitySystem.cs:169-173`) — while `MoveAdults` is sized by the surplus computed THIS turn
(`:136,182`). Food production is weather-noisy (measured settlement surplus swings 0.90 → 3.28
turn to turn), so the latch can fire on a surplus of 1.85 and the flow then be sized by 1.13,
giving `targetShare = 0.026` and a flow of 6 people rather than 48.

**Honest statement: the low mode and the high mode have separate, well-identified mechanisms
(§2.2/§2.3), and the region between them is thinly but genuinely populated — 2 of 70 latch events.
The gap is a sampling property of a bimodal generator with a thin bridge, not a hard structural
exclusion.** F4's *"never five"* is very nearly true and its cause is now known; it is not exactly
true.
