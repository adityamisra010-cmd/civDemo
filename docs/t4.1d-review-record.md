## WHAT THIS INVALIDATES, AND WHAT SURVIVES — STATED PRECISELY

**INVALID — withdrawn:**
- **T4.1's "both M3 artisan inheritances are ORDER-CONDITIONAL".** Its discriminator compared a
  CORRECT with-orders reading (the replay report, which matches on the registry NAME "Artisans")
  against an INCORRECT no-orders reading (class id 1 = peasants). **The comparison was between two
  different classes, not between two order regimes.** The conclusion does not follow and is
  withdrawn.

**UNAFFECTED — these did not use the class id:**
- **T4.1's chain finding** (gate open 12/12 by turn 300, bronze correctly reading stock 0 as an
  intra-turn intermediate, tools numerically inert). It read `Variables.ArtisanShare` and the
  good stocks, never the bucket class id. **Stands.**
- **T4.1's Q1 bind ratio** — arable, farmLabor and tool stocks. **Stands.**
- **T4.1b's discriminator table** — both ruled settlements at 1, neither anomalous one ruled. Read
  from the replay report by NAME. **Stands.**
- **The M4 spec's collapse measurement** (6 of 12 at zero on the held-exit log) — also
  name-matched. **Stands as a measurement**, and T4.1b already narrowed it to that specific log.

## THE LESSON, AND IT IS THE THIRD IN THIS FAMILY

**An id that is off by one produces a plausible series, not an error.** Peasant counts look like
artisan counts: same shape, same order of magnitude, monotone growth. Nothing failed; the number
was simply about a different thing. **The replay reporter was immune because it matches on the
registry NAME**, which is why the two paths disagreed at all — and it is why T3.12a's design rule
("read the table the owning system wrote, never re-derive") is worth more than it looked.

**Rule to add to the register:** when a probe indexes a registry by RAW ID, print the registry's
name for that id in the same line. The mix-up is invisible in the number and obvious in the label.

Same family as the sweep-capture line and the operation-looks-successful line: **the failure is in
what the instrument could distinguish, not in the measurement.**

# T4.14 LEAD — THE DIRECTOR'S BINDING-CONDITION HYPOTHESIS

**Pre-committed BEFORE measurement (§7.13).** Filed here because the probe runs in this packet.

**The predicate, verified at `sim.json:158`:**
```
emerge: food_surplus_ratio > 1.3 && population > 520     recede: food_surplus_ratio < 1.1
```
The registry's own doc: 520 is *"the registry's only SCALE-DEPENDENT term, so founding-size
variation reaches the crossing time through it"*, and *"520 sits above the ~350–500 jittered
founding sizes, so every settlement must GROW into its artisans."*

**HYPOTHESIS (director's, to test not assume): WHICH CONDITION BINDS LAST determines the cohort
size.** A settlement long past 520 that finally clears the food ratio converts a large eligible
pool in one turn; one creeping across 520 with the ratio long satisfied converts only whoever just
qualified. That would give a bimodal split with nothing in between.

**The objection the director already raised, and the place to start:** Mothian was founded at 583,
**above** 520, and still landed at 1 — but the chronicle shows it losing ~108 souls to migration by
year 50, so it may have fallen BELOW 520 and re-crossed from underneath. If so the rule is not
founding size but **which side of 520 a settlement last crossed from**, with migration putting a
large settlement on the wrong side.

**Observable:** per settlement, in the turn BEFORE its artisans first appear — which of the two
conditions was unsatisfied — plus the population trajectory across the 520 line.
**COMPOSITE? YES**, deliberately: "which binds last" and "how big the converted cohort is" are two
readings, and a rule that predicts the first without predicting the second has not explained the
split. Both reported.

**PRE-COMMITTED READINGS:**
- **the HIGH-mode settlements are population-bound last (food ratio already satisfied), and the
  LOW-mode ones are food-bound last** → hypothesis supported, and the mechanism is named.
- **both modes bind on the same condition** → hypothesis REFUTED; the split is elsewhere.
- **the two high-mode settlements never dipped below 520 while the low-mode ones did** → the
  refinement holds: it is the crossing DIRECTION, not founding size.
- **Mothian never fell below 520** → the director's own rescue of his objection fails, and the
  hypothesis is in trouble regardless of what the other settlements show.
