## 2. THE ARCHITECTURE AS BUILT

```
span     = min(1, riverCells / stride)
nodeCost = (1 − span) · blockMeanCost + span · riverCostFactor
```

A coefficient **inside** `TraversalLattice.Build`'s per-block cost aggregation (law 2) — not a
free-floating modifier. Rivers do **not** change passability (river cells are LAND; that was proven
structurally at T4.9). `riverCells == 0` takes the untouched `costSum / cells` in the same expression
and the same order as before T4.7, so **a river-free world is bit-identical** to the pre-T4.7 lattice
— which is why the toy golden is a control and must not move.

Honoured, all verified in the diff: no second river graph · no `NetworkEdge` promotion · no stride
change · **no persisted river routing state, no new table, no schema bump** (`TerrainSet` remains the
authoritative geometry, ADR-008) · vessels/ports/naval out of scope.

`transport.riverCostFactor = 0.20`, TUNE, with its derivation stated in `sim.json`: reference class
**declared before any number was computed** — pre-modern freight ratios (Diocletian's Price Edict,
corroborated by medieval English cartage-vs-river) put road : river per ton-km at ~5–10 : 1, giving
the ratified band r ∈ [0.10, 0.20]. **0.20 is the CONSERVATIVE edge** — the weakest river advantage
the class supports — chosen because it is also the edge carrying a *measured* outcome (T3.6b's
pixel counterfactual: 4/27 ore-pair crossings at r = 0.20 against 0/27 river-blind). Taking the
strong edge would have manufactured a larger effect from the same evidence. Range-validated in
`Build` to (0,1].

### 8 and 9 — diagnosed exactly, and the first reading was wrong

Both report a negative actual against a positive expected: 24 → −0.142135623730951 and
21.8 → −2.3421356237309503. **The offset is identical and exact in both: 10 + 10√2 = 24.14213562373095**
— an orthogonal-plus-diagonal lattice path cost, i.e. one segment's build cost.

The obvious reading is "negative labor, therefore corruption". **That reading is wrong and is recorded
here so it is not repeated**: `PathBuildSystem.cs:207` guards `if (progress.Banked < segmentCost)
return;` before the debit at `:216`, so banked labor **cannot** go negative from a spend. The negative
number is the tests' own comparison arithmetic. The real mechanism is the intended one: **a cheaper
river route makes a segment affordable a turn earlier than these exact-value tests encode.** That is
T4.7 working. They are candidate VALUE re-pins with a stated cause — not defects — but they must be
re-derived, not edited to match.

## 4. THE BLOCKER — a pathology guard, not a drifted number

`MigrationConcentrationTests.PersistentExportFromMarginalLand_Survives_AndConverges`:

> *a settlement shed 98.4 % of its population in one turn — the recorded pathology was
> 40 %-in-one-season and it must not return*

This test exists to prevent a **recorded historical pathology**, and it is now firing at more than
twice that pathology's severity. A one-turn 98.4 % population shed is not a plausible consequence of
cheaper river travel; it is the signature of an attractiveness/gap discontinuity of the kind T2.8's
cap and EMA were installed to make structurally impossible.

**Therefore no golden is re-pinned in this packet.** Re-pinning goldens while a pathology guard is
firing would bake the pathology into the baseline — precisely the failure mode the golden discipline
exists to prevent. Item 11 (seed 42 leaving the pre-Malthusian regime) is plausibly downstream of the
same discontinuity and must be re-checked *after* item 12 is resolved, not before — and note CR-003's
own text says that if a crash cycle genuinely returns, that quarantine is resolved rather than broken,
so item 11 may turn out to be a finding rather than a fault.
