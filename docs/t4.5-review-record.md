# T4.5 — NON-STATE PEOPLES (D-037 B3) — REVIEW RECORD

Branch `t4.5-non-state-peoples`, cut from `origin/main` `ba96b1c`. NOT MERGED, NOT SELF-CERTIFIED.

## 1. WHAT WAS BUILT — THREE MECHANISMS, NO NEW STATE

**1. The carrier is the absence of a `ControlRow`**, exactly as authorized. `ControlRow`'s own
contract already admits "exactly one state control row, OR none". No `StatelessSettlement` table,
no new population carrier, no new polity type, **no schema bump** (verified in §5).

**2. Subsistence is the existing `Herding` sector** through the existing `FromDeposits()` pathway.
No parallel herding model was written.

**3. The drought coupling — the actual implementation gap — is closed.** `Farm()` already consumed
`HarvestWeatherRow.Multiplier`; `FromDeposits()` did not. Weather is now passed by the CALLER:
the Herding call passes `HarvestWeatherFor(prev, settlement)`, the EXTRACTION call passes `1.0`.

The semantic location was traced rather than bolted on: weather multiplies `ratePerYear` in the
same position and order as `Farm` does it — once, before dt integration and before the D-004
remainder is added. Extraction's `× 1.0` is exact in IEEE-754, so its output is bit-identical.
Farm's inline weather lookup became the shared `HarvestWeatherFor` helper, extracted verbatim
(same scan order, same first-match break, same absent-row-is-1.0 default) so farming and herding
cannot drift onto two readings of one signal.

**4. Appropriation** (`AppropriationSystem`, `SystemId` 16, pipeline slot between `production` and
`consumption`) — a hungry non-state people takes grain. It owns **no tables** and publishes none.

- **No second definition of hunger.** The trigger is `ConsumptionSystem`'s existing
  `ConsumptionDeficitRow`, read from PREV like every other cross-system signal.
- **No new constant.** The amount is the raider's own measured shortfall,
  `DeficitRatio × DemandUnits`, both already on the row. No raid size, no greed factor, no
  threshold, no timer, no cooldown, no RNG.
- **No ledger reason was added.** `Ledger.Transfer` "conserves by construction (no flow entry)"
  and takes no `ReasonId`. An `Appropriation` reason was drafted and then REVERTED as dead code —
  the tree's own precedent is explicit: "band aging moves people BETWEEN stocks via
  Ledger.Transfer, which conserves by construction and records no source/sink row."
- **`ClampToAvailable`**, so a victim can be emptied but never overdrawn.
- **Victim** = the other settlement holding the most grain, tie-broken to the LOWEST id by a
  strictly-greater ascending scan — the (stock desc, id asc) composite key, on longs, no
  floating-point comparison.

## 2. ONE IMPLEMENTATION DECISION THAT NARROWED THE AUTHORIZED TRIGGER — STATED PLAINLY

The authorized design says the discriminator is the absence of a `ControlRow`. **Implemented
literally, that made every settlement in the world a raider**, because nothing in `Sim.Core`
writes `Controls` — T4.3 shipped claim/control/recognition as schema only, and there is no
`Polities` table at all. So "stateless" is currently universal.

**This was not a theoretical worry; it was measured.** With statelessness as the sole condition,
the full suite showed `CollapseStabilityTests.CollapsingWorld_DeadStaysDead_NoResurrectionCycle`
failing with *"no settlement died food-less in the collapsing world — the resurrection detector is
vacuous again"*. Hungry villages were taking their neighbours' grain and surviving, which emptied
the precondition of an ADR-012 resurrection guard. That is world-wide famine relief, not steppe
raiding.

**The fix, and why it is inside the authorized design rather than a change to it:** the raider must
also be **HERDING-DOMINANT** — its largest sector is Herding. This is D-037 B3's actual subject
("Pastoralists, hunter-gatherers …") and the authorized design's own words ("A herding-dominant
stateless settlement therefore already has an existing production pathway"). It introduces no new
mechanism (`Sectors.Raw` exists), no new constant (largest-sector is a comparison, not a
threshold), no schema, and no scope expansion. Dominance is STRICT: an even plough/herd split is a
mixed village, not a herding people, and is pinned as such.

With the condition in place the collapse guard passes again.

**RECORDED FOR THE DIRECTOR TO OVERRULE IF THEY DISAGREE:** this narrows an explicitly authorized
trigger. It is the one design-adjacent judgement in the packet.

## 3. THE CONSEQUENCE THAT MATTERS — THE RAID IS DORMANT IN LIVE WORLDS

`Sectors.Default` is `Farming 0.55, Herding 0.15, Extraction 0.10, Crafting 0.12, Construction
0.08` — farming-dominant. **Measured over all 300 turns of the canonical founded world (seed 42):
ZERO herding-dominant settlement-turns**, so appropriation never fires there.

The mechanism ships complete and fully tested, but **it cannot fire in a live world until worldgen
places pastoralists**. D-037 B3 says non-state peoples are "M4 worldgen, present from turn zero —
NOT spawned". Placing them is worldgen work this packet's authorized design did not cover (it
forbids schema changes and never mentions worldgen), so it was NOT done here and is queued in
`docs/queue.md`. **Stated rather than left to look like a live feature.**

Two test-design corrections worth recording, because both were real model findings:

- **A pure-pastoralist settlement is ALWAYS in deficit, in every weather.** The consumption basket
  is grain-dominant and a non-staple surplus substitutes INTO the staple but never the reverse, so
  a livestock-only settlement is short of grain regardless of the year and its deficit carries no
  drought signal at all. The end-to-end test therefore uses a MARGINAL MIXED settlement — which is
  also D-037 B3's "marginal terrain".
- **The mechanism is self-correcting and therefore phase-dependent**: hunger in turn *t* drives a
  raid in *t+1*, which feeds them, so *t+2* needs no raid. Assertions total over a window rather
  than sampling one turn, which would read a trough half the time.

# 10. RE-ISSUED PACKET — POINT-BY-POINT RECONCILIATION

The T4.5 packet was re-issued with the implementation already committed at `2d64776`. Every clause
was re-checked against the tree rather than against the earlier handback. Three clauses needed
work; one could not be satisfied as written and is answered from source.

## 10.1 "Add a dedicated ReasonId for the raid" — STRUCTURALLY IMPOSSIBLE, ANSWERED FROM SOURCE

The instruction is conditional ("if no existing reason is appropriate") and sits beside two others:
"Use the existing `Ledger.Transfer` mechanism" and "The transfer must be exactly conserved". Those
three cannot all hold, and the tree decides it:

**`Ledger` has exactly two mutation methods** (`Sim.Core/Kernel/Ledger.cs`):

```csharp
public long Transfer(ref Conserved from, ref Conserved to, long amount, OverdrawPolicy policy)
public long Flow(ref Conserved stock, ConservedQuantityId q, ReasonId reason, long amount, FlowDirection dir, OverdrawPolicy policy)
```

`Transfer` takes **no `ReasonId` at all** — its contract is *"Conserves by construction (no flow
entry)"*. All SEVEN `Transfer` call sites in `Sim.Core` (Migration, ClassMobility, Trade ×2,
Demographics, Appropriation) pass no reason, because the signature has none.

`ReasonIds`' own comment states the convention explicitly: *"Aging is deliberately absent: band
aging moves people BETWEEN stocks via `Ledger.Transfer`, which conserves by construction and records
no source/sink row."*

**Decision: no `ReasonId` added.** Adding one would be unreachable dead code. The alternative —
sinking grain from the victim and sourcing it to the raider under a new reason — would satisfy the
letter of "a dedicated reason" while **breaking the letter of "exactly conserved"**: it would record
grain as destroyed and new grain as created, corrupting every Σ-sourced audit that reads a source as
"grain entered the world". An `Appropriation` reason was drafted, recognised as dead, and reverted
before commit.

**On observability:** the tree's own pattern for an observable transfer is Transfer PLUS an owned
table (`TradeArbitrageSystem` writes `TradeFlowRow`). That route is closed here by this packet's own
fence ("Do not add: … Raid table", "No schema bump is expected"), and nothing in T4.5's acceptance
requires historical recording — the packet's clause is itself conditional ("If a raid needs
historical recording"). `ChronicleCollector` is a post-hoc observer of world states, not a sink
systems write into, so it needs no change either. **Recorded as a deliberate, sourced decision, not
an omission.**

## 10.2 FOUR TESTS ADDED FOR CLAUSES NOT PREVIOUSLY COVERED

- `NoGrainAnywhere_MeansNoRaid_AndNoPhantomTransfer` — the packet's "no raid occurs when the source
  lacks available grain". Every other settlement holds ZERO; world grain stays exactly 0.
- `ARaidedSettlement_CannotBecomeARaider_InTheSameTurn` — adversarial item 9 (chain reactions).
  Eligibility is read from PREV, so being emptied by an earlier raider in the same pass cannot make
  a settlement a raider in that pass.
- `EachRaiderTakesAtMostOnce_PerTurn_TheWholePassIsBounded` — adversarial item 7 (no intra-turn
  economic sub-simulation). Three hungry peoples against one granary: MEASURED 200 / 200 / 200 / 300
  with world grain unchanged at 900. N settlements produce at most N transfers; there is no
  iteration to a fixpoint.
- `RaiderAndVictimAreNeverTheSameRow_NoSelfTransfer` — a self-transfer would alias two `ref`s into
  one row and could mint grain. A lone settlement raids nobody.

## 10.3 ORDER DEPENDENCE — REAL, DETERMINISTIC, AND THE PROJECT'S OWN CONVENTION

The pass sweeps settlements in ascending id and each raider takes from the **currently** richest
other holder, reading the live next-turn stock table that earlier raiders have already mutated. So
outcomes DO depend on settlement id order.

This is deterministic (an array scan over `prev.Settlements`, no `Dictionary`/`HashSet`, no RNG) and
it is the same shape `TradeArbitrageSystem` uses. The victim argmax uses a strictly-greater ascending
scan = the (stock DESC, id ASC) composite key the constitution requires, on `long` stocks, with no
floating-point comparison anywhere. Pinned by
`Appropriation_IsDeterministic_AndBreaksTiesByLowestSettlementId`, which asserts bit-exact
`WorldHash` equality across two independent runs.

## 10.4 PIPELINE PLACEMENT RE-JUSTIFIED

`production → appropriation → consumption`. Appropriation reads PREV (`prev.ConsumptionDeficits`,
`prev.Controls`, `prev.SectorAllocations`, `prev.Settlements`) and writes only `next.GoodStocks`,
which is exactly the §3.2 one-turn-lag convention every other system follows. The placement is what
makes the relief *usable in the turn it arrives* rather than two turns later; it does not add a
second economic pass, and §10.2's bounded-pass test proves it.

# 11. SECOND ADVERSARIAL PASS — ONE REAL DEFECT FOUND AND FIXED

## 11.1 HOW THE PASS ACTUALLY WENT — STATED BEFORE THE RESULTS

A six-way adversarial fan-out was launched (conservation · ordering · eligibility · weather ·
pipeline · tests) with an independent refutation stage per finding. **It did NOT complete: 11 of 13
agents died on a session limit.** Only the CONSERVATION and ORDERING attacks finished, and every
refuter died, so the workflow's summary of "0 findings survived" is an ARTEFACT OF THE FAILURES, not
a clean bill of health. The two completed attacks returned four findings, which were adjudicated
here by direct source inspection; the four uncompleted attack areas were then covered manually
(§11.6). **Recorded this way so nobody reads the empty machine summary as a pass.**

## 11.2 FINDING 1 — REAL, BLOCKING, FIXED: two hungry peoples robbed each other in a circle

**The defect.** Victim selection read the LIVE (next-turn) stock table, which earlier raiders in the
same pass had already mutated. With two herding-dominant hungry settlements:

```
r=0: S0 takes 500 from S1        -> S0=500, S1=0
r=1: S1, now seeing S0 rich, takes 500 back -> S0=0,  S1=500
```

The pass ends with the world **bit-identical to its input**: two transfers executed, NEITHER hungry
settlement relieved, and the outcome decided by settlement row order rather than by any modelled
quantity. Every previously shipped raid test had exactly one herding-dominant settlement, so **no
test could see it**.

**The fix** (`RichestOtherGrainRow`): wealth is now read from **PREV** — what a settlement held when
the turn began — while the row it is moved FROM stays the live one, so clamping and conservation are
unaffected. A settlement that began the turn with nothing is never chosen, however much it has just
been handed. This also aligns the selection with the project's PREV-read convention that every other
system follows.

**Pinned by** `TwoHungryPeoples_DoNotRobEachOtherInACircle_TheRaidIsNotANoOp`, which asserts the
grain moves once and stays moved AND that the world hash actually changed — the pre-fix bug's
signature was a world identical to its input after two transfers.

## 11.3 FINDING 2 — REAL, DOCUMENTATION ONLY, FIXED: the coupling reaches FISH, not just herds

`ProductionSystem.InSector` defines the food sector as `livestock || fish`, and `WorldFounding` gives
every site a fish deposit, so T4.5's weather coupling moves the CATCH as well as the herds.

**The mechanism is correct as authorized** — the packet says to use the existing `foodSector`
discriminator, and `Sectors.Herding` is documented as "herding/fishing — the food-from-deposits
sector". Excluding fish would have required new discrimination inside `FromDeposits`, which the
packet forbids. **What was wrong was the paperwork:** both golden CAUSE records named only livestock.
Both were corrected to name the food-from-deposits sector explicitly.

**Recorded as a modelling question for later, not silently kept:** harvest weather is derived as a
statement about arable-land quality, and whether a drought should scale a fish catch by the same
multiplier is a genuine question. It is NOT a T4.5 defect and no constant or model was invented to
dodge it.

## 11.4 FINDING 3 — REAL, ALREADY KNOWN, DOCUMENTATION CORRECTED: the raid is unreachable in shipped worlds

The agent independently reached §3's dormancy conclusion and added detail worth keeping: it is not
merely that the default mix is farming-dominant — **`WorldFounding` writes no `SectorAllocationRow`
at all**, and the only writer in `Sim.Core` is `PathBuildSystem` acting on ORDERS. So in a headless
founded world `prev.SectorAllocations` is empty, `IsHerdingDominant` takes its no-row branch, and the
system moves nothing on every turn of a 6,000-year campaign.

It also caught **two stale claims in the system's own header**, both now corrected: that the
behaviour is "available to all" settlements (made false by the herding gate), and that "the
never-ordered default allocation is ALL FARMING" (false since T3.5b — `Sectors.Default` is
0.55/0.15/0.10/0.12/0.08). The header now states the dormancy plainly and points at `docs/queue.md`.

## 11.5 FINDING 4 — CONSIDERED AND REJECTED: "the raid amount is a rate spent under the wrong dt"

The claim: `DemandUnits` is a per-turn quantity computed under the PREV turn's dt, so at an era-band
boundary (dt 10 → 5) the raid over-takes by 2×, violating law 3.

**Rejected, on dimensional grounds.** `DeficitRatio × DemandUnits` is not a rate. It is the number of
whole food units the settlement FAILED TO OBTAIN during the turn that has already happened — a
STOCK, denominated in units, not units-per-year. Appropriating exactly that many units restores
exactly what was missed, and that quantity does not change because the next turn happens to be
shorter. Law 3 governs rates integrated with `dtYears`; a measured past shortfall is not one, which
is why the system correctly references neither `ctx.DtYears` nor `ctx.DtDays` (grep: zero hits).

Had the amount been "the shortfall per year, sustained across this turn", the finding would be
right; it is not that quantity.
