# T3.5b — subsistence default, variety reference point, ghost grievance, config guards

**Directed packet.** Scope FIXED by the four director rulings of 2026-07-27 (T3.5 acceptance).
Runs **after T3.5, before T3.6**. Not open to widening.

## Where this is homed, and why — the ruling asked me to state it

The ruling offered T3.8 (settlement size and housing) or T3.6 (trade and arbitrage), and invited a
reasoned alternative. **My judgement: neither — a directed packet immediately BEFORE T3.6**, on the
precedent of T3.2b and T3.4b, both of which were inserted mid-milestone for the same reason.

The argument is about what the remaining M3 packets are calibrated against, and it is decisive:

- **T3.6 trades goods.** With `Sectors.Default` at 100% farming, an undirected world contains grain
  and nothing else. Trade tested there is trade in a one-good world — goods flow low→high price,
  transport cost shapes what travels, bulk goods stay local: none of those acceptance clauses can be
  exercised when only the numéraire exists.
- **T3.7's merchants emerge on trade volume**, and its discriminating test is "merchants emerge in a
  trading world and not in an isolated one". A one-good world cannot supply the positive arm.
- **T3.10 requires the comparative-advantage emergence test** — settlements must specialize, and the
  specialization must track *relative* advantage. That is impossible without settlements producing
  different goods.

Homing this in T3.8 would leave T3.6, T3.7 and their corridors calibrated against a degenerate world
and then invalidate them. Homing it *inside* T3.6 would widen an adversarial-mandatory packet whose
own scope is already the milestone's second-largest risk. A small directed packet before T3.6 is the
cheapest correct option, and it keeps T3.6's blast radius its own.

## Item 1 — the founding default sector allocation

`Sectors.Default` (`Sim.Core/State/WorldState.cs`) returns `Farming: 1.0`, labelled "M2 semantics".
Replace with a plausible subsistence mix: **majority farming, minority extraction and crafting**,
plus whatever construction the housing work will need.

**DERIVED, NOT FITTED.** The ruling is explicit that this is TUNE data "justified as what a village
actually does, not fitted to make grievance look nice". So:

- State the reference class — what fraction of a neolithic/early-bronze village's labour-year went
  to cultivation vs herding, woodcutting/quarrying, and craft. Cite the reasoning, as T3.2b did for
  `yieldPerArableKm2PerYear = 26.0`.
- Fix the numbers from that reference class **before** looking at what they do to grievance,
  satisfaction or the corridors. Record the numbers and the justification, then measure.
- If the resulting world fails a corridor, that is a finding to report — **not** a licence to move
  the allocation until the corridor passes. Fitting here would reproduce exactly the error the
  corridor ruling of T3.4b condemned.

### Item 1a — the regime-flip threshold s\*, recomputed from SHIPPED values (pre-committed, §7.13)

**Written and committed BEFORE the mix is derived.** The directing prompt's arithmetic
(s\* ≈ 0.295) is not trusted; recomputed here from the tree:

- `outputPerFarmerPerYear` = **5.0** (`sim.json`), `outputPerHerderPerYear` = **3.0**,
  `toolYieldBonusMax` = **0.3** ⇒ tool multiplier m ∈ [1.0, 1.3] (m = 1 + 0.3·equipRatio).
- a ≈ **0.57** adult share (ADR-013's canonical value; the binding condition and its derivation are
  ADR-013 §4's, quoted: *"land binds whenever a·s·m·outputPerFarmerPerYear > c, at every yield"*).
- c ≈ **0.84** mean food consumption per head per year — cross-checked against the director's gate
  session (Diles: consumption ≈ 1,710/turn over 206 people at dt = 10 ⇒ 0.83/person-year) and the
  D-035 baskets (1.0/adult-year, cohort-weighted).

**Correction 1 (D-035): herding/fishing labour also produces food.** The threshold applies to total
food-producing labour weighted by output rate, not to farming alone. Land (plus deposits) stays the
binding side iff

    a · ( s_farm · m · 5.0  +  s_herd · ā · 3.0 )  >  c ≈ 0.84

with ā the settlement's mean food-deposit abundance ∈ [0, 1].

**Correction 2 (T3.3 tools): m > 1 wherever tools are stocked**, pushing the flip point DOWN.

**The corrected threshold, stated for the worst and best cases** (herding term at ā = 0 is the
conservative bound — a settlement with no livestock/fish deposits gets no food from herding):

| case | flip point s\*_farm |
| --- | --- |
| worst: m = 1.0, ā = 0 (no tools, no food deposits) | s\* = 0.84 / (0.57 × 5.0) = **0.295** |
| m = 1.3, ā = 0 | 0.84 / (0.57 × 6.5) = **0.227** |
| m = 1.0, ā = 1, s_herd = 0.15 | (0.84/0.57 − 0.45) / 5.0 = **0.205** |
| best: m = 1.3, ā = 1, s_herd = 0.15 | (0.84/0.57 − 0.45) / 6.5 = **0.157** |

The prompt's 0.295 is confirmed as the worst-case farming-only bound; the two corrections buy
headroom exactly as the ruling predicted.

**READINGS, FIXED NOW, before the mix exists:**

- Derived mix lands **ABOVE** the applicable s\* → the world stays land-bound. Report the new
  headroom multiple and proceed.
- Derived mix lands **BELOW** it → a REGIME CHANGE caused by a reference-class derivation. Report
  it as a finding about the equilibrium-density invariant's precondition (ADR-013: "asserted, not
  assumed" — `EquilibriumInvariantTests` goes red and the density corridor stops pointing at the
  yield constant). Do NOT raise the mix to clear the threshold — CR-003 §5.1 in full force.

### Item 1a APPLIED — the measured verdict (T3.5b implementation, director injection 3)

**The derived mix landed ABOVE s\* — reading 1 fires: the world stays land-bound. The basis of the
comparison, stated so nobody later misreads it:**

- The headline comparison — farm share **0.55** against s\* = **0.295** — is the **CONSERVATIVE
  reading**: it credits herding/fishing with zero food (ā = 0) and assumes no tools (m = 1).
  Headroom on that basis: 0.55 / 0.295 = **1.87×**.
- **Total food-producing labour is 0.70 of the labour-year** (farming 0.55 + herding/fishing 0.15),
  not 0.55. Under D-035 the food basket includes livestock and fish, so the full-credit reading is
  capacity a·(0.55·m·5.0 + 0.15·ā·3.0) against c = 0.84:
  - m = 1.0, ā = 1.0: 0.57 × 3.20 = 1.824 vs 0.84 → **2.17×**
  - m = 1.3, ā = 1.0: 0.57 × 4.025 = 2.294 vs 0.84 → **2.73×**
- **The 1.87–2.73× headroom range therefore comes from BOTH corrections**: the lower end is
  farm-only/no-tools (conservative); the upper end stacks the tool multiplier (m → 1.3) AND full
  food-deposit abundance (ā → 1). Neither correction alone spans the range.

The regime stays land-bound in every case; `EquilibriumInvariantTests` stays green; the density
corridor keeps pointing at the yield constant.

### Item 1b — the "food economy doesn't move" reading (pre-committed, §7.13)

This spec's own "Expect the food economy to move" is PROBABLY WRONG, and the director's gate
session is the evidence: Diles at turn 34 held ~647 years of reserve (110,779 food / 206 people at
0.83/person-year); the world held ~682 (2,750,689 / 4,862). Diles was already in deficit at 26%
farm share and would take ~586 turns to drain. A mix cutting farm share to ~0.6 removes ~40% of
harvest against a centuries-deep buffer: it slows accumulation, it does not produce scarcity.

**READINGS, FIXED NOW:**

- The food economy MOVES measurably → re-measure the corridor anchors as this spec directs; report
  the deltas.
- The food economy DOES NOT MOVE → that is DIRECT B-2 EVIDENCE, not a sign the mix is too gentle.
  Report it as such and escalate to M4 blocking material B-2. Do NOT push farm share down chasing a
  response — that is fitting, and it walks toward s\*.
- It moves in the WRONG DIRECTION or by an implausible magnitude → report and stop; adjust nothing.

Family resemblance to §7.13's worked example, stated in advance: an unbounded granary masks every
food-economy perturbation as easily as a defect masks a corridor distance. The correct response to
a masked perturbation is to say so with a number.

**Expect the food economy to move.** Labour out of farming is food not grown; the Malthusian
corridors and the equilibrium-density invariant are all downstream. Re-measure, do not re-fit.

## Item 2 — the variety reference point

Ruled: **neither existing candidate.** Perfect-evenness normalisation makes a fully supplied world
grieve forever; declared-basket normalisation lets a settlement self-certify out of the penalty by
declaring monotony. Both make an authoring choice mechanically load-bearing.

Replace with a **fixed nutritional diversity standard in data** — what a varied pre-modern diet
actually looked like — independent of both what the class asked for and what it received.

- Derive the standard from a stated reference class (the same discipline as item 1), not from the
  shipped baskets and not from what makes the numbers land well.
- The mapping must keep D-035-A's rationale intact and this must be **measured**, not asserted: a
  settlement feeding itself entirely on grain still scores strictly below one that imports fish, so
  it still has a reason to trade.
- Full satisfaction must become **reachable** — a settlement supplied at or above the standard
  scores exactly 1.0. This is the property the current form cannot express, and it revives
  `NeedsAggregation.Aggregate`'s exact-saturation branch, which T3.5's review measured **dead** in
  shipped config.
- Ship the anti-tautology bar: the standard is data, so a test must show satisfaction responding to
  the *standard* independently of the basket, or the two are not really separate inputs.

## Item 3 — ghost-class grievance

Confirmed pre-existing from T2.6 (the T2.13 fix deliberately covered extinct *settlements* only) and
reassigned correctly — **and fixed here anyway**, because T3.5 raised its magnitude from ~1e-3 to
~119, so what was invisible is now load-bearing on the goldens.

- Grievance must not accrue for a class with **no population**, consulting `ClassStates.Active`.
- Decide and document what an emerging class inherits: a class that emerges at turn 52 starting from
  zero is a semantic choice, not an obvious default, and the frozen decisions do not settle it.
- The T2.13 principle is the anchor: *grievance is held by people.*

## Item 4 — the two load-time checks

Adopt **both**. The ruling's reasoning is the governing one: *configs that are silently inert are
the deepest failure class this project has* — deepsea.png, the untested SectorAllocation path, the
rail deletable with 333 green.

1. **A basket entry naming a class id that is not in the class registry must FAIL at load.** Today
   `{"class": 999, ...}` loads clean and renders the need fully inert; T3.5's inertness lens
   demonstrated a bound need at weight 1e9 producing a bit-identical world hash through this path.
   With two classes shipped, a stray `3` is a realistic typo.
2. **The bound-need guard must be (class, need)-scoped, not registry-scoped.** A per-class hole is
   currently silent: removing artisans' Shelter lines makes artisans *less* aggrieved under total
   roof collapse (measured 0.7075 vs 0.7236), because dropping the need also drops its weight from
   the accrual scale.

That these change which configs are legal **is the point**. Rejection messages must be actionable on
the T0.4 precedent: name the file, the entry index, the offending value, and what a correct one
looks like. `Sim.Tests/Systems/NeedsGrievanceTests.cs`'s registry assertion encodes the weak
(registry-scoped) property and must be tightened with the guard.
