# M3 — GOODS, PRODUCTION & MARKETS: SPEC & TASK PACKETS
### The economy arrives. Settlements stop being identical food-machines and become places that make different things, trade them, and price them. Written post-M2-exit against code-truth, per S8 §4.

---

## 1. DECISIONS CLOSED THIS DOCUMENT

- **D-030 — No money at M3. Prices are grain-equivalent exchange ratios.** Grain is the numéraire (price ≡ 1 by definition); every other good's price is expressed in grain-equivalents. Rationale: coined money is an *institution*, not a starting condition, and introducing a conserved money stock now would drag monetary architecture (minting, debasement, credit) into the milestone that can least afford it. Historical warrant: Mesopotamian barley/silver accounting worked exactly this way. Money as an institution is deferred to its own milestone; the price tables are already denominated so the later switch is a re-denomination, not a rewrite.
- **D-031 — Goods roster v1 (14 goods, data-driven, `goods.json`).** Food: **grain** (numéraire), **livestock**, **fish**. Raw: **timber**, **stone**, **clay**, **copper ore**, **tin ore**, **fiber**, **hides**. Processed: **bronze**, **tools**, **pottery**, **cloth**. Housing is NOT a good — it is a durable per-settlement stock built from timber/stone/clay (§3). Sits inside the Scale Charter's 12–15 staging.
- **D-032 — Production is price-driven, not player-micromanaged.** The player allocates labor across a small number of **sectors** (farming · herding/fishing · extraction · crafting · construction) per settlement. *Within* a sector, what actually gets produced is decided by the sim from relative prices and local endowment. Rationale: the authority/bandwidth doctrine (Spine S1) forbids a slider per good, and price-driven allocation is both more historical and less UI. The M2 farm/path slider generalizes into this sector allocation.
- **D-033 — The price solver, fixed by mandate (Spine, "market solver mandate").** No global equilibrium solve, ever. Per settlement, per good: `p *= exp(λ · (excessDemand / marketScale) · dt)` **(AMENDED by ADR-016, 2026-07-26 — was `p += λ · p · (…) · dt`; exact integration replaces Euler because price COMPOUNDS. Every other element of the mandate is unchanged.)**, clamped to a TUNE band AND to a maximum per-turn relative change. Excess demand = consumption demand + input demand − production − stock release. Grain's price is pinned at 1. Damped, incremental, explainable, bounded — the same discipline that stabilized migration at T2.8, applied to prices before it can misbehave.
- **D-034 — Trade is arbitrage on the existing network, damped by the T2.8 pattern.** For each connected settlement pair and good, if the price gap exceeds transport cost (per-good bulk × edge cost, from the T1.3/T1.6 network — one object, three jobs), goods flow low→high price, **capped at a TUNE fraction of the gap-closing quantity**. Overshoot is structurally impossible at f < 1. Famine-flight's lesson applies inverted: flows respond to gaps, and a gap that closes stops the flow.
- **D-035 — Needs bindings extend from one to four.** Sustenance ← a food basket (grain/livestock/fish, class-weighted). **Shelter** ← housing stock per capita. **Comfort** ← pottery + cloth. Belonging/Faith, Safety, Health, Dignity, Prospects remain unbound and continue to contribute exactly nothing (the T2.6 zero-effect gate still binds). D-018's class-differentiated baskets become real here: peasants and artisans want measurably different things.
- **D-036 — One new class: Merchants** (emergence predicate on sustained trade volume, per the D-020 DSL). Laborers/Capitalists wait for wage employment and private firms respectively. One class per milestone stays the discipline.
- **D-017 CLOSED — Settlement growth.** A settlement carries a **size** stock grown from population and construction, which drives housing capacity, catchment radius bonus, and (render-side) footprint. Sprawl visuals are symbology — deferred per the queue.
- **Scaffolding demolition (mandated, per M2 §1):** the M2 artisan tool-yield multiplier and artisan construction-labor abstraction are DELETED. Tools become a real good that raises farm yield when stocked and consumed; construction consumes real materials and labor. If the demolition changes trajectories, that is the point — re-pin deliberately.

## 3. SYSTEM NOTES (kernel-contract shapes)

- **Goods stocks** are conserved `long` quantities per (settlement, good), registered as conservation quantities — the auditor covers all 14 by name. The M2 `FoodStore` migrates into grain. Remainder accumulators per row, T0.5 precedent.
- **Recipes** are data: inputs (goods + labor) → outputs, with a per-recipe era/knowledge gate expressed in the D-020 DSL (bronze requires the alloy recipe to be *available*, not a calendar date — law 4).
- **Housing** is a durable stock: built by the construction sector from timber/stone/clay, decays at a TUNE rate, and supplies the Shelter need per capita. Decay makes maintenance a real ongoing cost rather than a one-time build.
- **Prices** live in a per-(settlement, good) table as `double`; they are *not* conserved and never touch the Ledger. All goods movement — production, consumption, trade — goes through Ledger transfers.
- **Pipeline (M3 preset):** Catchment → Production → Consumption → Market (price update) → Trade → ClassMobility → Migration → Demographics → Needs/Grievance → Construction → Chronicle-detect. Prices update *after* consumption so they respond to realized scarcity, and *before* trade so arbitrage acts on current prices; state the one-turn-lag consequences in the header.
- **Extraction is endowment-bound:** ore, stone, clay, timber availability derive from the terrain fields already generated (T1.1/T1.2) plus a per-settlement deposit roll at founding. This is what makes settlements *different*, and it is the precondition for comparative advantage to emerge at all.
