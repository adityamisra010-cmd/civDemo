# T3.5 finding — the default world saturates Shelter and Comfort at zero

**Status: reported, not fixed. Director ruling requested.** Raised by the implementing agent during
T3.5, not by a review lens.

## What happens

`Sectors.Default` allocates **100% of labour to farming** (`Sim.Core/State/WorldState.cs`, labelled
"the never-ordered default: all labor farms (M2 semantics)"). An undirected settlement therefore
extracts no timber or stone, fires no pottery and weaves no cloth. T3.5 binds Shelter to a dwelling-
materials basket and Comfort to pottery/cloth, so in every world the director has not explicitly
allocated:

- Shelter satisfaction = **0.00**
- Comfort satisfaction = **0.00**
- Sustenance ≈ **0.85** (fed, but a grain monoculture — the D-035-A penalty, working as designed)

Under D-035-B's CES the two zeroes dominate. Measured on the dev world:

| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| Tier A gate — Shelter severity | 1.0 (fully unmet gate need) |
| Shelter weight after the gate | 0.9 × (1 + 3) = 3.6 |
| Comfort weight after the gate | 0.0 — the upper need is weighted **out**, correctly |
| CES aggregate S | ≈ **0.063** |
| grievance accrual | ≈ 2.06 per sim-year, in **every settlement, every turn** |

## Why this is a finding and not a bug

Every mechanism behaved as ruled. The Tier A gate fired and collapsed the upper need exactly as
d018:46 says it should. The CES refused to let a full belly buy off a missing roof, which is
precisely what D-035-B exists to guarantee. The model is saying something coherent: *you put every
pair of hands in the fields, so nobody built a house.*

The problem is what it does to **discrimination**. Grievance now sits near its ceiling in every
settlement in every default world, so it distinguishes nothing between them — and ADR-015 §7.5
forbids asserting on a quantity resting against its own limit. Concretely, it cost two existing
rigs their contrast and they had to be re-cut (both re-cuts are in the packet, each with its reason
stated):

- `Famine_RaisesGrievance_InTheStarvationWindow` — the famine's contribution became a rounding error
  against the saturated background, and the starving arm actually ended with **less** grievance than
  the fed control, because its higher mortality decayed the stock faster (D-021 generational decay).
  Re-cut to bind Sustenance alone, isolating the variable.
- `Plenty_DecaysGrievance_MeasuredHalfLifeDocumented` — "fed" no longer implies "supplied", so a fed
  production autoplay accrued instead of decaying. Re-cut onto a hand world where full supply can
  actually be stated.

Grievance drives no behaviour until M5 (read-isolation gate), so nothing downstream is wrong today.
What is at risk is the instrument: if grievance is saturated for the whole of M3 and M4, it is not
measuring anything when M5 finally reads it.

## The options, stated without a recommendation being smuggled in

1. **Accept and leave it.** The world is correct; the director allocates sectors from T3.9 and the
   saturation disappears the moment anyone does. Costs: grievance is uninformative until then, and
   every future rig that wants a grievance contrast must isolate its needs first.
2. **Change `Sectors.Default` to a subsistence mix** (some herding / extraction / crafting /
   construction). This is arguably what "M2 semantics" was always a placeholder for. Costs: it is a
   T3.3 artefact and outside T3.5's scope, it re-pins every golden a second time, and it changes the
   food economy — labour moved out of farming is food not grown.
3. **Give households self-provision** — subsistence families repair their own roofs and weave their
   own cloth from labour that never enters a sector pool. This is economically the most realistic
   reading, and it is the one that would keep Shelter meaningful without a sector order. Costs: it
   needs a carrier (household labour) that does not exist at M3, and inventing one to relieve a
   symptom is exactly the move D-035-C's carrier test exists to refuse.
4. **Defer Shelter's binding to T3.8**, when a real housing stock exists, and ship T3.5 with
   Sustenance and Comfort only. Costs: contradicts the m3-spec's T3.5 acceptance line, which names
   Shelter explicitly.

My reading, offered as a reading: option 1 is correct for **this packet** — the mechanism is right
and the scope is fixed — and the question of whether the all-farming default should survive M3
belongs to T3.8/T3.9, where housing and the sector controls actually land. I have not acted on that
reading beyond shipping option 1, and the choice is the director's.
