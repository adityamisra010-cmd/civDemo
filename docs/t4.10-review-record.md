## WHY THIS MAKES THE COEFFICIENT UNDERIVABLE

1. **The signal is a constant.** `FoodSufficiency = 1 − 0 = 1.0` at every settlement, every turn,
   every seed measured. Migration responds to the **gap** in attractiveness; a term that is
   identically equal at both ends of every pair contributes **exactly zero** to every gap in the
   shipped world.
2. **Therefore the coefficient is unfalsifiable.** No value of `AttractivenessFoodWeight` changes
   any gap-driven flow in the canonical world. This is verbatim the pathology `cr-003.md:107`
   forbids: *"A constant that is selected in zero turns of the whole campaign is unfalsifiable by
   construction. It cannot be validated, refuted, or benched."* Picking a number here would ship
   exactly that.
3. **The ratified derivation method is degenerate for this signal.** T3.4b's design point
   (`sim.json`'s `_docLandWeight`) is *"the weights are … set so the two terms contribute COMPARABLY
   to the differential at FED conditions."* The food differential at fed conditions is now
   **exactly 0**, so no weight can equalize it — the method divides by zero rather than yielding a
   value.
4. **The replacement method is another packet's assigned work.** `docs/t3.4c-remeasurement.md:162-170`
   rules the dispersion balance invalid (*"dispersion is not a model constant"*) and prescribes a
   **reference-condition level ratio** taken *"from a reference class rather than from a measurement
   of the sim."* The quantity needing a reference class is fertility-weighted arable km² per capita —
   an abstract index, not hectares. That exercise is explicitly owned by **T4.12 — the migration-weight
   packet** (`docs/m4-spec.md:273`, *"still unhomed: design point missed 2.3×–8.1×"*). Deriving it
   here would be doing T4.12; deriving it from my own probe would be the sim-measurement route T3.4c
   rejected. A prior attempt at exactly this re-derivation (T4.1f) was computed and **reverted**
   (`docs/t4.1f-review-record.md:64`).

## ALGEBRAIC CONSEQUENCE — THE SURVIVING EFFECT IS NOT A FOOD SEMANTIC

With sufficiency ≡ 1 the food weight does not vanish from the system entirely; it survives in the
T2.8 gap-closing cap. Writing `R_s = fw·suff_s·P_s + lw·A_s` (the dimensionally-correct placement —
see below), at `suff ≡ 1`:

```
m* numerator   = R_dst·P_src − R_src·P_dst = lw·(A_dst·P_src − A_src·P_dst)   ← fw cancels EXACTLY
m* denominator = R_src + R_dst             = fw·(P_src+P_dst) + lw·(A_src+A_dst)
```

So `fw` appears **only in the denominator**: a larger food weight silently *shrinks* `m*` and tightens
the cap. Its one remaining effect on the shipped world is as an undeclared damper on migration
volume — not as a statement about food attractiveness at all. Calibrating a "food attractiveness
weight" by its side-effect on a cap denominator would be a fitted constant wearing a semantic label.

## A DIMENSIONAL POINT THE IMPLEMENTATION WOULD HAVE HAD TO FIX

Worth recording because it survives whichever option is chosen: `DeficitRatio` is **intensive**
(already demand-relative), whereas the raw stock it replaces is **extensive**. Substituting it
directly into `resources[s]` — which is then divided by population at `instant[s] = resources[s] /
max(pop,1)` — would divide an already-normalized ratio by population a second time, making a small
well-fed settlement look arbitrarily more attractive than a large equally-well-fed one. The correct
placement is `resources[s] = fw·sufficiency·pop + lw·arableKm2`, which preserves `instant = R/P`
exactly and therefore keeps the `m*` closed form algebraically valid. Any future implementation must
use that form, not the naive substitution.

## SECOND FINDING — THE MEASURED COMPOSITION OF `R`, AND WHAT ACTUALLY MOVED THE CORRIDOR

At canonical seed 1, turn 100, using the measured values and the shipped weights:

- food term per capita = `0.02 × 1.08` = **0.0216**
- land term per capita = `0.078125 × 9.5` = **0.742**
- **food is ~2.8% of `R`; migration in the shipped world is already ~97% land-driven.**

And on the *differential* that actually drives flow:

- food differential = `0.02 × (1.0865 − 1.0722)` = **0.000286**
- land differential = `0.078125 × (10.0017 − 9.4761)` = **0.041063**
- **the food term already supplies ~0.7% of the gap.**

Two consequences follow, and both bear directly on the corridor question:

1. **The authorized change is very nearly a no-op on gap-driven migration.** Removing a term that
   contributes 0.7% of the gap moves gap-driven flow by ~0.7%. It would not restore the corridor and
   was never going to — the corridor moved for a different reason.
2. **T4.2 did not merely shrink the food signal; it flipped the regime.** T4.2's own record states
   stores fell *"from ~30 years to ~1.3"*, and T3.4c measured food out-contributing land by 2.3×–8.1×
   *before* T4.2. After T4.2, the measurement above shows land out-contributing food ~143× on the
   differential. **Migration flipped from food-dominated to land-dominated.** *(Stated precisely: the
   pre-T4.2 figures are T3.4c's dispersion ratio and T4.2's years-of-demand figure, not a controlled
   pre/post measurement of `R`'s composition in the canonical world — that comparison was not run.
   The post-T4.2 composition above IS directly measured.)*

The flip moved the sim **toward** its own ratified design position, not away from it — `sim.json`'s
`_docLandWeight`: *"in a frontier world the driver of movement is LAND OPPORTUNITY, because everyone
is fed and only room to farm distinguishes one site from another."* The migration corridor
`[0.001, 0.01]` was derived at T3.4b against the **food-dominated** regime that the same document
calls the wrong one. That is the strongest available argument that the corridor needs re-derivation
rather than restoration — and it is an argument from the ratified design position, not from the
inconvenience of a red test.

## A DOUBLE-COUNTING CONCERN, RAISED NOT RESOLVED

Hunger already enters migration through **two** ratified channels: `FamineFlightFactor = 8.0`
(gap-independent push out of a starving source) and `DestinationDeficitRepulsion = 1.0`
(`viability = max(0, 1 − deficit_dst)`, multiplying every arriving flow, hard-zero at deficit 1.0).
A graded sufficiency term inside the attractiveness gap would be a **third** hunger channel. The
system has explicit precedent for declining exactly this: `MigrationSystem.cs:74-78` records that a
crowding-saturation term was *"CONSIDERED AND DECLINED"* because *"a third term would be a
free-floating modifier stacked on a mechanism that already saturates (law 2)."* Whether the same
reasoning applies to a third hunger term is a director call, not mine.

## THREE OPTIONS — NONE TAKEN

- **A. Delete the food term from attractiveness** (`R = lw·arableKm2`), leaving hunger to the two
  existing ratified channels. Requires **no new constant** — it removes the underivable one rather
  than re-deriving it; matches the ratified STATED POSITION exactly; resolves the double-counting
  concern; makes `m*` reduce cleanly to `(A_dst·P_src − A_src·P_dst)/(A_src+A_dst)`, independent of
  both weights. Smallest change, strongest derivation story, but **larger in intent** than the
  authorized instruction, so not taken.
- **B. Implement sufficiency as authorized, with a provisional coefficient**, explicitly flagged
  unfalsifiable, and hand the balance question to T4.12. Delivers the authorized change but ships the
  constant `cr-003.md:107` forbids.
- **C. Route the coefficient to T4.12** (its assigned owner), implement the signal change only once
  T4.12 has produced a reference-class basis for the food/land balance.

**Recommendation: A, with C as the fallback** — but this is a design decision on a frozen-adjacent
mechanism and the packet's own STOP conditions reserve it. Reported for ruling.

# OPTION A IMPLEMENTED — THE FOOD TERM IS REMOVED FROM MIGRATION ATTRACTIVENESS

Director ruling on the Phase-1 handback: **Option A**. Do not invent a provisional coefficient, do
not route around the underivability by choosing an arbitrary weight — remove the term.

## THE CHANGE

```
R_i = LandWeight × arableKm2_i          (was: FoodWeight × food_i + LandWeight × arableKm2_i)
```

`instant_i = R_i / max(P_i, 1)` is unchanged in form, so the T2.8 gap-closing closed form still
solves the same equalisation problem. Food reaches migration through the channels it already had and
that were already ratified — famine flight (source push, `FamineFlightFactor`), destination-deficit
repulsion and the absolute food gate (both inside `viability`). **No new channel was added**, on the
same law-2 reasoning `MigrationSystem` already records for declining a stacked crowding term.

**Production files (3):**
- `Sim.Core/Systems/Migration/MigrationSystem.cs` — one behavioural line; the rest is the doc block.
- `Sim.Core/Systems/SimConfig.cs` — `AttractivenessFoodWeight` record field + its `RequireRate`
  validation removed (no remaining reader, and `JsonRequired` forbids leaving the key orphaned).
- `Sim.Data/content/sim.json` — `attractivenessFoodWeight: 0.02` removed; `_docLandWeight` carries a
  T4.10 note and preserves the T3.4b two-term derivation for provenance.

## PHASE 4 — MATHEMATICAL REVIEW, ITEM BY ITEM

- **Dead coefficient:** none. `AttractivenessFoodWeight` is deleted rather than left at 0.0.
  `AttractivenessLandWeight` is NOT dead — see the next point for where it still binds.
- **`m*` degeneracy:** with `R = lw·A`, **`lw` cancels exactly** from the cap:
  `m* = (A_dst·P_src − A_src·P_dst)/(A_src + A_dst)`. `lw` therefore no longer influences the CAP,
  but still scales `instant`, hence the gap, hence `gapDesire` — so it still determines whether the
  cap binds. Recorded because whoever re-derives `landWeight` (T4.12) needs to know its role changed
  shape.
- **Divide-by-zero:** none introduced. `resources` is used in exactly five places (declaration,
  write, `instant`, and the two `m*` sites); the only R-denominated division is already guarded by
  `denom > 0.0`, which now also covers the new case of a settlement with arable 0 but food > 0.
- **Population-size bias:** none. `R` stays extensive (km²), `instant = R/P` stays km²/person. This
  is also why the *rejected* sufficiency substitution would have been wrong: `DeficitRatio` is
  intensive, so putting it in `R` and dividing by P again would have made small well-fed settlements
  spuriously attractive.
- **Flow-direction inversion:** possible, and INTENDED — wherever the food and land per-capita
  differences previously disagreed in sign and food dominated, the gap now follows land alone. This
  was not assumed away: the rigs that depended on a food-driven direction were identified and
  migrated explicitly (below).

excluded.** The cause is therefore in the interval T3.12 → T4.7, whose contents are the T4.1 audit
family (T4.1, T4.1b–T4.1g), **T4.2 store bounding**, T4.5 and T4.8.

Two of those are prior suspects on the record rather than fresh guesses:

- **T4.2 store bounding** is the change T4.10's own Option A analysis blames for destroying the food
  signal's meaning ("T4.2 bounded that stock … which destroyed its meaning as an attractiveness
  signal"), and CR-012's corridor bisection already attributed **migration 100 % to T4.2**. That
  finding and this one agree, and CR-012's is the earlier and stronger evidence.
- **T4.1b / ADR-018 moved `minSpacingKm` 480 → 95.2**, and this corridor measures relocation between
  sites **≥480 km apart** — a distance gate whose threshold is the *old* spacing constant. If sites
  are now packed at 95 km, a 480 km gate counts a different and much rarer event.

The second is the cheaper and more decisive test, and it is a **definition** question rather than a
mechanism one: whether `canonical.migrationGrossPerDecade` still measures what its own note says it
measures after ADR-018 moved the spacing that the 480 km threshold was chosen against. That is
corridor-specification work, which `corridors.json` names as T4.10's own.

## §N+2 — THE MIGRATION DEFICIT IS **IDENTIFIED**: T4.2's GRANARY CAP, and nothing else

§N+1 classified the migration deficit **D — neither T4.7 nor T4.10**. This section carries the
causal tree down to the mechanism. **The cause is found, verified by a config-only single-variable
control, and narrowed to ONE of T4.2's two knobs.**

### N+2.1 SOURCE FIRST — the metric was exonerated before any simulation was run

The directive was to test the measurement specification before blaming the mechanism. Done by
reading, not running:

- **`MigrationGrossPerDecade` (`AutoplayMetrics.cs:139`) has NO distance gate.** It is
  `Σ MigrationGross / Σ (population × dtYears) × 10` — a pure population-normalised sum.
- **The collector has none either** (`AutoplayMetrics.cs:75`): `gross = Σ MigrationFlows[i].Outflow`,
  every inter-settlement move, unfiltered.
- **So the corridor note's "between sites spaced ≥480 km apart" was never a FILTER.** It was a
  description of the WORLD at calibration time: `minSpacingKm` was 480, so every settlement pair was
  ≥480 km apart by construction. ADR-018 moved spacing to 95.2 km, which changes what the number
  DESCRIBES but removes nothing from what it COUNTS.

**Hypothesis "the distance eligibility changed" is therefore FALSIFIED at the source level.** No
simulation was spent on it.

A second source finding, free from the existing metrics: the metric is **strongly
population-dependent within a single run**. Pooling all 20 treatment seeds by population bucket:

| mean population | rate/decade |
|---|---|
| 5,046 | 0.006764 |
| 17,312 | 0.001385 |
| 51,647 | 0.000126 |
| 98,173 | 0.000147 |

A 46× decline across a 19× population range. The whole-run average is therefore dominated by the
late, high-population turns. **This does NOT explain the regression**, because T3.12's worlds reached
81,160–132,280 and today's reach 79,950–129,665 — the same range — but it is a standing property of
the corridor worth recording.

### N+2.2 THE CONTROL — config-only, two TUNE knobs, no code touched

`consumption.grainSpoilagePerYear` and `consumption.granaryYearsOfDemand` are **TUNE parameters in
`sim.json`**, and CLAUDE.md places data-file tuning explicitly inside ordinary authority ("tuning is
play, not amendment"). So T4.2's mechanism can be neutralised **without touching a line of code**:

| arm | change from `main` `8586863` | migration mean | in band | density mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TREATMENT | none | 0.00052 | 2/20 | 0.4128 |
| **ARM C** | spoilage `0.08 → 0.0` **and** granary `1.5 → 1e6` years | **0.00288 (×5.56)** | **8/8** | 0.4129 |
| **ARM D** | spoilage `0.08 → 0.0` **only** | **0.00049 (×0.94)** | 1/8 | — |

(Treatment means over the same 8 seeds. Raw metrics committed beside this record.)

### N+2.3 THE ANSWER

**Neutralising T4.2's store bounding restores migration to 5.56× and puts every seed back inside the
band. Neutralising spoilage ALONE does nothing (×0.94).** Therefore:

> **The migration corridor deficit is caused by T4.2's GRANARY CAP — `granaryYearsOfDemand = 1.5` —
> and not by spoilage, not by T4.7, and not by T4.10.**

CR-012's "migration 100 % T4.2" is **verified rather than assumed**, and sharpened: 100 % the cap,
0 % the spoilage.

**The mechanism, end to end, every link already in the tree:**

```
granaryYearsOfDemand = 1.5
  → every settlement's grain stock is pinned at ≈1.5 years of ITS OWN demand
  → `food` becomes a near-constant multiple of that settlement's population
  → R = FoodWeight×food + LandWeight×arable loses its food-driven DISPERSION
  → A = R/P loses most of its inter-settlement spread
  → the gap (smoothed[dst] − smoothed[src]) that drives every flow collapses
  → gross migration collapses
```

Spoilage cannot matter because **the cap binds first**: a stock held at the ceiling is not reached by
a decay rate. And this is exactly the number T4.10's own Option A measured from the other side — post
-T4.2 the attractiveness differential is ~97 % land-driven, the food term supplying 0.7 %. **T4.10's
food-term removal did not cause the collapse; it removed a term T4.2 had already emptied**, which is
precisely why Arm B measured ×0.95.

### N+2.4 CLASSIFICATION — **F, MEASUREMENT MISMATCH**, not a migration defect

T4.2 is ratified and bounding the store is correct — an unbounded store saturates forever, which is
the B-2 shape the packet exists to kill. The corridor band `[0.001, 0.01]` was calibrated at T3.4b on
a world whose grain stock was an unbounded, highly-dispersed signal. **A ratified mechanism change
removed the corridor's driver; the corridor did not detect a regression, it outlived its own
premise** — the same shape CR-003 §7.6 records for the `starvedTotal == 0` tooth, and the same words
`corridors.json` already uses for this very corridor: *"CORRIDOR-SPECIFICATION QUESTION, NOT A
MIGRATION DEFECT."*

### N+2.5 WHAT WAS NOT DONE, and why

No band was moved, no quarantine altered, no constant tuned, no golden re-pinned, no production code
changed. Arms C and D were config-only measurement instruments in throwaway worktrees and are
committed nowhere. Re-banding a corridor and redesigning migration's attractiveness signal are both
on the director's reserved list; the decision is handed back with the evidence rather than taken.

**T4.10 remains PENDING REVIEW.**

## §N+2 — THE MECHANISM, MEASURED: WHY MIGRATION SHUTS OFF, AND THE SPACING HYPOTHESIS KILLED

Everything below is measured on `main` `8586863`, canonical seed 1, 650 turns, via a temporary
observer probe that was deleted afterwards (`git status --porcelain` empty). No production code, no
config, no corridor and no quarantine was changed by any of it.

### N+2.1 THE OBSERVATION IS STRONGER THAN A NORMALISATION ARTIFACT

Gross migration does not merely fall as a *rate* — it falls in **absolute** terms while the
population grows twenty-fold:

| turn | population | gross migration this turn | cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 4,372 | 32 | 748 |
| 50 | 6,142 | 15 | 1,673 |
| 150 | 13,165 | 12 | 2,875 |
| 300 | 33,918 | 3 | 4,649 |
| 650 | 128,466 | **1** | 5,124 |

5,124 relocations in 650 turns, in a world that ends with 128,466 people. Migration effectively
switches off as the world matures.

### N+2.2 THE IMMEDIATE MECHANISM: THE ATTRACTIVENESS GRADIENT COLLAPSES

Gap-driven flow is proportional to `smoothed[dst] − smoothed[src]`, the dispersion of per-capita
attractiveness `A = R/P`. Measured on the same run:

| turn | A mean | A coefficient of variation | A spread (max−min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 3.464 | 0.0530 | 6.867e-1 |
| 50 | 3.052 | 0.0111 | 1.410e-1 |
| 150 | 1.563 | 0.0060 | 3.182e-2 |
| 300 | 0.648 | 0.0006 | 1.578e-3 |
| 650 | 0.187 | 0.0007 | **5.143e-4** |

**The CV collapses 76× and the absolute spread 1,335×**, and gross migration tracks it down. The
settlements converge to the same per-capita attractiveness, so the gradient that drives migration
vanishes. Migration is not being blocked — there is nothing left to drive it.

### N+2.3 THE ADR-018 SPACING-GATE HYPOTHESIS IS **KILLED**, three independent ways

The hypothesis was that the corridor undercounts because it scores relocation between sites ≥480 km
apart while `minSpacingKm` is now 95.2.

1. **STRUCTURAL — no gate can exist.** `MigrationGrossPerDecade` sums `m.MigrationGross`, which
   `AutoplayCollector` builds as `Σ MigrationFlows[i].Outflow`. `MigrationFlowRow` is
   `(Settlement, Inflow, Outflow)` — **per settlement, carrying no destination and no distance at
   all**. There is no pair and no distance anywhere in the measurement chain, so no distance filter
   is even expressible. The "≥480 km" in the corridor note described the WORLD at calibration time
   (every pair was ≥480 km apart because `minSpacingKm` was 480), not a filter in the code.
2. **GEOMETRIC — the ≥480 km population has NOT become small.** Measured pairwise, converted from
   travel cost at 16.0 km per cost unit: at turn 5, **57 of 66** pairs are ≥480 km (median 911 km);
   at turn 650, **44 of 66** (median 659 km). The great majority of pairs still clear 480 km.
3. **WRONG SIGN.** Closer settlements are *easier* to migrate between, because damping is
   `exp(−travelCost / 25)`. Median pairwise damping **ROSE** from 0.1098 to 0.1985 as paths were
   built. A spacing change of this shape predicts MORE migration, not less.

**Disposition: hypothesis refuted. It should not be raised again.**

### N+2.4 THE CAUSE, BY SINGLE-VARIABLE CONTROL: T4.2's STORE BOUNDING

`consumption.grainSpoilagePerYear` and `consumption.granaryYearsOfDemand` are both TUNE parameters,
so the control is **config-only — not one line of production code was touched.** T4.2 neutralised
means spoilage 0.08 → 0.0 and granary 1.5 → 1e6 years of demand, i.e. the pre-T4.2 unbounded store.
Everything else identical: same commit, same seed, same worldgen, same rivers, same spacing.

| turn | gross — **treatment** (`main`) | gross — **T4.2 neutralised** | A spread — treatment | A spread — neutralised |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 32 | 33 | 6.867e-1 | 6.349e-1 |
| 50 | 15 | 38 | 1.410e-1 | 2.753e-1 |
| 150 | 12 | 37 | 3.182e-2 | 1.384e-1 |
| 300 | 3 | 47 | 1.578e-3 | 6.761e-2 |
| 650 | **1** | **48** | 5.143e-4 | **4.626e-2** |
| **cumulative** | **5,124** | **34,416** | | |

**6.7× on cumulative gross migration; 90× on retained attractiveness dispersion at turn 650.**
Final populations are 128,466 against 131,164 — within 2%, so this is not a population effect.

**OLD → NEW → CAUSE.** OLD: an unbounded grain store, whose level varied freely between settlements
and carried real dispersion into `R = FoodWeight × food + LandWeight × arable`. NEW: a store bounded
at 1.5 **years of demand**, a ceiling that is *itself per-capita-normalised*, so every settlement's
food stock sits at essentially the same multiple of its own demand. CAUSE: the food term stops
carrying inter-settlement dispersion; `A = R/P` loses its differentiating signal; the gradient
collapses; gap-driven migration → 0.

This **confirms CR-012's attribution and supplies the mechanism CR-012 asserted but did not
demonstrate.** It is not a defect in T4.2, which is ratified and correct, nor in MigrationSystem.

### N+2.6 WHAT THIS DOES **NOT** SETTLE

Whether the corridor band should be re-derived is a **corridor change, reserved to the director**.
Nothing here re-tunes a band, lifts a quarantine or re-pins a golden. The evidence supports Option A
