decisions, the pre-committed readings and the derivation review **before implementation** — every

## The mechanism (D-034, fixed)

Per CONNECTED settlement pair, per good, once per turn: if |price gap| > transport cost
(`bulkPerUnit × path cost`, on the T1.3/T1.6 network — one object, three jobs), goods flow
low→high price, capped at TUNE fraction `f` of the gap-closing quantity. `f` validated in (0, 1)
at load, D-020 actionable-rejection idiom. Conservation via `Ledger.Transfer`, `long`, exact.

## Decisions, stated in advance

**(a) Transit loss: NO. Nothing is lost in transit, explicitly.** Transport cost is the ARBITRAGE
THRESHOLD — a deadband on the price gap — not a consumed quantity. Reasons: a physical transit
sink needs a carrier to make sense, and carriers are T3.7 (merchants); a percentage loss without a
carrier would function as a store drain, which the fence names as the store-bounding backdoor; and
the deadband already carries the economic content (goods whose gap cannot clear the cost do not
move). M4 consequence, noted: armies marching on this same graph consume supply for real — the
transit sink arrives there, with its own named reason, and nothing here precludes it.

**(b) Instantaneous within the turn.** Reads from PREV (prices, stocks published last turn — the
§3.2 one-turn lag), transfers within the step. In-transit cargo would be a new serialized stock,
a schema bump and a new conservation surface, bought for nothing at M3's timescale: one Neolithic
turn is TEN YEARS, and no pre-modern land journey between adjacent settlements takes a decade.
M4 consequence, noted: military logistics at finer timescales may need in-transit state on this
graph; that is M4's decision and this design leaves the graph untouched for it.

**(c) Ordering: NOT load-bearing, by construction — and the residue pinned.** Two sweeps over the
same deterministic pair list (pairs by (min row, max row) settlement order; goods in registry
order):
1. COMPUTE: every pair-good's desired flow from PREV state alone (pure — no sweep-1 result feeds
   another sweep-1 computation);
2. APPLY: if a settlement's total desired outflow of a good exceeds its stock, every outflow from
   that settlement scales by the SAME factor `available / totalDesired` (computed from sweep-1
   totals, not incrementally), then integer-floors with the remainder allocated in pair order.
This is the T2.8 family — desires computed, then a cap applied — NOT an iteration: sweep 2 never
re-decides anything from sweep 2's own effects, there is no convergence loop, no residual, no
second look at prices. If implementation ever needs sweep 2 to feed back, STOP and file a CR (the
directed prompt's own instruction). Outcomes are therefore ordering-independent except the ±1-unit
integer remainders, which follow pair order — a documented (score, id)-style tie-break shipping a
tie-dense test, per CLAUDe.md.

## Bulk values — pre-existing, REVIEWED against the reference class before measurement

`goods.json` already declares `bulkPerUnit` (T3.2, "the D-034 transport bulk", TUNE). The
derivation obligation is discharged as a REVIEW, recorded here before any trade measurement runs.

Reference class: what actually moved over pre-modern LAND distances. Grain moved badly — the
classic result is that carting grain doubles its cost within a few tens of km, so bulk staples
stayed local except by water; metals, tools and cloth moved well (high value per unit mass);
livestock walked itself (low EFFECTIVE bulk despite mass); quarried stone essentially did not move
by land at all except for monumental exceptions.

What matters mechanically is bulk PER GRAIN-EQUIVALENT VALUE at typical prices, since the deadband
compares `bulk × edge cost` against a PRICE gap. Review of the shipped values on that axis:

| good | bulk | review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| grain | 1.0 | unit = a person-YEAR of food (~180 kg) at price ≡ 1 — the reference against which everything is judged. KEPT. |
| livestock | 4.0 | walks itself; 4.0 per unit at a multi-grain price is effectively mobile. KEPT. |
| fish | 1.0 | dried fish travelled (stockfish is the classic long-distance protein). KEPT. |
| timber | 6.0 | moves badly by land, floated by water — the network's river edges are cheap, which models exactly this. KEPT. |
| stone | 10.0 | the immobile extreme. KEPT. |
| clay | 5.0 | local by construction (pottery moves, clay does not). KEPT. |
| copper/tin ore | 8.0 | ore is beneficiated near the mine; METAL travels, ore does not. KEPT (bronze at 2.0 is the traveller). |
| fiber / hides | 2.0 / 3.0 | intermediate. KEPT. |
| bronze / tools / cloth | 2.0 / 1.5 / 1.0 | the classic travellers — high value per bulk once prices reflect labor content. KEPT. |
| pottery | 3.0 | travelled widely in reality (amphorae); 3.0 with a processed-good price is mobile at moderate distance. KEPT. |

**Verdict: NO value changes.** The shipped roster already encodes the reference class's ordering
(stone/ore immobile ← timber/clay ← fiber/hides/pottery ← livestock ← metal/tools/cloth/fish
mobile) once read per-value rather than per-unit. Fixed here, before measurement; if measurement
later shows e.g. stone travelling freely, that is a finding about the mechanism or the price
spread, and CR-003 §5.1 forbids fixing it by touching this table.

## Pre-committed readings (§7.13) — each with a DISCRIMINATING observable (the T3.5b lesson)

**R1 — TRADE VOLUME.** Expectation from the reference class: inter-settlement trade in a
pre-modern agrarian world without merchants or money is a few percent of production — expected
range **0.5%–10% of total production (by grain-equivalent value) moving per decade**, with the
share concentrated in low-bulk-per-value goods. Discriminating observables: (i) traded fraction
per good per decade, (ii) the count of pair-goods whose gap exceeds the deadband at all.
- In range → report and proceed.
- Near zero → mechanism live but throttled; report WHICH throttle by measurement — if (ii) is
  near zero the deadband binds (transport cost vs price spread); if (ii) is substantial but (i) is
  tiny, f binds. Do NOT raise f or lower bulk to manufacture flow.
- Implausibly high (most production moving) → report and STOP — the gap or the cap is wrong, not
  the constants.

**R2 — THE UNBOUNDED-GRANARY INTERACTION.** B-2 is live (~1,240-year reserves); grain price gaps
may be structurally tiny. Discriminating observable: per-good traded volume with grain SEPARATED
from the rest, plus the measured grain price spread across settlements.
- Grain near-zero while pottery/cloth/tools trade normally → **B-2 EVIDENCE, escalate to M4
  blocking material** — NOT a reason to touch grain's bulk, the cap, or the price band. T3.5b
  fired this exact reading once; expect it again.
- Grain trades comparably → report; the granary is not yet masking price formation at this scale.

**R3 — PRICE-SOLVER COUPLING.** Trade→stocks→prices→trade is a real positive loop; D-021 requires
the paired negative loop. The dampers, named: (1) `f` < 1 (the flow never overshoots the gap),
(2) the price step's per-year rail and absolute band (D-033), (3) the transport-cost deadband
(gaps below cost produce zero flow — a true dead zone, not a small response). Pre-commitment: a
driven stress soak (forced production asymmetry, maximum plausible gap) is measured with the
oscillation detector, and the binding damper is identified by ablation IN A TEST RIG ONLY (f→0.9,
rail widened, deadband→0 — one at a time, in the rig, never in shipped data).
- Stable with margin → report the margin and which damper binds.
- Unstable or marginal → **report and STOP; never retune to stabilize.** The report names the
  ratified item (D-021 paired-feedback) and escalates for a directed ruling.

## The three queue items — dispositions declared up front

- **Extent of market:** OUT unless trivially clean — the artisan predicate's `population > 520`
  generalizing to trade-connected demand belongs with T3.7 (merchants emerge on trade volume;
  the Smithian claim is at home there). Decision recorded at handback if anything changes.
- **Recipe-chain ordering:** trade moves inputs between settlements but does NOT execute recipes;
  if the trade pass turns out to interleave with production ordering anywhere, it will be stated
  as a contract and pinned — expected answer: it does not (trade runs on stocks, after production,
  reading PREV prices).
- **Price solver complexity:** `sim bench` per-phase measured and reported, trade phase included;
  the O(S²·G²) residue re-measured at today's scale. No optimization.

## Acceptance (the spec's five, traps named in the directed prompt)

1. Flow runs low→high AND ceases at a measured turn when the gap closes below transport cost —
   both halves asserted, the gap measured on both sides of the cessation.
2. Conservation exact world-wide (FsCheck over generated sequences) AND the drawdown pinned where
   flow is expected (the timber-stranding blind spot; trade joins that queue list either way).
3. Unreachable pairs move exactly zero, both directions, forever — non-vacuous (a reachable pair
   trades in the same world).
4. Oscillation detector proven to fire on a known-oscillating series and stay silent on constant /
   monotone / one-flip series, BEFORE it watches trade.
5. Transport cost measurably shapes what travels — bulk goods stay local, dense goods travel —
   with the bulk table fixed above, before this measurement.
