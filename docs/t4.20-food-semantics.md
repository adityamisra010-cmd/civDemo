## PHASE 2 — is the asymmetry intent? THE REPOSITORY RECORDS NO INTENT EITHER WAY

Searched: every doc under `docs/`, every ADR, `docs/t4.2-manifest.md`,
`docs/queue.md`, and every comment in the consumption, production, trade,
migration, appropriation, colonization and worldgen systems.

**The only statement that exists** is a scope note, quoted in full
(`Sim.Core/Systems/Consumption/ConsumptionSystem.cs:198-199`):

> Grain only. B-2a's base layer is STORED GRAIN; every other good's bounding is
> enrichment and out of this packet's fence.

Read it precisely. It says *this packet does not do the others*. It is a
**SCOPE claim, not a physics claim**: it does not assert that livestock and fish
are imperishable, and it does not assert that they are perishable-but-deferred.
It is silent on the physics, and so is everything else:

* `docs/t4.2-manifest.md` — the manifest of the packet that introduced store
  bounding — **never mentions livestock or fish at all** (zero case-insensitive
  matches for either word in the whole file).
* `GoodEntry` carries **no** perishability or storage field, so the data layer
  cannot express the distinction even if someone wanted it (above).
* `Ids.cs:127-133` explains grain's *ledger* privilege as **continuity**, not as
  physics: `Harvest` (3) is kept "for grain (continuity: food_surplus_ratio and
  migration's anyFood gate key on the grain row)" — i.e. the reason grain is
  special there is that the M2 monoculture's call sites already keyed on it.

So the honest finding is: **the three goods are materially equivalent Sustenance
inventories in every line of the basket mathematics, and materially
non-equivalent in storage, raiding, colonisation, founding and migration — and
no document or comment in the repository ever decided that they should be.**
That is a gap in the RECORD, not a proven defect in the model. A director
ruling, not an agent's judgement, is what closes it.

## PHASE 3 — the measured consequence

Measured in this lane on the canonical FOUNDED world, seed 42, 300 turns, 12
settlements, 3,588 settlement-turns (turn 1 excluded: it harvests zero, the
documented T4.18 warm-up artefact — `ObservedWorlds.cs`), read entirely from
`SettlementRecord` fields:

| quantity | measured |
| --- | --- |
| grain share of all food EATEN, world, 300 turns | **88.101 %** (livestock 7.108 %, fish 4.791 %) |
| grain share of all food PRODUCED, world, 300 turns | 88.236 % |
| grain share of the food INVENTORY at turn 300 | **0.825 %** (grain 44,735; livestock 2,937,399; fish 2,441,890) |
| per-settlement LIFETIME grain diet share | min 88.015 %, max 88.280 % — **spread 0.265 pp** |
| per-settlement-turn grain diet share | 87.043 % – 90.056 % |
| herding-dominant settlement-turns (grain < half of food produced) | **0** |
| trade legs in any food good, ever | **0** |

The shape of it: grain does ~88 % of the feeding while holding under 1 % of the
food on hand. Livestock and fish accumulate without limit precisely because
nothing bounds, spoils or overflows them, and nothing moves them — they are
produced, partly eaten, and the remainder piles up forever. Meanwhile every
settlement eats the same diet to within a third of a percentage point: the
herding sector exists and ships, but no canonical world has ever made a
settlement herding-dominant, so the asymmetry has never been stress-tested by
play.

## PHASE 5 — what this packet did, and what it deliberately did not

### Implemented

**Observability** (`Sim.Core/Observability` only). Two new fields on
`FoodSection`, both per-TURN totals in person-year-equivalents:

| field | §0 kind | definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FoodProduced` | **SUMMED** | Σ over the category-`food` goods of the READ `GoodStockRow.LastProducedUnits` the record already carries — the same integer sum `ClassMobilitySystem.cs:131-135` forms as its surplus numerator. One definition, not two; pinned by test through the published variable. |
| `FoodBalance` | **DIFFERENCED** | `FoodProduced − DemandUnits`. Both terms are READ/SUMMED `long`s in person-year-equivalents, so the subtraction is dimensionally sound and exact. |

`FoodRequired` was **not** added: it already exists as `FoodSection.DemandUnits`
(READ, `ConsumptionDeficitRow.DemandUnits`) and is reused.

`FoodSupportedPopulation` and `FoodConstrainedGrowth` were **not** added: both
presuppose a population-cap semantics on which no ruling exists.

**Telemetry vintage.** `TelemetryWriter` emits `food.foodProduced` and
`food.foodBalance`, which changes the emitted field set, so the tag moved
`telemetry/v1` → `telemetry/v2`. This is the TELEMETRY vintage only.
`CanonicalSchema.Version` is untouched at **24**; nothing in
`Sim.Core/Observability` is serialized into `WorldState`.

**UI** (`Sim.Ui` only, additive). A new pure builder
`SettlementInspectorModel.FoodFlowLines(record, dtYears)` and a second
`DrawRecordTab` call under the existing one in `SimUiGame.DrawSettlementFood`.
`FoodLines` and its pins are byte-identical and untouched — the grain STORE
display stays until its semantics are ruled on. The verdict word comes from the
**sign of the balance alone**; the zero case is "balanced" BY DEFINITION, not by
a threshold, and no new constant was invented. The turn length in the header is
READ from `TurnRecord.DtYears` — nothing is divided into per-year figures.

### Deliberately NOT done — the three not-derivable quantities

Per settlement, the record renders these as **"not recorded"** with a reason,
never as a number:

* granary capacity, in units
* spoilage
* overflow

All three are numerically **exact-recoverable**, and that is exactly why they
are refused. The only route is an observer-side reimplementation of
`ConsumptionSystem.BoundStore`'s private arithmetic — capacity from the annual
grain demand and the consumption config, then the spoil-then-overflow split in
its stated order. §0 forbids that even when the copy is exact, and the
`grainPresent` precedent on this very branch (commit `8f59166`, a private copy
of MigrationSystem's `anyFood` gate, deleted by the verifier) is the standing
ruling on what happens to such a copy.

The **world-level** split is honest and available: `Spoilage` (14) and
`GranaryOverflow` (15) are DIFFERENCED ledger legs in `TurnRecord.GrainAccount`
and reconcile with zero discrepancy. World-level figures may be shown as
world-level figures. They may never be presented per settlement.

The director's brief reports the residual as 37.7 % overflow by magnitude — not
measured in this lane, and cited only to make the point that renaming the
per-settlement `StoreLosses` residual "spoilage" would be FALSE on most turns. Its identity string (`Observer.cs:41-45`) is truthful as
written and is unchanged.

### The unlock that exists and is NOT AUTHORIZED

There is a measured, hash-neutral-looking refactor that would make the three
quantities legally derivable: extract `BoundStore`'s capacity and its
spoil/overflow split into **public static** functions on the consumption system
and have `BoundStore` call them, so the observer could RECOMPUTE through the
simulation's own code rather than copy it — the §0 "RECOMPUTED" route, the same
one `SettlementHappiness.Of` already serves.

**NOT AUTHORIZED.** It is a change under `Sim.Core/Systems`, outside this
packet's absolute fence, and it touches a system the goldens pin. It is queued
(`docs/queue.md`) for a director ruling and is not attempted here.
