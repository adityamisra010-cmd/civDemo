P2 flips named goods clean across the band. P1's per-good effect at s0 is invisible **because
those three goods are resting against a band edge** — precisely §7.5's hazard, met here on the
non-grain side too. P1's evidence is therefore the aggregate: the driven non-grain price sum
moves 1065.743342 → 1067.988966, the founded 1269.871152 → 1269.754467, in OPPOSITE directions.
The solver's mathematics changed and the population of prices moved with it; the individual
goods chosen for the table happen to be clamped.

**Said explicitly, because it is the stronger reading and was nearly left implicit (director
ruling, Q2): the two worlds' sums move in OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS, and that is better evidence than
a shared shift would have been.** A common-mode artifact — a build difference, a stray global,
an environment effect, anything not the perturbation — would push both worlds the same way. It
cannot push one up 2.25 and the other down 0.12. Opposite signs are only producible by the
solver actually running on two differently-conditioned states. The weaker-looking arm is the
better-evidenced one.

**And the shape of that is itself a finding: P1's evidence had to CHANGE SHAPE mid-proof, and
the reason is escalation 2 operating inside a red proof.** The per-good reading was planned; it
came back invisible; the aggregate was substituted, and only then did P1 show. Nothing was
wrong with the perturbation or the plan — the three goods happened to rest on a band edge, and
**a clamped price cannot show a perturbation that would otherwise move it.** This is §7.5's
hazard ("never assert on a quantity resting against its own limit") arriving UNINVITED. §7.5 is
written as a rule about how to author a guard; escalation 2 turns it into a PROPERTY OF THE
WORLD that degrades guards nobody authored carelessly. Filed as Q-B's fifth prediction
(`docs/queue.md`) — the first Q-B prediction that is about this project's instruments rather
than its simulation.

### What the driven golden actually ADDS — measured, not claimed (§1.1)

The spec required this to be measured rather than assumed, and the measurement contradicts the
convenient answer:

**The existing founded golden ALREADY catches both perturbations.** Its hash changes under P1
and under P2. T3.6's finding (50 non-grain series off 1.0 on a no-order world) predicted this,
and this run confirms it: 156 of 156 non-grain rows are off 1.0 on the founded world too. The
blocking gap as originally written — "ADR-016 changed the solver's mathematics while leaving all
three goldens byte-identical" — was already **half closed by T3.2b/T3.5b** spreading production
across sectors, before this packet existed.

So the driven golden's marginal contribution is **NOT price sensitivity.** It is:

1. **Flow sensitivity** — the founded world moves zero goods, so no existing golden can regress
   on the trade solver at all. The driven world moves 46 units across 23 rows and 2 goods.
2. **A second, differently-conditioned witness to the price solver** — the two goldens' price
   sums move in opposite directions under P1, so they are not redundant copies of one reading.
3. **Coverage of the order path itself** — the anti-vacuity guard fails if `SectorAllocation`
   delivery ever silently stops landing, which no golden previously observed.

That is a smaller claim than "closed the blocking gap", and it is the one the measurements
support.
