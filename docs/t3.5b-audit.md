# T3.5b — the §7.14 audit, run ONCE, LAST, after the mix settled

**The first exercise of ADR-015 §7.14** (a packet that introduces a controlled experiment audits
its own rigs against the world's live drivers). Run after item 1 moved the sector mix, so the
audit measures the world these rigs actually ship into. **Margins recorded, not pass/fail** — a
margin is what a future driver erodes.

**Live stochastic drivers in the shipped world:** harvest weather (`HarvestWeatherSystem`, the
only per-turn stochastic driver; RNG otherwise enters only at worldgen/founding). Migration and
prices are deterministic responses, not drivers, but both PROPAGATE weather — a rig running the
full pipeline receives weather through food-per-capita and price paths even if it never reads a
weather row.

## The audit's findings

1. **No T3.5 or T3.5b contrast rig has a live driver reaching both arms.** Every rig either runs a
   pipeline containing no stochastic system, strips weather with the §7.8 justification recorded,
   or is a soak whose driver is the subject. The rebase evidence ("every contrast criterion passed
   unchanged") is now explained rather than merely observed: the criteria were isolated all along —
   by construction, not by enumeration, which is why nobody had written it down.
2. **One margin was actually consumed by this packet and restored:** the demographic famine rig's
   depth guard read 0.64 against its own 0.6 honesty bound under the new default mix — the exact
   §7.14 failure shape (a packet's change eroding a rig's margin) — and was restored by explicit
   isolation. Had §7.14 existed at T3.5, that rig would have carried the isolation row from birth.
3. **The fishery-failure rig's absolute levels moved with the standard** (both classes score higher
   at full supply now) while its contrast structure survived. Recorded so the next packet sees
   erosion rather than discovering a flip: the load-bearing quantity is the ~2× loss ratio, not the
   levels.
