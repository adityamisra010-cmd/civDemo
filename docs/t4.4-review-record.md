## §1 WHAT CHANGED, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Colonization no longer triggers on the source's consumption deficit. `MigrationSystem` now
writes the departure demand it could not place — `BucketRow.UnplacedDeparture` — and
`ColonizationSystem` founds from that and nothing else. **No migration equation, flow, cap,
gate, EMA, attractiveness term, pair selection, remainder, overdraw rule or pipeline position
was changed.** The addition to `MigrationSystem` is a pure write of a quantity it had already
determined; it moves no person and no grain, which is proven by measurement in §6.

---

## §2 EXACT ROOT CAUSE OF THE OLD FAILURE

The old trigger was `prev.ConsumptionDeficitRow.DeficitRatio > 0`, with party
`floor(count × deficit)`. It failed for two independent structural reasons:

1. **A deficit ratio is scale-free, so emigration can never clear it.** Removing
   `deficit × count` people removes their demand too, leaving the ratio essentially unchanged
   next turn. The same settlement colonised again, every turn, indefinitely.
2. **`ConsumptionDeficitRow` cannot distinguish "cannot feed the people it has" from "was
   founded three turns ago and is not producing yet."** Both read as a deficit. Measured on
   `ae1ebbd`: at turn 40, **7 of 7** settlements in deficit were ones colonization had itself
   founded; 12 → 178 settlements by turn 77 while total population **fell** 4330 → 3192.

**And it was never authorized.** D-037 B1 reads: *"Migration currently runs
settlement-to-settlement, and ADR-012 rules that with no viable destination people die at
home. **Extend it**."* The antecedent of "it" is migration, and the only condition B1 names is
ADR-012's no-viable-destination. B1 never mentions consumption deficit. The deficit trigger
was borrowed from T4.5/D-037 **B3**, whose subject is **raiding**.

## §3 PART 3 — WHERE THE DEMAND WAS, AND A CORRECTION TO THE AUDIT

The packet instructed me not to trust the audit's description. **It was wrong, and this
matters.** The audit said the quantity was *"already computed and merely discarded."* It is
not. `MigrationSystem` forms the flight term **only** inside its destination loop:

```
total += perCount * damping[src,dst] * viability[dst]
         * (gapScale * gap + FamineFlightFactor * deficit[src]);     // line 271, pre-change
```

When no destination is both reachable and viable, **every product is zero and the desire is
never expressed as a quantity at all.** It is **structurally absent, not discarded** — there
was nothing to consume.

What exists is its ingredients, and ADR-012 names the quantity itself verbatim: *"flight
desire remains source-driven (`FamineFlightFactor × deficit_source`), uncapped by the gap
mechanism, exactly as D-021 ratified"*, and viability *"only redistributes WHERE the fleeing
go."* The readout writes exactly that ratified term with the destination redistribution
removed **because there is no destination**.

**The gap channel contributes nothing and cannot.** A gap is `max(0, S_dst − S_src)` and needs
a destination to exist. There is no destination-free gap desire to leave unplaced. So the
unplaced quantity is flight, and only flight.

### 3.1 THE ONE INTERPRETIVE JUDGEMENT I MADE — flagged, not buried
Part 4 says the party *"must not be derived from settlement deficit."* **The demand has
`deficit_source` as an input, because ADR-012 defines the Exit valve's desire that way and
there is no deficit-free source-driven migration desire anywhere in the ratified model.** I
read the prohibition as aimed at the old *trigger shape* — the list it sits in is otherwise
about fabricating people (minted, sampled, timer, RNG, new TUNE) — and the new quantity
differs from the old trigger in the property that matters: **it is zero whenever any viable
destination exists.** If the director meant the prohibition literally, this packet cannot be
built as specified and §7 R2 becomes the ruling.

### 3.2 A DEFECT MEASUREMENT CAUGHT IN MY OWN RULE
My first condition read "no destination has `damping > 0` and `viability > 0`" — which fires
on a world whose `SettlementDistances` table has never been computed. **A genuinely
unreachable pair still HAS a row** (it stores `+∞`, and `exp(−∞) = 0`); "no row" means the
network has not been built yet. Migration cannot tell the two apart and does not need to (both
give zero flow), but colonization must: only the second is B1's condition, and treating
missing data as isolation founds settlements out of an empty table. Fixed with `hasDistances`;
the `n == 1` case is exempt because there is no network to be missing and being alone in the
world **is** the condition.

## §4 EXACT NEW CAUSAL CHAIN

```
food deficit at the source
      -> MigrationSystem forms flight desire  (FamineFlightFactor x deficit, per bucket)
      -> is ANY destination reachable AND viable?   (ADR-012's gates, unchanged)
           YES -> migration transfers.  UnplacedDeparture = 0.  Colonization does nothing.
           NO  -> UnplacedDeparture = the flight desire, per bucket, keys intact
      -> ColonizationSystem floors it into whole people (D-004 remainder banked)
      -> frontier site under the ADR-018 spacing floor + explicit SiteCell distinctness
      -> Ledger.Transfer of the party, key for key, + provisions
      -> the daughter holds food, so ADR-012's own gate makes it a VIABLE DESTINATION
      -> next turn the founder's demand is ZERO.  Founding stops.
```

**The cascade brake is a mechanism, not a rule.** There is no cooldown, no age threshold, no
newborn immunity, no timer, no minimum-population constant and no new RNG stream anywhere in
the diff. The old trigger made a newborn **emit** a founding; this one makes a newborn
**absorb** one. Opposite signs on exactly the settlement that broke the first implementation.

## §5 CONSERVATION PROOF

- **Nothing is created.** The party is `Ledger.Transfer` only. No `InitialEndowment`, no
  source flow, no minting. `UnplacedDeparture` is a *desire*, not a stock — no person is held
  in it and none is conserved there.
- **Left exactly once, arrived exactly once.** `SourceLosesEXACTLYWhatTheDaughterGains`
  isolates the colonization step and asserts both halves: `srcBefore − moved == srcAfter`
  **and** world population unchanged. (The first version of this test was WRONG — it assumed
  the source's only outflow was the party, but the world's one fed settlement also *receives*
  gap-driven migrants. The premise was fixed, not the assertion.)
- **No double-spend.** Colonization reads **live post-migration** buckets, so whoever migration
  moved is already gone; `MigrationAndFoundingCannotDoubleSpendTheSamePeople` runs both and
  asserts exact population plus no negative bucket.
- **PREV/live boundary preserved.** The demand is computed by migration entirely from PREV
  (`prev.Buckets`, `prev.SettlementDistances`, `prev.ConsumptionDeficits`) and written to the
  owned table. Colonization draws from LIVE counts and clamps the draw to the live count
  before promising it, so the T4.5 circular-transfer class of error cannot arise: the party is
  never larger than what is actually there.
- **Nothing silently discarded.** The sub-person fraction is banked in `UnplacedRemainder`
  (D-004, the same discipline as `MigrationRemainder`). When the frontier is full the drawn
  whole units are *not* carried forward — nobody moved, so nobody is owed a move — and the
  people remain in their buckets, so no conservation question arises.
- **Whole-world audit.** `FoundingConservesPopulationEXACTLY_NothingIsMinted` runs two arms:
  one on the stranded rig asserting **exact** population equality with no epsilon, and one
  that hand-mutates no stock so the full `ConservationAuditor` applies, **including the grain
  the colonists carry as provisions**. Both green.

## §7 FINDINGS AND DIRECTOR DECISIONS REQUIRED

### F1 — THE MECHANISM NEVER FIRES IN A CANONICAL WORLD. **R1 required.**
B1's condition is "no viable destination", and ADR-012 makes a destination viable if **any**
settlement has `store > 0` **or** `lastHarvest > 0`. In a twelve-settlement world that is
essentially always true, so implemented literally the mechanism is correct, self-limiting —
and inert. **CR-003 §5.2(a) asks T4.4 for *"how population converts empty land into settled,
worked land, and therefore how the frontier eventually closes"*. This delivers none of that in
the canonical world.**

The reason is architectural, not a coding error: **B1 authorizes refugee founding, and refugee
founding is a collapse phenomenon.** Land-hunger expansion — a fed population seeking better
land — is the gap channel, and the gap channel requires a destination to exist, so it can
never produce unplaced demand.

**The identified alternative, NOT implemented:** viability is graded
(`max(0, 1 − Repulsion × deficit_j)`), so a destination with deficit 0.9 has viability 0.1 and
absorbs only a tenth of the desire aimed at it. Most flight desire goes unrealised even when
destinations are nominally viable. Reading "unplaced" as *desire that was not realised* rather
than *no viable destination at all* would fire far more often. **I did not implement it**: B1's
words are binary, and choosing the graded reading changes behaviour substantially. That is a
ruling, not an implementation choice.

### F2 — T4.4 MAKES A CERTIFIED TEST'S SCENARIO UNREACHABLE. **R3 required.**
`FirstReignTests.FirstReign_PostFix_HarvestDiesWithThePeople_NoFoodMountain` is **RED**, and
**not on its golden** — I re-pinned that to the measured value — but on its **shape asserts**,
which are explicitly labelled *"the anti-blind-repin guard … never delete these as redundant
with the golden."*

The fixture is a director-played session in which a 0 %-farm order starves a lone settlement
to extinction; the test asserts extinction between turns 5 and 25 and that the dead world
stays dead. **With colonization, the settlement no longer dies — its people leave and found
daughters that farm normally, because the 0 %-farm order binds only the settlement it was
issued to.**

I am not going to call that a defect or a feature. It is arguably D-021's Exit valve working
exactly as ratified (*"closing exits is a policy choice that redirects pressure into Voice"*),
and it is arguably an escape hatch that makes a director order unenforceable. **I did not touch
the test's assertions in either direction** — weakening them to pass would be precisely the
blind re-pin the guard exists to catch.

### R1 — Is B1's condition binary ("no viable destination at all") or graded ("desire that
went unrealised")? The binary reading is shipped and is inert in canonical worlds; the graded
reading is the only route I can see to CR-003 §5.2(a)'s frontier closure without inventing a
new driver.

### R2 — Part 4's *"must not be derived from settlement deficit"*: literal, or aimed at the old
trigger shape? See §3.1. Read literally, this packet is unbuildable, because ADR-012 defines
the Exit valve's desire in terms of deficit.

### R3 — F2: does the FirstReign scenario get re-based onto a world where colonization cannot
fire, or is colonization wrong to let a settlement escape a farming order?
