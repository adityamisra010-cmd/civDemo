## §1 WHAT SHIPPED

`ColonizationSystem` (`SystemId(18)`, `"colonization"`), placed in `pipeline.json` **after
`migration` and before `demographics`** (index 10 of 14). Population that migration left
behind departs into unclaimed land and founds a new settlement — D-037 B1, verbatim:
*"groups may depart into UNCLAIMED land and found new settlements. A settlement founded by
departing population NEED NOT belong to the polity they left — refugee foundings may be
stateless."*

| concern | how it is satisfied | authority |
|---|---|---|
| trigger | source's PREV `ConsumptionDeficitRow.DeficitRatio > 0` — the same row and same read T4.5 uses | D-037 B1/B3 |
| party size | `floor(count × deficitRatio)` per bucket, key-for-key, clamped to count | derived, no constant |
| conservation | every person and every grain moves by `Ledger.Transfer`; **nothing is sourced** | law 1 |
| site choice | `SettlementSiting.ChooseFrontierSite` — same score field and same score floor as turn-zero siting | ADR-008 |
| spacing | the **physical settlement-distinctness floor** kept as a travel-cost distance, unchanged in value | DIRECTOR RULING, ADR-018, D-041 E2 |
| site distinctness | an **explicit** occupied-SiteCell rejection, separate from and not implied by spacing | DIRECTOR RULING |
| ADR-012 hazard | new settlement's `SmoothedAttractivenessRow` seeded from the SOURCE's smoothed value | ADR-012 |
| determinism | array scans only; no RNG stream added; ascending settlement order is the resolution order | law 5 |
| schema | **zero change.** `SettlementRow.FoundedTurn` already existed and was written only by `WorldFounding` as 0 | — |

**Spacing was treated exactly as ruled.** It is a physical distinctness floor and nothing
else: it is not a catchment rule, not land ownership, not attractiveness, not colonization
viability, not economic distance, not political territory. Catchment exclusivity did **not**
replace it, the numerical value was **not** recalibrated, and SiteCell distinctness is
enforced by its own explicit rejection — `ColonizationSystem`'s site loop keeps the two
rejections on separate lines, and `ColonizationTests` drives them apart by setting
`MinSpacingKm = 0.0` and proving the SiteCell rejection still fires on its own.

**Two shared-algorithm extractions**, so dynamic founding cannot drift from turn-zero
founding: `SettlementSiting.ScoreFloor` and `WorldFounding.AddDepositsForSite`. Turn-zero
founding now calls the extracted deposit routine, so both paths describe ground identically.

## §3 A DEFECT I SHIPPED AND MEASUREMENT CAUGHT — harness/CLI divergence

All 30 golden tests passed after the first implementation pass. That was **not** evidence
that T4.4 moves no golden; it was evidence that the golden harnesses were not running the
system. The harnesses called `SystemCatalog.All(cfg)` with no worldgen, so `colonization` was
null and no-opped there, while the CLI passed worldgen and did run it. Measured directly:

- CLI founded 300-turn run: `30d6511ff116a4aa487725452e7b749c351e880865e08263572ae0c245ddab01`
- in-test pin at the time: `c0e3c8422c58e8443ac117142fa7ac70578022c43ce51b5a3bed68c4595d254a`

The `ci.yml` `FOUNDED_GOLDEN` absolute pin (T1.9) is exactly the instrument that catches this
class of divergence, and it is the reason the defect surfaced at all. Fixed by wiring
matching worldgen into the four founded harnesses (`SnapshotTests` founded golden,
`DrivenGoldenTests`, `FirstReignTests`, `CalibrationBatteryTests` — canonical arm to
`Worldgen`, dev arm to `DevWorldgen`). `SnapshotTests`' turn-200 control was deliberately left
alone: it runs the toy preset, has no terrain, and is the no-unrelated-movement control.

## §5 THE FINDING — THE FOUNDING TRIGGER SELECTS NEWBORNS, NOT OVERPOPULATED SETTLEMENTS

### 5.1 How it surfaced
The driven golden did not terminate. Two independent runs of the same 300-turn test burned
**33 and 21 minutes** of CPU without finishing, against **2m34** for the equally-long founded
golden on the same tree. Per ADR-015 §7.1 a run that will not terminate is itself a finding
and is recorded as one rather than waited on; both runs were stopped and the cause measured
instead.

### 5.2 What the measurement shows
Instrumented driven run, settlement count and total population per turn:

| turn | 1 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 | 77 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| settlements | 12 | 19 | 40 | 48 | 78 | 97 | 123 | 152 | **178** |
| population | 4330 | 3898 | 3844 | 4246 | 3414 | 3545 | 3607 | 3384 | **3192** |

Settlements grow without saturating (~2.3/turn and still climbing at turn 77) while total
population **falls**. This is population **fragmentation** — a fixed population shattered into
ever more, ever smaller settlements — not frontier settlement. The non-termination is a
consequence, not a separate problem: `CatchmentSystem.IsStale` fires every turn because the
settlement count changes every turn, and `MigrationSystem` allocates `new int[maxId+1]`
pairwise matrices, so cost grows superlinearly in a settlement count that never stops rising.

### 5.3 Attribution — measured, not inferred
Instrumenting which settlements are actually in deficit, split by whether they existed at
turn zero (`FoundedTurn == 0`) or were founded by this system:

| turn | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| in deficit | 2 | 4 | 2 | 7 | 4 | 10 |
| …originals | 0 | 2 | 0 | **0** | 1 | 3 |
| …founded by colonization | 2 | 2 | 2 | **7** | 4 | 7 |

The deficits driving founding are overwhelmingly in **newly founded** settlements. The
original twelve are mostly fed. So the cascade is self-sustaining: a settlement founded with
a tiny party, **zero housing** and only carried provisions runs a consumption deficit on its
first turns; that deficit fires the trigger; it founds another; and so on.

### 5.4 The claim of mine that this refutes
`ColonizationSystem`'s own doc comment asserts the party size is *"self-limiting by
construction."* **It is not, and I should not have asserted it without measuring it.**
`DeficitRatio` is **scale-free**: removing `deficit × count` people also removes their demand,
so the ratio is essentially unchanged next turn and the same settlement colonises again,
every turn, indefinitely. Emigration cannot clear a ratio.

The deeper problem is that `ConsumptionDeficitRow` **cannot distinguish** "cannot feed the
people it has" from "was founded three turns ago and is not producing yet." They are the same
row, and the trigger fires on both.

### 5.5 What I tried, in scope, and what it did — a damper, not a fix
The one strengthening available without inventing a mechanism is to make the **already
authorized clearing cost binding**: an expedition must be OUTFITTED, i.e. the same per-capita
provisions share `TransferProvisions` already computes must be satisfiable rather than
silently clamped to nothing (today the code comments *"refugees leave with nothing"* and
founds anyway). Measured as an uncommitted probe:

| turn | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| settlements, as shipped | 19 | 40 | 48 | 78 | 97 | **123** |
| settlements, with binding clearing cost | 17 | 26 | 35 | 49 | 60 | **73** |

It roughly halves the rate and the runtime (3m44 → 1m42 for 60 turns), but the cascade
**survives**: ~1.2 foundings/turn, still no saturation, deficits still dominated by newborns,
population still falling (4330 → 3157). A half-fix that only slows divergence would be worse
than reporting, so **the probe was reverted and is not in this commit.**

### 5.6 DECISION REQUIRED — this is a genuine director boundary
The founding trigger authorized by the packet, and derived in the design pass, does not
select the population D-037 B1 describes. Two candidate directions, neither of which I am
authorized to take:

**Option 1 — re-derive the trigger from migration viability.** D-037 B1's actual condition is
*"with no viable destination people die at home"* — that is `MigrationSystem`'s **viability**,
not consumption deficit. Colonization would become the fallback for departure demand migration
could not place, which is self-limiting because placing the party discharges the demand.
*Blast radius:* `MigrationSystem` does not expose unplaced demand today, so it must either
record it (a change to the T2.8/T2.13 director-stabilised equation's contract) or colonization
must recompute that equation itself — which the design pass explicitly rejected as
synthesising invented quantities.

**Option 2 — make the clearing cost binding and accept a residual rate.** In scope, no new
constant, measured above. *But it does not converge*, so it needs a second gate, and every
second gate I can construct is either a new constant, a timer, or a threshold — all three
explicitly prohibited by the packet.

**Recommendation: Option 1.** It is the only one that is self-limiting by an actual mechanism
rather than by damping, and it is what the ratified text says. It requires a ruling because
its blast radius reaches a contract the packet forbids me to touch on my own judgement.

- **No adversarial review.** ADR-015 §6 requires an independent agent in its own pinned
  worktree. None has run. Nothing in this document is an independent verdict; §5 is my own
  measurement of my own code.
- **No full-suite run** on the final tree, and no determinism harness run (orderless, replay,
  founded orderless, founded replay, CLI vs pinned `FOUNDED_GOLDEN`). Three goldens are known

- **No golden re-pin, and no `ci.yml` change.**
- The remaining packet measurements (frontier closure, spacing distribution, minimum actual
  site separation, resurrection-cycle indicators) are **not** done: every one of them is a
  measurement of a founding rate that §5 shows is wrong, so they would measure the defect.
- No frozen document was modified. CR-003's quarantine is untouched and unreinterpreted,
  corridor bands are untouched, and D-037, D-041, ADR-012, ADR-018 and the M0 kernel are
  unmodified. No new RNG stream. No polity carrier. `ClaimRow` and `ControlRow` are
  unreinterpreted and unwritten.

## §7 EARLIER FINDING, recorded and deliberately not acted on

`SettlementSiting`'s spacing prefilter is a **capped** Dijkstra whose contract treats unreached
nodes as trivially far enough, so unreachable land passes the spacing test at any floor —
**spacing does not imply reachability**. Pinned as
`UnreachableLandSatisfiesSpacingTrivially_DocumentingTheSEMANTICS`. Adding a reachability
requirement needs a travel budget, i.e. a new constant, so it is deliberately **not** done in
this packet. This also corrected a test of mine whose premise was wrong
(`NoLegalFrontierSite_MeansNoFounding_AndNoThrow` asserted a founding would not happen at an
enormous spacing floor; it did happen, for exactly this reason).
