## The T3.7-seed measurement — water routes vs the ore deadband (certification directive)

Reference class stated BEFORE computing (rig header, WaterRouteCounterfactualTests):
pre-modern freight ratios, road : river ≈ 5–10 : 1 and road : sea ≈ 25–60 : 1 (the Diocletian-
Edict-anchored corpus + medieval cartage-vs-river rates) — so the judged river band is water
at r ∈ [0.10, 0.20] of ideal-land cost, one water class at river rates (treating sea as river
UNDERSTATES the advantage: conservative against the conclusion). Crossing condition for the
measured full-band tin gap: pathCost < 19.95 / (8 × 0.16) = 15.586 cost units. Pair set:
tin-richest 3 × tin-poorest 3 per seed (the Item 0 floor-vs-ceiling proxy), seeds 42/7/303.

**Two-stage result, both stages recorded:**
1. LATTICE-resolution counterfactual (block-majority water = effectively the SEA network):
   land-only costs for the ore pairs run 31–154 units, no pair crosses; and even FREE water
   never crosses (r* = 0 for all 27 pairs) — because the stride-4 water mask cannot see
   rivers, the residual "land legs" include every river course, and they alone exceed the
   crossing cost. This stage's honest finding is a RESOLUTION limit, not an economics answer.
2. PIXEL-resolution counterfactual (river mask as routable water — the actual hypothesis;
   site pixels sit ON the river mask, so water starts at the doorstep, and the land-only
   validation column returns Infinity for exactly that reason — the lattice numbers above are
   the land baseline):
   - r = 0.50 (water at merely HALF ideal-land cost — far above any historical rate): 0 of 27
     cross, but the best pairs miss by a hair (16.1/16.7 vs 15.586).
   - r = 0.35: 2 of 27 cross. **The first crossing sits at r ≈ 0.5–0.35** — the gap first
     opens at water rates ~2–3× MORE EXPENSIVE than the dearest historical river rate.
   - r = 0.20 (conservative end of the reference band): **4 of 27 cross, ≥ 1 pair on every
     seed** (42: 6→0 at 9.2; 7: 9→10 at 10.7; 303: 0→8 at 6.7 and 2→4 at 6.5).
   - r = 0.10 (cheap end): 7 of 27 cross.

**THE NUMBER, as asked:** tin-ore's full-band gap first crosses its deadband at a water-cost
ratio between 0.35 and 0.5 of ideal land for the best river-connected pairs — comfortably
ABOVE (cheaper than needed) the entire historical river band, and at the reference band
itself 4–7 of the 27 largest-gap pairs open, at least one per seed. Overland-only, nothing
opens at ANY divergence (Item 0). READING: the model has been CORRECT about overland bulk
haulage — bulk trade at these distances is riverine or it is nothing, which is the
reference class's own claim — and the missing piece is D-009's anticipated water edges, NOT
a frozen constant. The next packet is a MECHANISM ("add water routes", on river polylines /
the pixel river mask, since stride-4 blocks cannot represent rivers), not a CR. Caveats
stated: the crossing condition assumes the measured full-band gap persists once flows begin
(it will not — trade closes gaps; the counterfactual bounds the OPENING, not the volume);
no portage/seasonality frictions; the 0.25 pixel-to-node unit conversion keeps all figures
in SettlementDistances' cost units. Escalation 2 (band-edge pinning) is untouched by any of
this — it remains a D-033 price-solver matter with its own owner.
