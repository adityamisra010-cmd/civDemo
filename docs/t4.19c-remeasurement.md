## §2 METHOD — EXACT COMMANDS, WHICH TREE, WHICH COMPUTATION

### §2.1 The density battery

The prescribed battery is the nightly one, `.github/workflows/ci.yml` job `calibration-nightly`
(line 188): `dotnet run --project Sim.Cli -c Release -- autoplay --seeds 20 --turns 650 --metrics
nightly-metrics.json`. That is the instrument the T4.10 lift evidence used (`corridors.json`
`liftEvidence`: "20-seed / 650-turn sweep at e6cf705"). Run verbatim, Release, on each tree:

| arm | tree | command | seeds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NEW | `c41c896`, this branch's base, worktree `lane-t419c` | `dotnet build Sim.Cli -c Release` then `dotnet run --project Sim.Cli -c Release --no-build -- autoplay --seeds 20 --turns 650 --metrics new-metrics.json` | 1–20 (`--seed-base` default 1) |
| OLD | `feaf218`, a detached scratch worktree (`git worktree add --detach … feaf218`), **as-is** | identical | 1–20 |

`sim autoplay` founds each seed with `HeadlessFounding.Found(seed)` (canonical 1024 px worldgen,
N = 12, production pipeline, no orders) and steps 650 turns; the era table takes every seed to
`finalYear` 4500 exactly. The OLD arm is `feaf218` unmodified: it carries neither lane C's founding
vector nor the CR-014 cap fix. Lane A measured the cap fix hash-neutral at turn 300 on the old
DRIVEN world; the founded autoplay worlds complete at `feaf218` without the fix (no order ever
raises the craft input path the fix guards), so the OLD arm here is the pre-T4.19 world exactly as
T4.18 left it, and the NEW − OLD difference carries every T4.19 change together, not the founding
vector in isolation. (Between the two trees `Sim.Core`/`Sim.Data`/`Sim.Cli` differ by the
observability layer, `NeedsGrievanceSystem`, the CR-014 expression in `ProductionSystem.Craft`, and
`sim.json`'s `founding.cohortCounts`; of these only the last two can move a simulated quantity in
an order-free autoplay, and the cap fix's guard is a path the founded worlds do not take.)

**The computation is the shipped one, not a re-derivation.** `AutoplayCollector.Finish`
(`Sim.Core/Kernel/AutoplayMetrics.cs`) records `FinalPopulation` = Σ `Buckets[i].Count` and
`ArableKm2` = Σ `CatchmentSummaries[i].EffectiveArableKm2` at the last turn, and
`CalibrationAnalysis.DensityPerArableKm2(m)` = `FinalPopulation / ArableKm2` (line 197). Both the
CLI's `derived.densityPerArableKm2` and `CalibrationBatteryTests.Canonical_FedCorridors_AllInBand`
(line 299) call that one function. The tables below read `derived.densityPerArableKm2` straight
from each arm's metrics JSON and re-check it against `finalPopulation / arableKm2` from the same
file (equal to 1e-12 on all 40 rows) and `finalYear == 4500` (all 40 rows). Year-4500 population
IS `finalPopulation`; "effective arable" IS `arableKm2`. Correlations are Pearson across the 20
seeds, the T3.12 breach record's §D computation.

### §2.2 The Artisan emergence measurement

`ClassSystemTests.Artisans_EmergeInFedAutoplay_PlateauAtTheCap_DocumentedWindow` (lines 76–200)
measures ONE world: `WorldFounding.Found(TestConfigs.DevWorldgen(), TestConfigs.Sim(), 42)` —
the dev preset (canonical worldgen at 256 px, N = 4), the production pipeline under the canonical
era table — and defines `emergenceTurn` as the first turn `t` (1-based, after `exec.Step`) at
which `ClassMobilitySystem.AdultsOfClass(world.Buckets, S0, Artisans) > 0` for settlement 0. That
is "first artisan adult present in settlement 0", NOT "first turn `ClassStateRow.Active == 1`",
and the two are not the same event (§4.3). The window asserted is `emergenceTurn ∈ [10, 95]`.

A throwaway harness outside the tree (scratchpad only; the committed tree carries no code change,
§6) reproduces exactly that recipe — same three loaders, same `SizePx = 256` / `SettlementCount =
4` override, same executor, same `S0`, same `ClassId(2)`, same predicate — for seeds 1–20 (the
battery's seeds) plus 42 (the test's pin, as a control), on each arm, and records per seed:
settlement 0's founding population and its population after turns 1–4; the first turn the
published `population` variable exceeds 520 and the first turn the published `food_surplus_ratio`
exceeds 1.3; the latch turn (first `Active == 1`); the two variables the latch read when it fired
(Prev's rows, i.e. the values published the turn before); the emergence turn; and artisans / adults
at emergence. Seed 42 reproduces lane C's §8 figures on both arms to the person (old: founds 459,
394 on turn 1, emerges 22; new: founds 468, 528 on turn 2, latch and emergence at 4), which is the
harness's check that it measures what the test measures.

### §4.4 WHY the corrected founding reaches the predicate sooner — from the mechanism

The predicate has two conjuncts. **The surplus conjunct is never the binding one, on either arm.**
`food_surplus_ratio` is published at turn 1 as 0 (no harvest has happened), at turn 2 as 0 (the
turn-1 harvest is structurally zero — `CatchmentSummaries` is empty at turn 0, lane C §9), and
carries the first real harvest from turn 3, when it exceeds 1.3 on every one of the 20 seeds of
both arms (first turn > 1.3: turn 3 on 17 of 20 seeds per arm, turn 4 on seeds 15, 19 and 20 —
the same three on both arms). The value the latch
reads when it finally fires is 1.32–3.15 (OLD) and 1.58–3.84 (NEW) — always clear of 1.3, and on
the NEW arm slightly higher, because a larger and younger workforce harvests more per mouth. So
the claim "a younger pyramid reaches the surplus threshold sooner" is not what the numbers say; the
surplus threshold is crossed at turn 3–4 regardless.

**The population conjunct is the gate.** The latch fires exactly one turn after the `population`
variable first exceeds 520 on 14 of 20 OLD seeds and 12 of 20 NEW seeds; every exception is one of
two shapes. (a) Seeds founding above 520 (OLD 6, 7, 10, 13, 20; NEW 6, 7, 10, 13, 16, 20) publish
`population > 520` at turn 1 but have no real surplus until turn 3, so the earliest the conjunction
can hold is at turn 4 — reached by NEW 6, 7, 10 (latch 4) and NEW 20 / OLD 6 (latch 5); the rest
of that group (OLD 7, 10, 13, 20; NEW 13, 16) had FALLEN back below 520 by turn 2 and latched only
after regrowing (43, 49, 67, 42; 45, 39). (b) Three seeds crossed 520, dipped under it, and
re-crossed a few turns later (OLD 3: 88 → 94; NEW 12: 5 → 7; NEW 15: 79 → 82). The lag chain is
fixed: population after turn t is published as the variable at turn t + 1; the latch at turn t + 2
reads it from Prev; the promotion flow runs in that same Step with `active = 1`
(`promoteRatePerYear` 0.08 on the gap to the target share, integrated over dt = 10 y), so the
first promoted artisans appear on the latch turn itself on every seed but NEW 7 (latch 4, first
artisans at 5). Founding above 520 and staying there therefore gives turn 4, the mechanism's
floor, and the NEW seed 42 sits exactly there.

**What the old vector did to that gate.** `feaf218`'s `cohortCounts`
`[46,44,40,30,28,26,24,22,20,18,17,15,30,20,12,8]` put 70/400 = 17.5 % of every founding
population in cohorts 12–15 (ages 60–79), where `mortalityPerYear` runs 0.107–0.305/yr; integrated
over the first dt = 10 y turn that is the opening collapse T4.18 diagnosed. On the 20 dev seeds
settlement 0 loses 5.1–18.8 % of its people on turn 1 (mean −12.7 %, 20/20 fall) and keeps
falling for two to three more turns on most of them (§4.1: seed 1 358 → 318 → 315; seed 42 459 →
394). The corrected vector `[57,56,51,45,40,34,29,24,19,15,11,8,5,3,2,1]` is the kernel's own
stable age structure (11/400 = 2.75 % aged 60+); settlement 0 GROWS on turn 1 on 19/20 seeds (mean
+3.8 %). A settlement founding at 459 that must first fall to 394 and regrow reaches 520 at turn
21 (published) and latches at 22; the same settlement founding at 468 and rising reaches 528 by
turn 2 and latches at 4. The shift is the removed transient — the distance the population no
longer has to make up — and it moves every seed by an amount that depends on how far below 520
its post-collapse trough sat: −1 turn for seed 6 (founded 591, back above 520 by turn 3 on the old vector, never below
it on the new) and −45 turns for seeds 10 and 19. Nothing in the surplus, the slope, the cap or the rate changed.
