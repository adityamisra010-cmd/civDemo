# CR-002 — The canonical world is out of the density corridor, and the corridor is measuring geometry

**Status:** OPEN — awaiting director ruling
**Raised:** T3.1 acceptance follow-up (director-required corridor re-derivation)
**Author:** implementing agent

---

## 1. The frozen items in conflict

- **The calibration corridor `canonical.densityPerArableKm2` = [0.15, 0.6]** (TUNE data, but
  governed by the director's standing rule: *"A historical corridor may not be loosened because
  the measured value moved; that turns the instrument into a mirror."*)
- **T3.1, accepted and ratified**, which changed what the corridor's denominator MEANS (the
  moisture BFS now credits river adjacency, so interior river valleys count as arable land).

After T3.1 the canonical world **straddles the floor**: 0.1415 (seed 7), 0.1468 (42), 0.1486 (1), 0.1630 (2) — three of four measured seeds below 0.15, one inside. The corridor
may not be moved to accommodate it, and the packet that moved the world is accepted. Hence a CR.

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 The world did not get emptier — the denominator's unit changed

Same seed, same 650-turn horizon, measured pre- and post-T3.1:

| | pre (main) | post (T3.1) | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| population | 115,627 | 120,310 | **+4.1 %** |
| "arable" km² | 388,145 | 809,711 | **×2.09** |
| raw catchment km² | 726,528 | 1,588,992 | ×2.19 |
| world mean fertility (land) | 0.266 | 0.364 | ×1.37 |
| **density** | **0.298** | **0.149** | **÷2.00** |

The population *rose*. The measured density halved because the denominator doubled: river valleys
now score as suitable land (×1.37 on mean fertility), and settlements sited by region-score sit in
basins with land around them rather than on coastal points surrounded by 25×-cost sea (×2.19 on
raw catchment area).

### 2.2 The re-derivation does not support a floor of 0.12

Four independent derivations, each from a named reference class, followed by three adversarial
reviews:

| reference class | derived floor | supports 0.12? |
|---|---|---|
| Global Neolithic over habitable land | 0.12 | yes — *conceded as "the tight edge"; author recommends 0.10* |
| Neolithic Europe (LBK, Cologne protocol) | 0.12 | yes — *conceded as "the single most extreme corner of the whole class"; central estimate 0.148–0.209* |
| Site-catchment analysis (Vita-Finzi & Higgs) | 0.72 | **no** |
| Frontier / unfilled agrarian | 0.25 | **no** |

Both adversarial reviews refuted the 0.12 derivation, on two independent grounds:

1. **The cancellation identity.** Deriving the floor as `P_hist / (H × s̄)` where `s̄` is the sim's
   *own* measured in-catchment mean suitability cancels against `measured = P_sim / (A_cat × s̄)`.
   The ratio floor/measured reduces to `raw_hist / raw_sim`, independent of the suitability
   formula — so the floor sits at a fixed ~87 % of whatever the map reports, forever. That is the
   rejected mirror with an extra multiplication in front of it.
2. **The counterfactual.** Run the same recipe on the *pre*-change map (in-catchment s̄ ≈ 0.21–0.26)
   and it yields a floor of ~0.26 — which excludes today's measurement by 1.7×. A method that
   produces a different answer depending on which side of the change you run it on is not a
   derivation.
3. **The capacity discount is already inside the historical numbers.** The argument for a low floor
   was "the catchment is unfilled capacity, not land under plough." But every historical comparand
   is *already* capacity-denominated: Zimmermann's LBK Rhineland 0.42–0.44 p/km² is computed over
   the whole 37,989 km² atlas region *including the empty forest between settlement cells* (and
   that source reports the Bandkeramik used only 31 % of the land its own agronomy called
   suitable); Neolithic Britain's 0.24–1.19 covers the whole island, mostly wildwood. Taking the
   discount a second time double-counts it.

Against those anchors the sim's **0.0757–0.0784 persons per raw catchment km²** is **1.6–2.2×
emptier than Illinois Territory in 1810** (0.12–0.17), the emptiest documented sustained farming
frontier. The single comparison that passes — global habitable-land density, 0.067 at 4000 BC and
0.135 at 3000 BC — mismatches denominators, since its 104 M km² includes Siberia, Amazonia and
every forager, while the sim's denominator discards the 75 % of the continent no settlement reaches.

**Verdict: the corridor stands at 0.15 and the world is out of corridor on three of four seeds.**

The derived band from the correct class is **[0.25, 2.30]** — i.e. the honest floor is *above* the
standing one. The band is **held at [0.15, 0.6] rather than raised**, because raising it is also a
change to the instrument and needs the same ruling. The old note's "~10–30/km² of mature agrarian
land use" justification is withdrawn: those are worked-area densities, not commensurable with an
isochrone denominator.

### 2.3 What the metric is actually measuring

The catchment radius is **205 km** — 41× the classic 5 km / one-hour working radius, and 51 hours'
walk one way. No farming society works land at that distance. Density enters quadratically in
radius, so this one parameter dominates the metric:

| catchment radius | same 120,310 people read as |
|---|---|
| 5 km | 250 /weighted km² |
| 20 km | 15.6 |
| 40 km | **3.9** |
| 60 km | 1.74 |
| **205 km (current)** | **0.149** |

At a 40 km radius the identical population lands squarely inside the settled-agrarian range. **The
deviation is a geometry artefact, not a demographic one.** The population is set by founding stock
× the T2.7-calibrated growth rate and is not obviously wrong; the land it is divided by is.

## 3. Options (minimal, in increasing blast radius)

**Option A — rule the deviation acceptable and re-band on a stated design basis.**
Accept that this world is deliberately sparser than any historical farming society, state that
explicitly as a D-015 scale decision, and re-derive the band from *that* premise rather than from
history. Cheapest. Cost: the corridor stops being a historical instrument and becomes a
design-intent instrument; that should be written down, not left implicit.

**Option B — fix the geometry: reduce `CatchmentSystem.TravelBudget` (currently 15.0).**
A budget giving a ~40 km working radius puts the density at ~3.9 and inside every historical class.
Blast radius: the catchment feeds Farming's land ceiling, Migration's attractiveness, PathBuild's
targets, and the D-016 recompute triggers — **every golden moves and the fed/Malthus corridors need
re-measuring**. This is the option that makes the metric mean what its name says.

**Option C — split the metric.**
Keep the isochrone as the *economic reach* it actually is, and add a separate, smaller *worked-land*
radius for the production land ceiling and for this corridor. Most faithful to both roles; most work.

## 4. Recommendation

**Option B**, deferred to T3.10 (calibration extension) rather than taken now. The reasoning: the
deviation is real but it is a *geometry* defect that T3.3–T3.6 will not touch, and changing the
catchment radius mid-milestone would move every golden twice. T3.10 already owns corridor
re-derivation and the BINDING MalthusLite restoration; this belongs with them.

Until ruled, the deviation is recorded here and pinned by
`Canonical_DensityCorridor_KnownDeviation_PendingCr002` — which asserts the corridor's real band,
records the failure explicitly, and **fails if the value drifts further** — so nothing silently
passes and nothing silently worsens.

## 5. What this CR does not claim

It does not claim the population is right. The absolute check is unflattering from the other
direction: 120,310 people on 5.87 M km² of continent is ~10× less populous than Neolithic Europe
in absolute terms. Option B fixes the *ratio* without touching that; whether the world should also
hold more people is a separate D-015 question the director may want to fold into the same ruling.
