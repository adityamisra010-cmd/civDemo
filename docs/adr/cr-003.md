## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 The derivations (four reference classes, three adversarial attacks, all refuted)

Food units (person-years of adult-equivalent sustenance) per fertility-weighted km² per year:

| reference class | derived | stated as |
|---|---|---|
| Swidden / long-fallow (Boserup forest- and bush-fallow; Conklin, Rösch, chitemene) | **16.04** | the land-rich, labour-scarce FLOOR |
| Temperate European Neolithic (LBK; Zimmermann/Lüning, Bogaard, Rösch) | **12.94** | central; corrected to ~24.4 by the units attack (see below) |
| Near Eastern Neolithic–EBA rainfed (Halstead, Araus δ¹³C, Wilkinson) | **37.5** | central for a more intensive regime |
| Medieval NW-European open field (Slicher van Bath, Campbell, Titow) | **152.7** | explicitly a CEILING, out of the sim's technological class |
| **SYNTHESIS — the value shipped** | **26.0** | see below |

The synthesis did not average the four. It re-derived the REFERENCE CLASS from the sim's own
worldgen, which is what settled it: `fertility = clamp(tempSuit × moisture × 1.6-if-river-adjacent,
0, 1)` with `moisture = 1/(1 + d_water_px/40)` and 4 km pixels means **f = 1.0 is attainable only
within 8 km of a river channel and at a mean annual temperature in [8.4, 23.6] °C**. The sim's
"ideal square kilometre" is river-valley floor and first terrace — well-watered valley bottom, not
average upland — so every land-use share must be a share of *that* landscape, which none of the
four shelf reference classes had assumed. The chain, its inverse checks and full sensitivity are in
`docs/adr/adr-013` §4 and in the `_docValue` note in `sim.json`. Inverse check: 0.88 arable ha per
average person, against Wilkinson's and Halstead's 0.5–1.0 for pre-mechanised rainfed — inside,
mid-range.

At 26.0 the land term is still selected by the `min()` in **734 of 19 560 (settlement, turn) pairs**
over the canonical campaign — rare, but not the dead data it becomes at 37.5 and above. That
observability was the tie-breaker for preferring the lower half of the derived range, and it is a
criterion about falsifiability, not about hitting a band.

Three adversarial attacks, all of which **refuted** their own claim:

- *Fitting.* No fitting signature: the four span 11.8×, all four overshoot every reference band
  they were shown (unanimous overshoot is the opposite of fitting), the dominant free parameter was
  not pushed upward in any of them, and the conclusion survives every chain's own pessimistic
  endpoint. The CR-002 cancellation identity was checked for specifically and is absent.
- *Units.* All four chains reproduce exactly; hectares↔km² and the per-node factor are each applied
  once; fertility weighting is applied once, never twice. One real error found, confined to the LBK
  chain: it uses a real-land arable ratio as an ideal-fertility ratio, a 1.89× understatement, so
  its corrected value is ~24.4 rather than 12.94.
- *Labour interaction.* See §2.3 — this one is the reason for the CR.

**The old value is not rescued by any of this.** 28.0 per node = 0.109 per fertility-weighted km² =
0.219 kg of grain per hectare of landscape per year. Sow 100 % of the landscape every year with no
fallow, no seed retention and no loss and the implied sown yield still fails to return a 120–200
kg/ha sowing rate by ~700×. It also encodes 0.058 people per real km², below documented
temperate-forest *forager* densities.

### 2.3 Why: the world never reaches its food ceiling, so the constant stops being observable

Over the actual 6 016-year campaign, population is set by the T2.7 demographic vectors integrated
over the era table, **not** by carrying capacity:

- Canonical final population: **406 111** at yield 15.0, **406 105** at 37.5, **406 105** at 152.7.
  A 10.2× yield range moves world population by **six people**.
- At 37.5 and at 152.7 the land term is selected by the Leontief `min()` in **0 of 1 630 turns**,
  across all twelve settlements. Two values 4.07× apart produce bit-identical populations because
  the constant never enters any computation.
- The arithmetic is elementary once stated: twelve settlements of ~400 people growing at the fed
  rate of ~0.076 %/yr for 6 000 years reach 4 800 · e^4.56 ≈ 4.6 × 10⁵ — which is what is measured.
  Carrying capacity at yield 15 is 15 × 53 383 / 0.8175 ≈ 9.8 × 10⁵. **The demographic clock runs
  out before the land does.**

For the ceiling to bind inside the campaign, yield must be **≤ 6.2** per fertility-weighted km².

**A constant that is selected in zero turns of the whole campaign is unfalsifiable by construction.**
It cannot be validated, refuted, or benched, and the only negative feedback the model has —
Malthusian land pressure — is silently deleted from the `min()`. That is a mechanism loss, not a
calibration miss, and it is independent of which corridor you judge it against.

### 2.4 The corridors were passing on the product of the two errors

Before T3.2b the world ran a yield 256× too coarse **and** a hinterland ~4× too large. Capacity was
0.109 × 809 711 / 0.8175 ≈ 1.08 × 10⁵ — which the demographic clock happens to reach at about turn
800, producing exactly the crash the corridor was banded on. Fixing **either** error alone breaks
it: at 0.109 with the corrected 50 km hinterland the canonical world ends at 7 077 people and
crashes at turn 134. The Malthus corridors were therefore never measuring agronomy; they were
measuring the coincidence that two compensating mistakes put carrying capacity just under where
6 000 years of demography lands.

## 3. Options (≤3 minimal, as required)

**Option 1 — Ship the derived constant; quarantine the Malthus corridors as an obsolete
instrument.** Keeps T3.2b item 1's directive intact. Records that the sim has no Malthusian regime
inside its campaign, which is *historically defensible*: global population from 4000 BC to AD 2100
grew nearly monotonically, and Malthusian crashes are regional and episodic, not global. Cost: the
model's only negative feedback is inert, and it stays inert until a mechanism (Option 3) lands.
Also: at the higher derived values the constant is not merely inert but unfalsifiable, which argues
for the low end of the derived range even under this option.

**Option 2 — Rule that the land coefficient is capacity-AS-REALISED, and set it near 1.6.** The
honest form of the argument: in a model where capacity is always eventually realised and nothing
else bounds growth, the land coefficient is doing the work of every missing friction — clearance
labour, disease, warfare, social organisation, inability to found daughter settlements. Setting it
to a pure agronomic maximum therefore over-states the world. 1.633 per fertility-weighted km² is
1.25 people per real km² at the measured in-catchment fertility of 0.766, which sits at the top of
Neolithic Britain's documented 0.24–1.19 range — so it is not absurd. **But this is the argument
that can rationalise any number**, it is indistinguishable in effect from fitting, and it directly
contradicts the packet's instruction. I do not recommend it without the director naming it
explicitly as a ruling.

**Option 3 — Ship the derived constant AND schedule the missing mechanism.** Same as Option 1 for
this packet, plus a named follow-up packet for the thing that actually bounded pre-modern
populations below agronomic capacity: land clearance / colonisation. This is already sitting in
`docs/queue.md` as the reframed expansion-opportunity item — the world is now ~1.2 % settled, and a
growing population has no way to take the other 98.8 %. It is also the mechanism that would restore
Malthusian behaviour honestly: a frontier that fills, rather than a yield constant tuned until the
world starves on schedule.

## 5. DIRECTOR RULING (2026-07-26) — Option 3, accepted

Ship the derived 26.0 per fertility-weighted km². Keep the corridors quarantined as built. Option 2
(rule the coefficient as capacity-as-realised, ~1.6) is REFUSED, for exactly the reason §3 gave:
from inside the code it is indistinguishable from fitting a constant to make a corridor pass.
Refusing to make that call unilaterally, while writing the honest form of the opposing argument
anyway, is recorded as the standard.

### 5.1 The rationale (the ruling's reasoning, recorded verbatim in substance)

**A FRONTIER SOCIETY IS NOT MALTHUSIAN.** Twelve settlements holding 1.9 % of a continent are not
pressing against a land ceiling; they are surrounded by unclaimed land. Malthusian crisis is what
happens when a frontier CLOSES. This world has no mechanism by which land ever fills — no
colonization, no land clearance — so a pre-Malthusian world is the CORRECT output of the corrected
constants, not a defect in them.

The M2 corridors were therefore never measuring agronomy. They were measuring a coincidence: a
yield 256× too coarse multiplied by a hinterland ~4× too large put carrying capacity where the
demographic clock happened to reach it around turn 800. Two errors produced one plausible-looking
crash. The diagnosis in §2 is accepted in full.

**The Malthusian trap must EMERGE when land fills.** It must never be hardwired, and it must never
be restored by choosing a constant that reproduces the old crash.

### 5.2 Amendment: TWO missing mechanisms, not one

§3's "land clearance / colonisation" is correct but incomplete. There are two missing mechanisms,
they are different sizes, and they belong in different milestones:

**(a) COLONIZATION / LAND CLEARANCE — M4.** How population converts empty land into settled,
worked land, and therefore how the frontier eventually closes and Malthusian pressure legitimately
emerges. A large system — founding rules, site selection, clearing cost, what constrains sprawl —
thematically and mechanically at home in M4 alongside expansion and borders. QUEUED with CR-003
named as the finding that exposed its absence; design bound by D-037 Part B1 (colonization from
below). No part of it is attempted in M3.

**(b) HARVEST VARIANCE — M3, directed packet T3.4b.** How a land-abundant society starves anyway.
Pre-modern people did not only die when land ran out; they died in bad years — drought, flood,
blight, cold summers — and a frontier society is MORE vulnerable to this, not less: thin reserves,
no market to buy from. Without it the corrected M3 world has no scarcity driver except player
error, the chronicle has nothing to report, migration is dead, and six test families stay
toothless. Harvest variance was deferred to M4 to protect M3's risk budget; CR-003 inverts that
calculus, because M3 without it is a world where nothing can go wrong. Scope and acceptance are
fixed in the ruling and recorded in `docs/m3-spec.md` §4 (T3.4b), including: spatial AND temporal
weather correlation required; the multiplier acts on OUTPUT, never on the derived 26.0; famine
emerges from the food balance, never from a trigger; the six quarantined families enumerated at
acceptance with each un-quarantined or explicitly justified; migration alive in the dev world; the
migration attractiveness weights re-DERIVED (§2.6) rather than rescaled to old behaviour.

### 5.3 Revised M3 packet order

T3.2b (merged) → T3.3 (production + scaffolding demolition, SOLO) → T3.4 (price solver, SOLO,
adversarial-mandatory, undiluted) → **T3.4b (harvest variance, new)** → T3.5 onward per the spec.

### 5.4 What not to do (standing constraints from the ruling)

- The Malthus corridors are not restored by adjusting any constant. They stay quarantined until a
  mechanism legitimately makes them true again.
- The derived 26.0 is not provisional and not negotiable. It is derived; it stands until a better
  derivation displaces it, not until a corridor prefers otherwise.

## 6. DIRECTOR-LEVEL ERROR (2026-07-27) — the premise this CR scoped T3.4b on is false

**Recorded at the director's instruction, in the same form as the earlier director-level errors.**

§5.2(b) commissioned T3.4b on the premise of a frontier society with **"thin reserves"** — the idea
being to show how a land-abundant society starves anyway. **Measured, the reserves are ~2,900 years
of consumption deep. The premise is wrong by a factor of ~45.**

The ruling asserted a property of the world without measuring it, and the packet was scoped on that
assertion. Every downstream consequence follows from that one unmeasured claim.

**Measured, T3.4b review (32 unrigged worlds, 1000 turns, shipped config):**

| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| starvation deaths | **0** |
| famine chronicle events | **0** |
| max consumption deficit, any settlement-turn | **exactly 0.0000** |
| worst realised weather multiplier | **0.19** (an 81% harvest failure) |
| median harvest / demand | **2.83× every turn** |
| steady-state store depth | **~2,700–3,000 years of consumption** |
| harvest/demand at year 6,000 | **2.29** |
| `landSide / laborSide`, median | **2.13** |

**The recorded cause was also wrong.** §5.2(b) and the quarantine fixtures attribute the
zero-starvation world to *"the land ceiling is never touched"*. It is not the land ceiling. Grain
never spoils, has no storage cap, and has no alternative use, so every surplus year accumulates

**What is NOT wrong: the mechanism.** A verifier constructed famine at the *shipped* σ with only
store depth changed, and read real chronicle lines from a mature world — named settlements, real
numbers — against a weather-off control at exactly zero. Famine emerges from bad years against thin
stores, driven by weather and only by weather. T3.4b delivered what §3 asked for. What this CR got
wrong is the claim that the world would supply the conditions for it to fire.

**Consequence:** the frontier-closure reasoning in §5.2 identifies the wrong constraint. The real one
is recorded as B-2 in `docs/m4-blocking-material.md` and redirects M4.

### 7.1 OLD PREMISE (historical — retained, not overwritten)

As written into the dev Malthus quarantine and asserted by

> **"THE WORLD IS PRE-MALTHUSIAN — exactly, not approximately. Any starvation at all means the land

### 7.2 FINDING — WHY THAT PREMISE IS FALSE AT THE MECHANISM LEVEL

The premise conflates two different quantities: the existence of a consumption DEFICIT, and the
flooring of a whole-person starvation DEATH.

**Measured, seed 42, dev worldgen, 1000 turns, both arms:**

| | `origin/main` (pre-T4.7) | T4.7 |
|---|---|---|
| turn×settlement deficit events | **14** | **6** |
| worst deficit recorded | 0.004065 | 0.014350 |
| turns with the granary at zero | 1 | 2 |
| starvation deaths | 0 | 4 |

**`origin/main` was ALREADY running nonzero consumption deficits — fourteen of them, including a
turn that emptied a granary completely.** The zero-death reading was therefore never evidence of a
pre-Malthusian world; it was evidence that no deficit had yet been severe enough to floor one whole
person through the D-004 remainder accumulators. T4.7 did not break a clean invariant. It moved an
already-nonzero quantity across an INTEGER BOUNDARY.

This is continuous with §6 of this CR, which already recorded the mechanism correctly: *"Famine
emerges from bad years against thin stores, driven by weather and only by weather."* §6 also states
grain *"has no storage cap"* — that ceased to be true when T4.2 shipped `granaryYearsOfDemand`, and
the T4.7 finding is the first occasion on which that cap has bitten.

**Note on scale, recorded because it explains the era-confinement and NOTHING is authorized from
it:** in the Neolithic band a turn is 10 sim-years while the granary holds 1.5 sim-years of demand,
so the store covers ~0.135 of a single turn's consumption. At t=500 (dt = 5) and t=1000 (dt = 2) the
same settlements hold 0.26 and 0.66 of a turn's demand and record a deficit of exactly zero. Both
observed events fall in the coarse-dt band. **No T4.2 change is proposed or authorized**, and
nothing here establishes that T4.2 is wrong — a ten-year turn compresses a decade of weather into
one draw, and a granary holding 1.5 years of food genuinely could not carry a settlement through a
decade-long bad harvest.

### 7.3 NEW PROPERTY UNDER QUARANTINE

CR-003 is concerned with a **sustained, self-reinforcing Malthusian crash mechanism** — not with the
mere existence of starvation mortality. The two are explicitly distinguished:

**ORDINARY TRANSIENT STARVATION — NOT what CR-003 quarantines:**
> food shock → temporary deficit → bounded mortality → recovery

**MALTHUSIAN CRASH — what CR-003 quarantines:**
> food deficit → mortality / population decline → reduced productive capacity or worsening deficit
> → further mortality → sustained, positive-feedback collapse

**The operational test is the EXISTING one and no new threshold is invented here.**
`AutoplayMetrics.Crashes(m, minDepth)` counts peak-to-trough population drawdowns of at least
`minDepth` of the running peak, and the quarantine already invokes it at **0.20**. That definition
stands unamended and remains the arbiter of "crash".

### 7.4 THE T4.7 EVENTS UNDER THE RESTATED PREMISE

The two observed events (turn 57, 3 deaths; turn 126, 1 death — both settlement S2) are **ORDINARY
TRANSIENT STARVATION** on every measure the definition names:

- `crashCount` = **0/20** in both arms — no drawdown reaches 20 % of any running peak.
- **No positive feedback:** S2's harvest at t=57 is 7,683, the highest in its window; mortality did
  not reduce productive capacity.
- **Complete recovery:** S2 goes 459 (t=56) → 794 (t=123) → 20,366 (t=1000).
- **Not sustained:** S2 is in deficit for 3 turns out of 1000; the events are 69 turns apart and
  shrinking (3 deaths, then 1).
- **No population pressure:** arable rose ~262 % while world population moved +0.06 %, and deficit
  frequency FELL 57 %. A world entering Malthusian constraint does not run fewer deficits.

### 7.5 WHY THE QUARANTINE REMAINS OPEN

CR-003 lifts on *"a mechanism legitimately restoring"* the Malthus corridors. **The measured world
moved in the opposite direction** — less constrained, fewer deficits, more land per head, unchanged
population, no crash. The T4.7 evidence is therefore evidence **AGAINST** lifting the quarantine,
not for it. The corridors `dev.crashCount`, `dev.postCrashPopulation`, `dev.starvationRatePer1000`
and `dev.firstCrashTurn` remain quarantined, with their bands unmoved.

### 7.6 CONSEQUENCE FOR THE TEST — NOT ACTIONED HERE

`AssertMalthusKnownDeviation`'s `starvedTotal == 0` tooth asserts the OLD premise and will therefore
report a premise change on any world where one deficit floors one person, whether or not a crash
mechanism exists. Re-expressing that tooth against §7.3's property is **corridor work owned by
T4.10** (`corridors.json` names the owner as the M4 CR-002 packet). **It is not done here, and
T4.7 does not modify it.** `Dev_MalthusCorridors_AllInBand(seed 42)` accordingly remains RED on the
T4.7 branch, correctly reporting a real premise change.
