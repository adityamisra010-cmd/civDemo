## 5. The migration weight design point (Item 1b, under its pre-committed ruling)

`sim.json`'s `_docLandWeight` states the design point as: *"the weights are set so the two terms
contribute COMPARABLY to the differential at FED conditions"*, evidenced as **land 0.354 against
food ~0.35** — a ratio of ~1.01 — using **max−min across settlements** as the differential.

### The metric defect, confirmed and WIDER than predicted

max−min is an extreme-order statistic: its expectation grows with sample size even when the
underlying distribution is fixed. The pre-committed ruling anticipated that. **Measured, the problem
is worse in a way the ruling did not anticipate — the metric is unstable in TWO dimensions.**

Dev worldgen, turn 120, weighted per-capita terms:

| N | sd(food) | sd(land) | **sd ratio L/F** | range(food) | range(land) | **range ratio L/F** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 0.2343 | 0.0124 | **0.053** | 0.331 | 0.0176 | 0.053 |
| 4 | 0.2912 | 0.1378 | **0.473** | 0.679 | 0.290 | 0.428 |
| 6 | 0.5430 | 0.1464 | **0.270** | 1.552 | 0.321 | 0.207 |
| 9 | 0.5882 | 0.1306 | **0.222** | 1.818 | 0.310 | 0.171 |

Canonical 1024 px, N = 12: **sd ratio 0.124 (seed 1), 0.410 (seed 2).**

Three things follow, and the third is the one that matters:

1. **`sd(land)` IS N-stable** — 0.131 to 0.146 for N ≥ 4. That is the fixed map property behaving
   exactly as theory predicts, and it confirms sd is a sound N-independent *statistic*.
2. **`sd(food)` genuinely grows with N** — 0.234 → 0.588. Not a sampling artifact: more settlements
   means a genuinely more heterogeneous food landscape. Switching from range to sd does **not** fix
   this, because the underlying dispersion really does move.
3. **The ratio is ALSO seed-dependent at fixed N** — 0.124 vs 0.410 between two canonical seeds, a
   3.3× spread, comparable in size to the entire N-effect. **So no single weight can satisfy a
   dispersion-balance design point in any configuration-independent way, in ANY dispersion metric.**
   The quantity the design point fixes is not a constant of the model.

### The miss, reported and not repaired

Against the stated target of ~1.0, measured after the variance fix:

| configuration | sd ratio | range ratio | miss vs target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| dev N = 4 (**the derivation's own rig**) | 0.473 | **0.428** | **2.3×** |
| dev N = 9 | 0.222 | 0.171 | 4.5× |
| canonical N = 12, seed 1 | 0.124 | 0.134 | 8.1× |
| canonical N = 12, seed 2 | 0.410 | 0.323 | 2.4× |

**The design point is missed everywhere, including in the rig it was derived on**, where the doc
records ~1.01 and the corrected world measures 0.428 on the same statistic. The direction is the
telling part: **land contributes LESS than food at every configuration**, which is the same
diagnosis the T3.4b re-derivation existed to fix (*"the land signal was effectively dead"*). The
re-derivation moved that from ~20× to between 2.3× and 8.1×, but did not reach "comparable".

### What a sound design point would fix instead — PROPOSED, NOT APPLIED

A dispersion balance cannot be the design point, because dispersion is not a model constant. The
N- and seed-independent alternative is a **reference-condition level ratio**: name a reference fed
state (a stated food-per-capita and arable-per-capita, from a reference class rather than from a
measurement of the sim) and set `landWeight / foodWeight` so the two terms are equal *there*. That is
N-independent and seed-independent by construction, because it is defined at a stated condition
rather than estimated from whatever sample the world happened to produce — the same discipline
T3.2b's yield derivation used.

**Per the pre-committed ruling, this packet stops here.** The weights are NOT adjusted. The
re-derivation was an accepted T3.4b deliverable, and re-opening it on a metric defect would be scope
creep toward a nicer number. Deriving a reference-condition design point, and reporting what it
implies for the weights, is **a separate directed packet with its own derivation and its own lens**.

**Nothing in this section was tuned. The re-measurement reports; it does not repair.**

## 7. Spatial correlation and regional bad years

| quantity | T3.4b (inflated) | **T3.4c corrected** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mean pairwise weather correlation | 0.249 | **0.219** |
| all settlements simultaneously below mean | 0.952% (39× independent) | **5.56%** |
| ≥ 8 of 9 below mean | 32.5% (1.68× independent) | **15.6%** |

Note the two arms move in *opposite* directions and both are expected. Mean pairwise correlation
falls slightly (0.249 → 0.219) because the pre-fix blend inflated each settlement's own variance
without inflating the shared component, which depressed the *correlation* — the T3.4b lens predicted
exactly this, that correcting the defect would make spatial correlation **stronger** relative to own-
variance. The regional-bad-year rates are measured here on a 9-settlement dev world against T3.4b's
12-settlement canonical figures, so the two columns are not like-for-like; what they jointly establish
is that **whole-world bad years remain common** — 5.6% of turns have every settlement below its mean,
against 1/2⁹ = 0.195% under independence, a **28× lift**. The ruling's requirement that regional bad
years be real is met in the corrected world.
